Fix specification · models/gfn.py · periodic DoF

One Kernel on the Circle

Forward rollouts, backward rollouts, and replay must score the same backward kernel. Today each scores a different one whenever a trajectory crosses a periodic bound — and replay scores garbage.

Angular state dims are wrapped after every SDE step, but the log-probability computations are not wrap-aware. Two defects follow: the Brownian-bridge backward drift −y⋅ct is linear in the raw coordinate and hence depends on which representative of the wrapped state scores it (forward paths use the pre-wrap state, backward and replay paths the post-wrap state); and replayed trajectories, which store only wrapped states, are scored with displacements off by a full period at every crossing. The fix is one invariance rule — every scored log-density must be a function of the wrapped trajectory — implemented as three small edits: nearest-image residuals in the two Gaussian log-prob functions, the bridge drift evaluated at the wrapped representative, and wrapping moved to immediately after sampling. On-policy logPF is numerically unchanged; only logPB at crossing steps moves, and replay becomes exactly consistent with generation.

01Setup

Dims flagged in ang_mask live on a circle. In latent units the domain is (−1,1] with period L=2; the wrap operator (implemented by _wrap_ang, gfn.py:483) is

W(x)=((x+1)mod2)−1∈(−1,1],

applied per angular dim, identity on linear dims. Two states are the same physical point iff they differ by Lk, k∈ℤ, on angular dims. The policy networks never see the raw coordinate — expand_state_for_policy (gfn.py:990) feeds (sinπx,cosπx), which is exactly W-invariant. The per-step kernels, per angular dim:

PF(y∣x)=𝒩(y;x+μθ(x~,t)Δt,σθ2Δt)(1)
PB(x∣y)=𝒩(x;y+b(y),βt2),b(y)=−y⋅ct⋅κθ(y~),(2)

where ct=ΔV/V(tnext) is the bridge contraction (var_drift_coeff, gfn.py:364) and κθ the learned learn_pb correction, bounded to 1±40%. Everything network-produced (μθ,σθ,κθ) is W-invariant by construction. The one term in the entire per-step computation that is not is the bare factor −y in the backward drift.

Three code paths score transitions

_fwd_step
Samples raw y, scores both densities on the pre-wrap pair, then wraps (gfn.py:527–535).
PB drift evaluated at raw y
_bwd_step
Drift from the wrapped current state (gfn.py:557–559), samples raw prev, scores, then wraps.
PB drift evaluated at W(y)
_replay_step
Reads stored trajectories — which contain only wrapped states (gfn.py:664, 788) — and differences them directly.
drift at W(y), displacement from W·states

Neither gauss_logprob (gfn.py:997) nor fwd_gauss_logprob (gfn.py:1003) applies any wrap to the residual — the one attempt is commented out at gfn.py:999, and it was on the wrong variable anyway (the standardized residual, whose period is L/σ^, not 2π).

02Defect 1 — the backward drift is representative-dependent

Because b is linear in the raw coordinate,

b(y+Lk)=b(y)−Lk⋅ct⋅κθ,(3)

and Lkctκθ is not a lattice translation of the density: the two representatives of the same physical state define two different backward kernels. A forward rollout that crosses scores PB at the raw next state; backward rollouts and replay evaluate the identical physical state at the wrapped representative.

Defect 1
PB is not a function of the state on the circle. The same physical transition receives different backward densities depending on which code path scores it — and κθ, fed sin/cos features, is wrap-invariant by construction and cannot express the difference.

Worked example, one angular dim: the state sits at x=0.95 and steps by +0.10: raw y=1.05, stored W(y)=−0.95. The forward path uses drift −1.05ctκθ; the backward/replay representative gives +0.95ctκθ — opposite directions around the circle. With a uniform grid ct≈1/(i+1) at step i, so early crossings carry a drift gap of order the step noise itself; late crossings a small one. This defect alone is a persistent bias between the kernels the three paths train against, not a blowup.

03Defect 2 — replay is off by a full period

Stored trajectories keep only wrapped states, and _replay_step differences them raw. At the crossing step of the example, the stored displacement is −0.95−0.95=−1.90 where the true step was +0.10. Against a per-step std of σ^≈0.1–0.3 that is a residual of tens of standard deviations — O(102) spurious nats — in both logPF and logPB, at every crossing step of every replayed trajectory. The gradient side is worse than the loss side: μθ at boundary-adjacent states is pushed toward a ±L/Δt jump it can never realize.

Defect 2 · the instability mechanism
Every trajectory that crosses a periodic bound enters the buffer as a replay-time outlier: two huge, fictitious residuals and violent policy gradients localized exactly at the walls. Poison rate ∝ (replay fraction) × (crossing rate). Forward-only training is immune, because _fwd_step scores its own crossings on the raw pair.

This also breaks the stated contract that buffered trajectories can be “exactly replayed” (train.py:4237): for any trajectory with a crossing, replay does not reproduce the rollout’s own log-probabilities. Note the buffered data was never wrong — wrapped states are the right thing to store; only the scoring is.

04The fix — one invariance rule

Every scored log-density must be a function of the wrapped trajectory:logP(τ)=logP(W(τ)).
Invariant under shifting any state of the trajectory by any multiple of the period, independently per state. Then all three paths compute the identical number from the stored representation, by construction — exact replay is restored as a theorem, not a hope.

Three rules achieve it:

R1 · nearest-image residuals
In both log-prob functions, wrap the residual on angular dims before standardizing: z=W(Δx−drift). Shifting either endpoint by Lk shifts Δx by a multiple of L, which W absorbs. This is the maximum-likelihood image of the wrapped Gaussian — the correct single-term approximation of the true circular kernel.
R2 · bridge drift at the wrapped representative
In _eval_pb_logprob, evaluate b(y)=−W(y)⋅ct⋅κθ. This makes PB single-valued on the circle and identical to what _bwd_step already does: the bridge pulls toward the source the short way around, from the canonical representative.
R3 · wrap immediately after sampling
In _fwd_step and _bwd_step, move _wrap_ang from after the log-prob block to directly after propagation. With R1+R2 in place the scored values no longer depend on this ordering — the move just makes the invariant manifest and keeps “the state” meaning one thing everywhere.

Under the fixed convention the worked example scores identically on every path: the backward residual is W(1.90−0.95ctκθ)=−0.10−0.95ctκθ — small, because on the circle 0.95 really is close to −0.95. Crossing transitions get sane backward mass; replay reproduces generation bit-for-bit.

05What changes numerically

The reason to trust this change is how little it moves:

QuantityWhereEffect of the fix
logPF, on-policy (fwd & bwd rollouts) all steps unchanged — the residual is the drawn noise, far inside one period, so W is the identity on it
logPB, non-crossing steps all paths unchanged — W(y)=y already
logPB, crossing steps, fwd path gfn.py:875 shifts by the drift gap Lctκθ — the only on-policy value that moves
replay of crossing trajectories both densities repaired: O(102)-nat fictitious residuals collapse to the on-policy values
steps of size ≥L/2 (large-exploration tails) rare now scored at the nearer image — the correct circular density, slightly higher than today’s
κθ (learned PB correction) checkpoints was trained against the inconsistent kernel; expect brief re-adaptation, no schema change

Expected magnitudes with boundary modes (2–4 angular dims, T ≈ 100)

Per crossing, the fwd-vs-canonical gap is ≈L|δ|/V(t)≈0.1–0.3 nats (the ΔV cancels between ct and βt2) — and it is one-signed: the raw convention’s drift always points back along the actual history, so it always flatters the observed transition. A chain converged onto a boundary mode flickers across the seam every few steps for the last quarter of the rollout: ~5–15 crossings per boundary dim, so ~10–40 per trajectory over 2–4 dims — a standing bias of ~1–5 nats per boundary-mode trajectory, ~0 on interior trajectories. Downstream channels: a persistent fwd-vs-bwd log Z handoff gap of ~0.5–3 nats concentrated on boundary-mode conditions; over-visiting of wall modes under fwd-dominant mixes (the TB target for log PF inherits the inflation); and an un-fittable tb_err floor of ~1–3 nats on the boundary subpopulation — invisible at tb_err 20, binding below ~3. κθ cannot cancel it: it shifts both streams together, so it re-centers the disagreement without closing it.

Scaling: per-crossing cost ∝T−1/2 but flicker count grows like T when mode width sits at the noise floor, so the total bias is constant-to-T growing under refinement, while the single-winding truncation error dies super-exponentially: small steps rescue the approximation, not the bias. Replay, for scale: ~180 nats × 10–40 crossings ≈ thousands of nats of fiction per stored boundary-mode trajectory, in both densities — and softmax admission preferentially retains exactly those low-energy wall-mode entries. Post-fix falsifiable signatures: fwd-vs-bwd log Z deltas close by ~1 nat specifically on boundary-mode conditions, and the boundary-condition tail of the tb_err distribution compresses. The conformer sampler is the maximal-exposure case: anti torsions sit exactly at ±π, so every anti-state trajectory is a boundary-mode trajectory by construction.

06Approximation honesty

The true kernel on the circle is the wrapped Gaussian ∑k𝒩(z+Lk); R1 keeps only the largest term. The nearest neglected image sits at least (L−|z|)/σ^ away — ten-plus standard deviations at operating noise levels, a relative mass below e−50 — and both directions share the convention, so the truncation largely cancels in the TB ratio. If it ever matters (very hot exploration), the exact version is a logsumexp over k∈{−1,0,1}; not warranted now.

The exact circular reversal, and where the single image sits inside it

For the reference process (wrapped Brownian motion from the source), the exact backward kernel follows from two lines of Bayes on the image sums — complete the square once per image:

PB⋆(u∣s)=∑k∈{−1,0,1}πk𝒩(u;(W(s)+Lk)(1−ctκθ),βt2),πk∝φ(W(s)+Lk;0,V(tnext)).(4)

A mixture over lifts: each component is the code’s own bridge applied to one image of the state, weighted by the probability the reference process arrived via that image. The two “candidate drifts” one is tempted to argue about at a crossing are exactly the k=0 and k=1 components of (4). The single-image kernel of R1+R2 is the max-weight term (k=0, since |W(s)|≤L/2), and it is the dominant term in both regimes: early in the trajectory V is small and the weights collapse exponentially onto the near lift; late in the trajectory the weights can approach 50/50 near the antipode, but the component means are separated by only Lct≪βt, so the components nearly coincide. Worst-case error of the single image: O((Lct/βt)2) nats, in the antipodal band. If post-fix diagnostics ever show a residual floor pinned there, (4) is the drop-in exact kernel: three means and a logsumexp on angular dims, still a function of (W(s),t) only — no winding required.

Adopting (4) — implementation notes: it is a PB-only change (~30–40 lines). Score: per-dim 3-component logsumexp in _eval_pb_logprob, lifts W(y)+{−L,0,L}, weights from var_accum(t_next), κθ applied per component; linear dims unchanged. Sample: _bwd_step draws a per-ang-dim categorical over the three lifts (one pre-drawn uniform tensor alongside eps, keeping the step checkpoint-pure), then the existing Gaussian. PF, endpoints, and all tests are untouched; add one continuity check across the seam. What (4) buys over the single image: removal of the Lct-sized kernel discontinuity at the seam (a kink smooth sin/cos-fed networks cannot represent — a residual floor of roughly 0.1–0.2 nats pinned at seam conditions) plus a systematic ~0.1–0.5 nats per boundary trajectory. Cosmetic at crystal precision (1–3 nat tb_err); material for conformer benchmarks measuring log Z at the 0.1-nat level with anti modes on the seam. Suggested wiring: one config boolean (pb_exact_reversal, default on) — a legitimate two-kernel A/B arm, not a compat shim.

DPLR: no interaction, twice over. DPLR is a forward-covariance feature — PB stays diagonal by design (gfn.py:575) — so the mixture never meets it; and with dplr_mask_angular on (the intended setting — see the get_periodic_dimensions docstring), angular dims carry no low-rank component, making the per-dim residual wrap exact for PF as well. Guardrail: assert the mask whenever angular dims and DPLR coexist.

07Implementation

The whole fix is small because _wrap_ang already does per-ang-dim wrapping on a full-width tensor — it works on residuals as well as states.

Step functions stay pure (the wrap is a deterministic tensor op), so traj_checkpoint recompute and compile_policy are unaffected; the wrap uses the same ang_idx index machinery already on the hot path. Deterministic endpoints need nothing: the pinned first forward step keeps logPB=0, and the deterministic jump to the source in _bwd_step is covered by R1 automatically.

08Tests, in order

  1. Representative invariance. Random transitions; shift any angular input by ±L; assert logPF and logPB unchanged to fp tolerance. Impossible before the fix.
  2. Exact replay round-trip. Seeded get_traj_fwd with forced crossings (bias the init toward the bound, or crank exploration), feed the returned states to get_traj_replay: allclose on both log-prob stacks. Must fail pre-fix on any crossing batch, pass post-fix.
  3. No-crossing regression. Seeded batch with no angular step exceeding L/2: pre- and post-fix values identical. Proves nothing moved off the boundary.
  4. (Optional) boundary-mode toy. 1D periodic target with the mode at ±1, replay-heavy mix: destabilizes pre-fix, trains post-fix.

09Rollout notes

  • No config flag. A broken kernel is not a behavior to preserve (house no-backward-compat policy). Old checkpoints load unchanged; κθ re-adapts within its ±40% range.
  • Buffers stay valid. Wrapped states were always the correct stored representation; no rebuild, no flush.
  • Expected signatures on periodic problems: the replay tb-error outlier band should collapse immediately; means_b diagnostics shift at crossings; z-level estimators may move slightly, so compare runs only within matched code.
  • Falsifiable pre-check on existing data: score any stored buffer, flag trajectories with a consecutive-state angular jump above L/2, and compare their replay residuals against the rest. Today the two populations should separate almost perfectly; post-fix they should merge.
  • Scope note: the conformer sampler’s state is entirely angular (torsions), so it inherits every part of this fix; crystal problems inherit it on phi/r and on the periodic_centroids axes. Eval-side KDE/figure code that differences wrapped samples is out of scope here.

10Alternative considered — storing unwrapped trajectories

Storing raw (never-wrapped) states makes replay exact by construction, so it matches the nearest-image fix on Defect 2. The question is whether it additionally rescues “drift toward the pre-wrap origin.” It cannot, for three stacked reasons:

  1. Winding intake. Backward rollouts start from buffer anchors, MCMC-reheated seeds, and dataset/curated terminals — none of which possess a winding. They would necessarily start at k=0, i.e. the wrapped representative. Forward-generated and backward-generated visits to the same physical state would still score different backward kernels: Defect 1 survives, relocated to the anchor intake. Worse, κθ then receives identical sin/cos inputs carrying two path-family-dependent regression targets — a single function must average them, an irreducible loss floor pinned exactly at the walls.
  2. Winding as physical state. Followed to completion (reward defined on the covering line as R(W(x))), the target is improper: a periodic reward on ℝ has infinite total mass, so exact TB balance is unattainable and the reachable images of each boundary mode split its mass while Z inflates by the covered-image count — a wrapped-marginal bias concentrated precisely on wall-adjacent modes. The alternative closure — confining the sampler to the fundamental domain with energy walls — reinstates the face-pinning that the periodic feature exists to remove.
  3. No middle ground. The winding is either part of the state (with the pathologies above) or a gauge degree of freedom — and gauge variables must not appear in scored quantities. The nearest-image fix is exactly the gauge-invariant scoring.

Relatedly: agreement of the forward and backward drifts is not a requirement of the framework and never held anywhere — on linear dims the bridge points at the source while PF points outward toward modes on every step of every outbound trajectory. What the two directions must agree on is the identity of the displacement being scored, which is what R1 enforces. The entire cost of the source-image convention is a residual shift of exactly L⋅ct⋅κθ between the two candidate images — sub-σ^ beyond the earliest steps — versus the L≈19σ^ cliff that was the actual bug.
