# Learning rate

*Drift: **M** (mixed). Verified against commit `78de930`, 2026-10-04; the controller section, the config keys and the code list re-verified 2026-10-28 against the working tree after the bracket's deletion and the replacement of burn-in and the promotion ramp by `cfg:lr_control.schedule`, neither of which is in a commit yet. Sources at the end.*

Five config keys name a learning rate, four of them can be reached by one run-level scale, and one controller writes every rate an optimizer group runs at. This page covers those keys, the optimizers they reach, the controller and its tripwire, and what the width and length scaling argument says a rate is. Gradient clipping is a separate bar ([gradient-clipping](gradient-clipping.md)); what a stage transition does to the optimizers belongs to [stage-protocol-engine](stage-protocol-engine.md); the batch a rate is applied over is [batch-size](batch-size.md).

## The rate keys and the optimizers

`train.py::Modeller.init_schedulers_optimizers` builds five Adam optimizers and rebuilds them at every stage transition: `fwd`, `bwd`, `replay`, `fused` and `flow`. The first four take the policy parameters; `flow` takes the Z head alone; `fused` takes the policy parameters *and* the flow head as a trailing param group constructed at `cfg:lr_flow`, so a fused step updates Z through the same optimizer. The four policy optimizers are constructed at `cfg:lr_policy`, `cfg:lr_back`, `cfg:lr_replay` and `cfg:lr_fused` each multiplied by the current stage's schedule value at entry (`controller.py::LRController.entry_scale`, segment 0's `to`), or by 1.0 with no `lr_control` block; that is the value in force for the first train step of a build, before the controller's first `step()` runs.

Which of them is stepped follows from the stage's `train_mode`, not from the key. `train.py::Modeller.step_loss` steps `optimizers[step_type]`, and additionally steps `optimizers['flow']` whenever `step_type` is not `fused`. A `bwd` stage steps `bwd` and `flow`; a `fused` stage steps `fused` alone, its trailing group carrying the flow head. The optimizers a stage does not step are still constructed and still have their rates written and published; they accumulate no Adam moments while the stage runs, and the rebuild at the next transition discards what any of them holds.

`auto` is resolved at load. `utils.py::resolve_derived_config` walks `utils.py::_LR_KEYS` (`lr_policy`, `lr_replay`, `lr_back`, `lr_fused`), replaces any `auto` or absent value with `cfg:lr_control.seed_lr`, and records the names it replaced on `args.lr_servo_managed`. An `auto` rate with no `lr_control` block raises at load (`utils.py::_require_lr_control`, `config_invariants.py::auto_lr_requires_lr_control`). `lr_flow` is not in `_LR_KEYS`, and `auto` is rejected for it at load.

`controller.py::LRController._apply_lrs` writes every group each time it runs, which is from `LRController.step` on the 10-step reporting clock (`train.py::Modeller.step_lr_schedule`) and from `LRController.set_scale` each time the scale is set: `lr = base x scale` for a group whose base key is in the managed set, `base` unmodified otherwise, and `cfg:lr_flow` for the flow groups unless `cfg:lr_control.control_flow_lr` is set. `cfg:max_lr` caps every group this method writes, the flow group included; `cfg:min_lr` floors every group except the flow groups taken by the pinned branch, which are written at `cfg:lr_flow` and capped but not floored. `train.py::Modeller.ten_step_reporting` publishes the live rates per optimizer as `lr_fwd`, `lr_bwd`, `lr_replay`, `lr_fused` and `lr_flow`, with `lr_fused_flow` carrying the fused optimizer's trailing flow group; the multiplier and the rail counters are `lr_ctrl/scale`, `lr_ctrl/lr_floored_groups` and `lr_ctrl/lr_capped_groups`, the last only where a cap is configured. The stage action `set_lr_flow:<float>` sets `cfg:lr_flow` at a boundary.

## The controller

`cfg:lr_control.mode` is `fixed`, the only mode; `bracket`, the candidate-grid search, was deleted on 2026-10-28, and `controller.py::LRController` raises on it at construction, as `config_invariants.py::lr_control_is_well_formed` reports it at load. The scale is

$$\text{scale} = s(n) \times \text{cut},$$

with $n$ the train steps since the stage's entry step and $s$ the stage's schedule: `cfg:stage.lr_schedule` where the stage declares one, else `cfg:lr_control.schedule` (`LRController._schedule`). A schedule is an ordered list of segments `{to, steps, kind}`. Segment 0 starts at its own `to`, and its kind must be `hold`; each later segment moves from the previous segment's `to` to its own over `steps` train steps, with progress $p \in [0, 1]$ and endpoints $a \to b$: `hold` is $b$ throughout (a jump at the segment's start), `linear` is $a + (b - a)p$, `geometric` and `exp` are both $a (b/a)^p$, and `cosine` is $b + (a - b)(1 + \cos \pi p)/2$. `steps: 0` is a jump; `steps: null` runs the segment to the stage's `max_steps` and is allowed only on the last segment. Past the last segment its `to` holds (`controller.py::lr_schedule_value`, `lr_schedule_ends`). `config_invariants.py::lr_schedule_problems` refuses an unknown kind, a `to` that is not a positive finite number, a negative or non-integer `steps`, a first segment other than a hold, a `null` segment that is not last, and, judged against the stage's cap, a non-hold `null` segment on a stage with no `max_steps`: `protocol.py::Stage._parse_lr_schedule` raises on a stage's own schedule, `LRController._schedule` on the run-level one when a capless stage would run it, and `lr_control_is_well_formed` reports both at load. An `lr_control` block with no `schedule` raises.

The clock is anchored at the stage's entry step, stamped in `stage_ctrl['lr_sched_entry']` by `LRController.on_stage_change` or, for a stage entered without one, at the first `LRController.tick` (`LRController._elapsed`). `stage_ctrl` is checkpointed, so a rewind or a resume continues mid-schedule, and the schedule itself is re-read from the config by each process. `LRController.tick` runs once per host-loop iteration and sets the scale to $s(n) \times \text{cut}$ whenever that product differs from the value it last applied, so a scale set by another caller of `LRController.set_scale` stands until the schedule or the cut moves. `cut` lives in the checkpointed `lr_ctrl` dict (`{ver, scale, cut}`, `LRController._state`); a dict of another `ver` is discarded. The phase is burn-in while $n$ is inside segment 0 and cruise after it; `LRController.in_warmup` is an alias of `in_burn_in`. With no `lr_control` block at all the run takes `config_invariants.py::legacy_lr_schedule`'s defaults, a hold at 0.05 for 3000 steps and a geometric move to 1.0 over 1000, and no key is scale-managed. `cfg:lr_control.verbose` is accepted by the schema and read by nothing.

`burn_in_steps`, `burn_in_scale`, `promotion_ramp_steps` and `fixed_scale` were replaced by `cfg:lr_control.schedule` on 2026-10-28 (`config_invariants.py::LR_SCHEDULE_REPLACED_KEYS`). Off the conformer route they raise at `LRController` construction and `config_invariants.py::lr_schedule_replaces_burn_in_keys` reports them at load (`config_invariants.py::replaced_lr_schedule_keys`). On `energy_function` `conformer_torsions`, whose configs still carry them, a block with no `schedule` is read through `legacy_lr_schedule` as `[{to: burn_in_scale, steps: burn_in_steps, kind: hold}, {to: fixed_scale, steps: promotion_ramp_steps, kind: geometric}]`, and a block carrying both raises. They are not in `utils.py::_RETIRED_KEYS`, because `utils.py::preflight_config` is shared with that route.

The nine bracket keys (`config_invariants.py::LR_CONTROL_RETIRED_KEYS`) raise at `LRController` construction off the conformer route, and `config_invariants.py::lr_bracket_keys_are_retired` reports them at load; on `energy_function` `conformer_torsions`, whose configs still carry some of them, both ignore them (`config_invariants.py::retired_lr_control_keys`).

Divergence is judged by `hard_failure_bars.py::HardFailureBars`. Per channel in `fwd`, `bwd`, `replay`, `fused`, the loss bar is $\mathrm{hi} + k\,(\mathrm{hi} - \mathrm{lo})$ over the tail `cfg:lr_control.hard_failure.root_window` of the window, with $k$ = `loss_excursion_k`; the gradient bar is `grad_excursion_x` times the largest finite pre-clip norm in the same window. A channel with fewer than `min_observations` finite values gets no bar. `loss_abs` and `grad_abs` are absolute backstops, and a non-finite reading fires by kind. The bars are fitted at the tick on which $n$ reaches the end of segment 0, from the window as it stands, its last `root_window` observations (`LRController._fit_warmup_bars`); where no channel then holds `min_observations`, and in a process that starts past segment 0 (a resume), `LRController.tick` fits them once the window holds `root_window` observations.

`LRController.observe` runs on every training step with the step's loss and `last_grad_norm_pre_clip`, appends to the root window and judges. An excursion verdict is gated by a cooldown of `cfg:lr_control.fire_cooldown_steps`, increments `_moderate_fires` and prints `lr_ctrl FIRE`; any other verdict prints `lr_ctrl DISASTER`. Both return `'diverged'` and lead into `train.py::Modeller.fire_loss_spike`, which picks a rewind target in the order same-stage `last_ok`, fresh same-stage `best`, same-stage `running`, this stage's `stage_start`, then bare `best` (`train.py::Modeller._rewind_checkpoint_path`), restores weights, optimizers, modeller state and the metric tracker from it, and keeps `step_ind`. The live buffers are kept. `last_ok` is a link to `running`, remade on the same 50-step clock that writes `running` whenever the combo-loss record's latest sample is at or below the record minimum plus `cfg:rewind_tolerance` times the absolute value of that minimum.

`LRController.on_divergence` is then called once, post-restore. It is the single counting seat for `lr_ctrl/divergences`, and it cuts: `lr_ctrl['cut']`, restored from the rewind target, and the live scale are multiplied by `cfg:lr_control.fire_cut_factor`, and the cut multiplies the schedule for the rest of the stage. The canonical config sets `fire_cut_factor` to 1.0, at which the cut is an identity. `cfg:max_reloads_per_1k_steps` is a rate budget: `fire_loss_spike` raises `FrozenTrainingState` once total rewinds exceed that rate times steps elapsed, floor 3. Where no rewind target exists the cut is taken alone and a warning is printed. The first bars are fitted at segment 0's rate. With `cruise_rederive` set, `LRController._arm_cruise_bar` queues a refit at the tick on which $n$ reaches the end of the last segment whose `to` differs from the previous segment's, provided a bar has been fitted by then; a schedule in which no later `to` differs queues none, and a hold between two rate changes runs on the bars fitted before it. `LRController._tick_cruise_bar` counts the calls of `LRController.tick`, one per training-loop iteration, from the queueing on. At a count of `cruise_settle_steps` the windows are cleared; the bars are then refitted once a channel's window holds `root_window` observations, or at a count of `cruise_settle_steps` plus four times `root_window` where no window has filled by then (`lr_bracket/bars_redrawn`); a channel the new window cannot fit keeps its previous bar, and all of them are kept when nothing can be fitted (`LRController._redraw_bars_for_cruise`). The first bars stay live until the refit. `LRController.on_stage_change`, called by `protocol.py::StageProtocol.advance` with the new stage current, stamps the new entry step, resets the cut to 1, clears the windows, the bars and a pending refit, and sets the scale to the new stage's segment-0 `to`. `LRController.report` publishes `lr_ctrl/scale`, `lr_ctrl/cut`, `lr_ctrl/segment` (the index of the segment $n$ is in), `lr_bracket/phase` and `lr_bracket/promoted_steps`, the cruise ticks this process has counted.

`cfg:stage.hot_lr_sensor` is a per-stage drawdown sensor on one logged channel (`protocol.py::Stage._parse_hot_lr_sensor`, `train.py::Modeller.observe_hot_lr`, `lr_hot_sensor.py::HotSensor`). It pushes the tracker's smoothed value for `channel` into a window of `rows` fresh rows, takes the `floor_percentile` of that window as a floor, and fires on a single row when the drawdown, $\log(\text{value}/\text{floor})$ under `form: log` or $\text{value} - \text{floor}$ under `form: absolute`, exceeds `above`. `action` is a closed vocabulary of `report`, the value taken by omission, which moves nothing, and `fire`, which increments the controller's `_moderate_fires`, takes and re-arms the controller's fire cooldown, drops the sensor's window and calls `fire_loss_spike`. `floor_percentile` defaults to 10.0 and `form` to `log`. `HotSensor` raises unless the `MetricTracker` period the run builds equals the period the thresholds were calibrated at, `lr_hot_sensor.py::CALIBRATED_PERIOD` = 100. The canonical config carries four declarations across its two protocols, all of them leaving `action` at its default: `bwd/mle` absolute over 31 rows above 5.0 on `train_prior` in both protocols, `fwd/scatter_err` over 11 rows above 2.0 on `equilibration` in `unconditional_tb`, and `bwd/logw_std_within` over 11 rows above 3.0 on `var_conditioning` in `conditional_vargrad`.

## Scaling in width and length

`docs/design/width_and_length_scaling.md` derives one distinction. The TB residual $\delta = \log Z + \sum_t (\log P_F - \log P_B) - \log R$ is intensive: $T$ discretizes a fixed horizon, so $\delta$ converges to its Girsanov limit rather than accumulating, and the bounded heads make it width-independent. Anything measured in loss space is therefore $(W, T)$-flat: the Huber knee, the fracs, `deactivate_threshold`, the divergence loss bars. The raw gradient flows through weights shared across all $T$ steps, so it is extensive, growing with $T$ and as $\sqrt{W}$. One value is derived from that: `cfg:gradient_norm_clip` written `auto`, resolved at load against a table of measured pre-clip medians and a $\sqrt{W}$ factor. The note names as the primitives of a rescale the `cfg:model.*_hidden_dim` fields, set equal to each other, and `cfg:integrator.T` with `cfg:eval_T`, which must match it; in the code the crystal route has one length, `cfg:model.T` (`model.sde.T` in `mk_dev.yaml`), read for training and evaluation alike. The note states that the learning rates carry no $(W, T)$ rule, and none is applied in code. Note and code differ on what `auto` means: the note says an `auto` rate is seeded at `adaptive_lr.seed_lr` and owned by whichever adaptive sensor the stage declares; the code seeds it at `cfg:lr_control.seed_lr`, and a stage's `lr_sensor` reaches no learning rate.

## Adaptive LR sensing, as of 2026-09-20

A stage may declare `cfg:stage.lr_sensor`, parsed by `protocol.py::Stage._parse_lr_sensor` against `protocol.py::LR_SENSOR_KINDS`, which is `ray` and `none`. `kind: ray` accepts `period`, a positive multiple of 10, and `n_sub`, at least 2; it runs the alpha-star ray calibration (`ray_calibration.py`, batches harvested by `lr_larder.py`), which scores the composite the stage's step descends over a set of step sizes and reports through `raycal/`. `kind: none` takes no other keys. `ray` reaches no learning rate: `LRController.on_calibration` records a count and a last value and returns, and `lr_ctrl/calibrations` is published only when the sensor ran. `kind: hyper`, the hypergradient cosine between the current gradient and the displacement the previous optimizer step realised, was removed on 2026-09-20; it is listed in `protocol.py::RETIRED_LR_SENSOR_KINDS` and a stage declaring it raises at load rather than being ignored. No stage in the canonical config declares an `lr_sensor` block.

## Could be tooling

Two checks are mechanical. The first is a stepped-versus-constructed table: `train_mode` fixes which optimizers `step_loss` reaches and `lr_servo_managed` fixes which keys the scale reaches, so a generator can print per stage which of the five `lr_*` keys is live and which is inert for the stage's whole length. A key inert in every stage reads as a rate and is never applied, and that is derivable at config-generation time rather than at step 0. The second is a resolved-rate print, per optimizer: `base x scale` at each segment boundary of each stage's schedule and after $n$ fire cuts, rails marked where they bind.
