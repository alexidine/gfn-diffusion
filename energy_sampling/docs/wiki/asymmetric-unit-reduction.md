# Asymmetric unit reduction

*Drift: **T** (theory). Verified against commit `a637e70`, 2026-09-20. Sources at the end.*

A crystal here is parameterised by a cell plus a *pose* for each asymmetric unit: a fractional centroid `aunit_centroid` and an orientation `aunit_orientation`. The pose is not uniquely determined by the crystal. Poses related by $G$'s own operators are already identified, by `crystal_building/utils.py::identify_canonical_asymmetric_unit` picking the copy whose fractional centroid lands in the tabulated asymmetric unit box. The box table it is passed is `constants/asymmetric_units.py::ASYM_UNITS`, which is `::RAW_ASYM_UNITS` padded with $[1,1,1]$ for every space-group number the raw table has no entry for. What survives that fold is larger: an affine map not in $G$ that carries $G$ to itself sends one pose to a different pose for the *same crystal*, in the same setting, under the same operators `constants/space_group_info.py::SYM_OPS`. Those maps form the Euclidean normaliser $N_E(G)$; the residual pose redundancy is $N_E(G)/G$.

This page is about the pose only. The cell parameters' redundancy under integer basis change is [cell-fundamental-domains](cell-fundamental-domains.md); which latent rows the diffusion holds fixed is [latent-dimension-structure](latent-dimension-structure.md).

## The group: a discrete quotient and a continuous part

$N_E(G)/G$ splits into two pieces that behave differently.

**Discrete cosets.** `constants/space_group_info.py::NORMALIZER_OPS` maps each space-group number, as a string key, to fractional $(W, w)$ pairs: the coset representatives of $N_E(G)$ beyond the identity, generated from cctbx's `expand_addl_generators_of_euclidean_normalizer(True, True)` by `constants/generate_normalizers.py`. Read at the stamped commit, 228 of the 230 entries are non-empty; the candidate pose count is one plus the list length, 8 for sg 2 and sg 14, 4 for sg 15, 16 for sg 19. In 132 entries some $W$ has negative determinant.

**A continuous part.** A fractional direction $d$ fixed by every rotation part of $G$, $d \in \bigcap_k \ker(R_k - I)$, generates a continuous subgroup of $N_E(G)$: translating the centroid along $d$ displaces every symmetry copy by the same $d$, a rigid translation of the crystal, so the coordinate is an origin choice. `constants/space_group_info.py::CONTINUOUS_DIMS` holds these as cctbx structure seminvariants, three vectors per space group; 68 of the 230 entries have a non-zero component. `crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.build_free_axis_tensor` reduces that to a `[231, 3]` boolean lookup of free centroid axes, its docstring recording agreement with the direct derivation from `SYM_OPS` for all 230. P1 has all three free; the centrosymmetric groups have none.

## The fundamental domain of the pose

A fundamental domain for the pose holds exactly one point of each $N_E(G)/G$ orbit in $(\text{centroid}, \text{orientation})$ space. Where the quotient is generated by pure translations the orbit acts on the centroid alone and the domain is a sub-box of the asymmetric unit box: for sg 14 `docs/findings.md` F-007 records measured extents $[0.25, 0.25, 0.5]$ against the aunit box $[1, 0.25, 1]$, a volume of one eighth, matching the coset count. Where a representative has a non-trivial point part $W$ the orbit moves the orientation too and the domain is not a product box. `docs/design/fundamental_domain.md` records that per-axis division of the seminvariant moduli gives the right domain volume and the wrong shape, and that only sg 14 has been measured against a prediction.

An improper representative is not always a redundancy: it maps a chiral molecule to its enantiomer, a different crystal rather than a second description of one. The admissible coset set therefore depends on the molecule, which the derivation records as making the domain per molecule for the 132 space groups with a negative-determinant representative.

## What the code has

`crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps._transform_aunit_params` applies one fractional affine operation $(R_{\text{frac}}, t_{\text{frac}})$ to `(aunit_centroid, aunit_orientation, aunit_handedness)`, returning the pose describing that operation applied to every atom: the centroid transforms fractionally and is wrapped, the orientation is conjugated into Cartesian as $R_{\text{cart}} = T_{fc} R_{\text{frac}} T_{cf}$, composed with the stored rotation and right-multiplied by $\mathrm{diag}(\det R_{\text{cart}}, 1, 1)$, and the handedness is multiplied by $\det R_{\text{cart}}$.

`crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.get_fd_params` walks the cosets with that primitive, asserting one space group per batch and raising `NotImplementedError` for a space group with no representatives on file. Candidate 0 is the current pose; for each $(W, w)$ it builds the transformed pose, folds it back into $G$'s box by rebuilding the crystal (`pose_aunit`, `build_unit_cell`, `reparameterize_unit_cell`) rather than re-deriving the fold symbolically, and marks it invalid when $\det W < 0$ and the molecule is chiral, or when the rebuild reports the asymmetric unit not well defined. The winner is a lexicographic minimum over centroid $x, y, z$ then orientation $x, y, z$ at `atol` `1e-6`; the in-code note records that a joint norm under-reduces when a generator couples two centroid dimensions. `::fold_to_fd` is the in-place counterpart. `is_chiral` defaults to `None`, at which the gate reduces to `(~True) | (det_W > 0)`, admitting only proper representatives.

Neither `get_fd_params` nor `fold_to_fd` has a caller outside `crystal_ops.py` in either repository. In the training path, `::latent_transform` divides the centroid by the asymmetric unit box edge `auvs` drawn from `ASYM_UNITS` and rescales to $[-1, 1]$, and `::inv_latent_transform` multiplies back, so the latent centroid spans $G$'s box, not a normaliser fundamental domain.

The continuous part is wired. `crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.canonicalize_free_axes` sets the centroid to `auv * 0.5`, the aunit box centre, on every free axis, which is latent 0 under that transform; its docstring records the centre being chosen over the face because frac 0 is a wrap seam where `auv == 1`. It returns `False` untouched at $Z' > 1$, where the free translation is one global shift shared by all $Z'$ molecules. `::latent_params` calls it under `gauge_fix_free_axes`, default `True`, which the toy routes pass as `False`.

## Orientation

`aunit_orientation` is a rotation vector, axis $\mathbf{r}/\|\mathbf{r}\|$ and angle $\|\mathbf{r}\|$, converted by `common/geometry_utils.py::rotvec2rotmat` and `::rotmat2rotvec`; no quaternion representation appears. The double cover $\mathbf{r} \sim -\mathbf{r}$ with angle $2\pi - \|\mathbf{r}\|$ is quotiented by `crystal_building/utils.py::canonicalize_rotvec`, flipping any rotvec of negative $z$ onto the $+z$ half sphere; `geometry_utils.py::sph_rotvec2lat` then maps $\theta \in [0, \pi/2]$, $\phi \in [-\pi, \pi]$, $r \in [0, 2\pi]$ onto $[-1, 1]$.

The rotvec is expressed relative to the molecule's *standard* frame, so that convention is part of the pose parameterisation. `mol_methods.py::MolDataMethods.orient_molecule` with `mode` in `{'standardized', 'std', 'standard'}` recentres and calls `crystal_building/utils.py::align_mol_batch_to_standard_axes`, which takes principal inertial axes over heavy atoms from `geometry_utils.py::batch_molecule_principal_axes_torch` and rotates the molecule so that basis maps onto $\mathrm{diag}(h, 1, 1)$ for supplied handedness $h$. The axis signs are fixed inside that principal-axes function: each axis's overlap with the centroid-to-furthest-heavy-atom vector (`::batch_get_furthest_node_vector`, `::get_overlaps`) is forced positive by `::correct_Ip_directions`, whose two call sites both carry the comment that the fix "fails for mirror planes, on top of symmetric and spherical tops". `::correct_Ip_directions` is defined twice in the same file, the second shadowing the first. The source eigenbasis has unconstrained determinant and the target is fixed, so the map is orthogonal but not necessarily in $SO(3)$; `handedness` sets only the target frame. The sign fix depends on which heavy atom is furthest and on eigenvector ordering, either of which can change under a rotation of the input, so the map is not in general idempotent. `build_qm9_conditions.py::pin_to_trainer_frame` applies the standardisation, applies it a second time on a probe clone, jitters the atoms of every molecule that moved by more than `tol` and retries up to `max_attempts`, and raises when structures still marked `crystal_valid=1` have moved by more than `1e-3` A. A reflection cannot be encoded in a rotvec, and `::rotmat2rotvec` prints `These are reflections (not in SO(3)) and cannot be converted to rotation vectors.`

`crystal_building/utils.py::get_aunit_positions` applies that alignment when `std_orientation=True` and only recentres when `False`, so the stored rotvec means a different Cartesian orientation under the two settings. `energies/molecular_crystal.py` builds its `analyze_kwargs` with `std_orientation=False`, overridable through `cfg:energy_config.analyze_kwargs`, while `crystal_building.py::MolCrystalBuilding.pose_aunit` defaults to `True`. `buffer.py::CrystalBuffer._orient_stored_batch` calls `orient_molecule(mode="std")` once at admission, its docstring stating that std orientation is idempotent.

## The 39 space groups

`docs/design/fundamental_domain.md` defines the deployable set for the normaliser reduction by four conditions: a tabulated asymmetric unit box, passing the box coverage check (`dataset_utils/normalizer_reduction/validate_asym_units.py`), $N_E(G)/G$ generated by pure translations so the fold never acts in orientation space, and no free dimensions. The listed set is `2, 10-15, 47, 49, 51-58, 60-67, 69, 71-74, 91, 92, 95, 96, 124, 128, 131, 135, 142`, 39 numbers. Three conditions are checkable against the tables at the stamped commit: all 39 have an entry in `RAW_ASYM_UNITS` (123 of 230 do), all 39 have all-identity point parts in `NORMALIZER_OPS` (82 of 230 do), and none has a non-zero `CONTINUOUS_DIMS` vector.

## Where the derivation and the code differ

The derivation says nothing consumes the normaliser machinery and `CONTINUOUS_DIMS` has zero importers; the code imports it in `crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.build_free_axis_tensor` and `models/dead_latent_rows.py` and runs `canonicalize_free_axes` inside `latent_params` by default, while `NORMALIZER_OPS` is read only by the callerless `get_fd_params`.

The derivation's fix for a continuous redundancy is to delete the axis, $\text{data\_ndim} = 6 + 6Z' - n_{\text{dead}}$; the code keeps the full width, `models/dead_latent_rows.py::latent_ndim` returning $6 + 6Z'$, and gauge-fixes the row to the box centre instead.

The derivation lists the discarded `h_folded` in `get_fd_params` as a plumbing gap; the code still discards it, so the folded handedness is not written back.

The derivation treats the free-axis gauge fix as applying at any $Z'$; the code returns untouched at $Z' > 1$, naming sg 9 $Z'=2$ as the open case.

The docstring of `geometry_utils.py::lat2sph_rotvec` gives the latent $\theta$ range as $[\pi/4, 3\pi/4]$; the arithmetic in both that function and its inverse `::sph_rotvec2lat` gives $[0, \pi/2]$.
