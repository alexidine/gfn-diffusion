# Crystal force fields

*Drift: **T** (theory). Verified against commit `78de930`, 2026-10-04. Sources at the end.*

A molecular crystal's reward is built from an *intermolecular* energy: the energy of one molecule in the periodic field of its neighbours, with the molecule's internal energy held out; the conformer is rigid on this route. The model supplying that number is selected by one string, `cfg:energy_function`, read into `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal`: an explicit pair sum over a finite cluster (`elj` and its siblings `lj`, `qlj`, `silu`), or a machine-learned interatomic potential (`uma`, `mace`). The Buckingham-form `bh` and the electrostatic `es` are further pair-sum compute keys of mxtaltools' `analyze`; `::MolecularCrystal.generator_energy` has no branch for them, its crystal branch listing `lj`, `qlj`, `elj`, `silu`, `uma` and `mace` and its final `else` being `assert False` with the message `<name> not implemented`. What follows is the functional forms, the parameters they carry, the cluster they are evaluated on, and what "lattice energy" means per backend. How an MLIP call is routed, chunked, timed and recovered from is [mlip-energy-routes](mlip-energy-routes.md). How the per-molecule energy becomes a reward, with the density, bounding, pressure and cell-reduction terms it is summed with, is [reward-construction](reward-construction.md); the reduction term's geometry is [cell-fundamental-domains](cell-fundamental-domains.md).

## The cluster every model scores

Every pair-sum route reads a finite cluster of explicit atoms, built by `crystal_building.py::MolCrystalBuilding.mol2cluster`: the asymmetric unit is posed, the unit cell is filled by the space group's stored operators, and `crystal_building/utils.py::ucell2cluster` tiles that cell over integer translations.

Which translations are kept is a molecule test (MXtalTools working tree of 2026-10-18, not yet committed; this paragraph is not covered by the stamp above). `ucell2cluster` passes the all-atom centroids of the unit cell's molecules, and `crystal_building/utils.py::get_cart_translations` keeps a lattice translation when some molecule of the translated cell has its centroid within $\rho = \text{cutoff} + \text{zp\_buffer} + 2R + \epsilon$ of the centroid of the cluster's molecule 0: the distance the paring below flags at (the search adds a 1e-4 Å margin), so the cell of every unflagged molecule is built, and a cell is built only if it holds a molecule within that distance. Molecule 0 is the first image in the built unit cell (`crystal_building/utils.py::reference_molecule_reach`), not the posed `pos`: the unit cell sits about `aunit_centroid` clipped to $[0, 1 - 10^{-4}]$, `pos` about the unclipped value, so at a centroid component of 1.0 the two are $10^{-4}$ of that lattice vector apart. $R$ is molecule 0's all-atom reach, the largest distance from its unweighted all-atom centroid to any of its atoms (`crystal_building/utils.py::molecule_reach`), and $\epsilon$ is `crystal_building/utils.py::REACH_MARGIN` (1e-3 Å, for float32 rounding). Every molecule of the cluster is an image of molecule 0 with the same $R$, so two molecules hold an atom pair closer than the cutoff only if their centroids are closer than $\text{cutoff} + 2R$: the test is exact, hydrogens included, and a flagged molecule holds no pair within the cutoff (`tests/test_cluster_reach.py` and the brute-force lattice sums of `tests/characterization/test_characterization_oracle.py`, MXtalTools). Until 2026-10-23 the distance was $2r_{\text{mol}} + \text{cutoff} + 0.1$ about the posed `pos`, with $r_{\text{mol}}$ the heavy-atom radius about the heavy-atom centroid (`radius`), which hydrogens reach past: the builder missed a few pairs inside the cutoff (pyridone P2₁/c, 2 of 4995 at 10 Å, ΔeLJ −0.0037 raw; phenacetin P2₁/c, 4 of 2305 at 6 Å, ΔeLJ −0.088 raw). Exactness costs atoms where hydrogens reach past the heavy-atom radius: at 10 Å the window (`aux_ind < 2`) grows 7.4 % on 512 MIPCAS sg 2 prior rows and 38.5 % on acridine sg 14, and shrinks 1.6 % on qm9full (MXtalTools `tests/characterization/README.md`, regeneration log of 2026-10-23). The zero translation is always kept. The search is exact in any cell basis: each lattice basis is size-reduced first (`crystal_building/utils.py::_reduce_lattice_bases`), and each molecule's sphere is searched over the integer box its interplanar spacings bound, so the set of unflagged molecules does not depend on the basis the cell is written in or on the crystal's batch-mates (the kept cells and the atom order do depend on the basis). `supercell_size` is accepted and ignored. A crystal keeps at most `crystal_building/utils.py::MAX_CELLS_PER_CRYSTAL` translations, the cells whose nearest molecule is closest (owner decision, 2026-10-18); only a cell much smaller than its molecule reaches the cap, and its energy then comes from those nearest cells. A crystal with a non-finite or singular cell, or a non-finite pose, keeps only its zero translation with a warning, and `crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.analyze` returns NaN in all its per-graph outputs (`crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis._nan_undefined_crystals`), so one bad draw neither stops the batch nor scores a finite energy (working tree of 2026-10-19; before, a non-finite cell raised in the builder and a non-finite pose scored eLJ 0). Called without molecule centroids, the function keeps a translation whose vector lies within $2r_{\text{mol}} + \text{cutoff} + 0.1 + D$ of the centroid, with

$$D = \max_{\mathbf{v}\in\{100,010,001,110,101,011,111\}} \left| \mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{A} \right|,$$

the largest norm over the seven non-origin corners of the cell parallelepiped. Until 2026-10-18 that sphere test was the builder's, searched over one integer cube per batch in the given basis and capped by `supercell_size`; in oblique bases it missed needed cells, and eLJ came out wrong by up to tens of raw units, by amounts that depended on the batch. When the batch's `max_z_prime` is above 1, `mol2cluster` splits each row into one graph per asymmetric-unit molecule, and the cluster of unit $v$ holds images of $v$ only; `zp_buffer` (`crystal_building.py::MolCrystalBuilding._unit_reach_buffer`, from each unit's molecule 0 in its built unit cell) is then $\max_u (R_u + |c_u - c_v|) - R_v$ over the units $u$ of $v$'s own row, so that unit $v$'s cluster reaches $\text{cutoff} + R_v + \max_u(R_u + |c_u - c_v|)$ and holds every image of $v$ with an atom within the cutoff of any asymmetric-unit molecule of its crystal. It is exactly zero for a Z'=1 row, and it reads no pad slot and no batch-mate, so a row builds the same cluster as it would alone in a width-1 batch. Until 2026-10-23 the buffer was the largest distance between the row's own centroid slots, on top of a reach built from the row's summed heavy-atom radius (pad slots ignored since 2026-10-22). `crystal_building/utils.py::_pare_cluster_molwise` then sets `aux_ind = 2` on every atom of a molecule whose centroid is further than $\rho$ from molecule 0's; those atoms stay in the cluster and enter no edge, so the interacting set is whole molecules rather than cut mid-molecule.
`crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.construct_radial_graph` then calls `vdw_analysis.py::get_intermolecular_dists_dict`, which builds two edge lists through `radial_graph.py::build_radial_graph`: `edge_index`, with both ends in the asymmetric unit (`aux_ind == 0`), and `edge_index_inter`, whose row 0 (the source) is an atom of the convolutional window (`aux_ind == 1`) and whose row 1 (the target) is an asymmetric-unit atom. Where $Z' > 1$, edges inside the asymmetric unit that cross `mol_ind` are moved from the first list to the second, so "intermolecular" means *between molecules*, not *between asymmetric units*. Every pair potential below is a sum over `edge_index_inter` alone, scattered by `intermolecular_dist_batch`, the graph index of the *source* atom; each edge ends on an asymmetric-unit atom, so the sum is a per-asymmetric-unit quantity. Both `cutoff` and `max_num_neighbors` are caps, and a neighbour count saturating at either truncates the list. `MolecularCrystal.analyze_crystal_batch` passes `cutoff=10`, `supercell_size=10`, `std_orientation=False`, with `cfg:energy_config.analyze_kwargs` overriding on top.

## ELJ

The base form, `vdw_analysis.py::compute_lj_edgewise`, is a 12-6 Lennard-Jones with $\varepsilon$ hard-coded to 1 and $\sigma$ taken as the *sum of the two atoms' van der Waals radii* from `constants/atom_properties.py::VDW_RADII`, an element-indexed table:

$$V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r) = 4\left[\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\tfrac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^{6}\right], \qquad \sigma_{ij} = R_i + R_j .$$

The only per-element-pair parameter is the radius sum: no per-pair well depth, no combining rule, no atom typing. $\sigma_{ij}$ here is the *zero crossing*, so $V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(\sigma_{ij}) = 0$ and the minimum sits at $2^{1/6}\sigma_{ij}$, outside the radii sum.

`vdw_analysis.py::exponential_edgewise_lj_energy` replaces the $r^{-12}$ branch below $\sigma_{ij}$ with an exponential matched in value and slope at $\sigma_{ij}$. With $k = k_{\text{factor}}/\sigma_{ij}$, $F_0 = -24/\sigma_{ij}$ the LJ slope there, $A = -F_0/k = 24/k_{\text{factor}}$ and $B = -A$,

$$V_{\mathrm{ELJ}}(r) = \begin{cases} A\,e^{-k(r-\sigma_{ij})} - A, & r < \sigma_{ij}\\ V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r), & r \ge \sigma_{ij}\end{cases}$$

which is $C^1$ at the join and finite at contact: $V_{\mathrm{ELJ}}(0) = A\,(e^{k_{\text{factor}}} - 1)$, independent of the radii. `k_factor` is a pure stiffness, moving neither the minimum nor the attractive tail; `crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_eLJ_energy` exposes it as `repulsion`, with `stiffness = repulsion * 2.5`. The branch above $\sigma_{ij}$ is the 12-6 form unchanged, truncated at the cutoff with no shift and no tail correction, so the sum depends on the cutoff and steps by the pair energy at the cutoff whenever a pair enters or leaves the list. An optional envelope removes the step: `compute_LJ_energy`, `compute_qLJ_energy` and `compute_eLJ_energy` take `lj_envelope`, a width $w$ in Angstrom forwarded from `analyze`/`compute`, and multiply each pair energy by `vdw_analysis.py::lj_cutoff_envelope`, the quintic smoothstep from 1 at $r_c - w$ to 0 at $r_c$, which is $C^2$ at both ends. $r_c$ is the radius the pair list was built to, which `::get_intermolecular_dists_dict` records as the distance dict's `cutoff`; a width outside $(0, r_c]$ or a dict without that key raises. The default is `None`, which leaves the energy unswitched and bit-identical to the unenveloped sum (`tests/test_lj_envelope.py` in mxtaltools).

**Units.** An ELJ sum has $\varepsilon = 1$ and is dimensionless; it carries no energy scale. The conversion to kJ/mol is a single multiplicative constant. `compute_eLJ_energy` multiplies its result by the per-graph attribute `lj_coeff` (`::MolCrystalAnalysis._lj_coeff_for`, which defaults to 1.0 when absent and raises on a tensor that is neither scalar nor one value per graph). On a GFN run that attribute is stamped by `MolecularCrystal.stamp_lj_coeff` and checked by `::assert_lj_coeff_stamped`; its value is `cfg:energy_config.lj_coeff` unless the prior file carries a `thermal_scaling_factor`, which `train.py::Modeller.init_prior_dataset` writes over the run's coefficient. `calibrate_qm9_energy.py` produces that factor as the ratio of the bottom-quantile means of UMA and ELJ energies over the same structures, the quantile being `--quantile`, default 0.1, and the ELJ leg divided by `z_prime` first to match what training compares. Scaling the energy scales the effective temperature with it: with the factor applied `cfg:energy_config.temperature` is in kJ/mol, without it the same number is in raw ELJ units. A separate `cfg:energy_config.lj_rescale` multiplies again, on the `lj`/`qlj`/`elj` routes only, inside `::generator_energy`; the key is absent from `configs/mk_dev.yaml` and the code default is `None`, which skips the multiply.

## The typed exp-6 field

`mxtaltools/analysis/exp6_ff.py` types atoms and evaluates a pair potential built from published Buckingham exp-6 parameters, in kJ/mol and ångström. `::PARAMETER_SETS` holds two sets as tabulated in Appendix D of the DMACRYS manual: `w99` (Williams 2001), whose carbon is typed by its number of bonded atoms, hydrogen by what it is bonded to (carbon, alcohol oxygen, carboxylic-acid oxygen, nitrogen), nitrogen as one-connected or by its bonded-hydrogen count and oxygen by its number of bonded atoms, with no dispersion on the polar hydrogens; and `fit`, one type each for C, N and O and hydrogen split by what it is bonded to. The per-type $(A_i, B_i, C_i)$ combine as $A_{ij} = \sqrt{A_iA_j}$, $B_{ij} = \tfrac12(B_i+B_j)$, $C_{ij} = \sqrt{C_iC_j}$ into

$$E_{ij}(r) = A_{ij}\,e^{-B_{ij}r} - \frac{C_{ij}}{r^{6}}.$$

Both sets also carry F and Cl from the FIT table and the S parameters the manual lists for use with FIT on C=S and thioether sulfur, none of which is part of W99; a hydrogen on S takes the hydrogen-on-C type, and any other element raises. `::pair_tables` builds and caches the type-pair tables.

`form`, an argument of `::crystal_exp6_energy` and `::cluster_exp6_energy`, selects the shape each pair is evaluated in. The default, `'elj'`, is the ELJ shape above with $\sigma_{ij}$ and $\varepsilon_{ij}$ matched to the pair's exp-6 minimum, $\sigma_{ij} = r_{\min}/2^{1/6}$ and $\varepsilon_{ij} = -E_{ij}(r_{\min})$: `::elj_pair_energy` is the 12-6 above $\sigma_{ij}$ and the exponential wall below it with stiffness `k_factor`, default 2.5, the value `compute_eLJ_energy` uses. A pair with $C_{ij} = 0$ has no minimum and stays $A_{ij}e^{-B_{ij}r}$. `'exp6'` evaluates $E_{ij}$ itself, which turns over at short range and falls to $-\infty$; `::exp6_pair_energy` continues it linearly below the inner inflection point of its repulsive branch, where its slope is steepest, so the pair energy is $C^1$, decreasing and finite down to $r = 0$.

Types come from `::type_atoms`, graph rules on the bond graph that `::perceive_bonds` builds from distances: two atoms of one molecule are bonded when their distance is below the sum of their covalent radii (`::COVALENT_RADII`) plus `::BOND_TOL`. Typing runs on the asymmetric unit. `::with_ff_columns` returns a copy of the batch whose `x` has three columns, partial charge, type index, and for each hydrogen the index offset to its bonded atom, and `_instantiate_cluster` copies `x` from each asymmetric-unit atom to all of its images as it copies `z`, so every image atom carries its type and its hydrogen partner; column 0 stays where `::get_intermolecular_dists_dict` reads charges. With `h_shift` positive every hydrogen interaction site, for the pair energy and Coulomb alike, sits that far along the bond towards its bonded atom; the set's own value is the default, 0.1 for `w99` and 0 for `fit`.

An optional `envelope` multiplies each of those pair energies, and not the Coulomb term below, by `lj_cutoff_envelope`.

`::shifted_force_coulomb` is $1389.35\,q_iq_j\left(1/r - 1/r_c + (r - r_c)/r_c^2\right)$, zero in value and slope at the cutoff $r_c$, with $r$ clamped below at `::COULOMB_R_MIN`. The charges are a required argument of `::crystal_exp6_energy`: `'mmff'` or `'gasteiger'` computes RDKit charges for each molecule in its own atom order (`::rdkit_charges`, `::batch_charges`), with bond orders and formal charges taken from the crystal's `smiles` (one per molecule, joined by `|`, when $Z' > 1$) and mapped onto the geometry, or perceived from the geometry when the crystal has no SMILES or its number of `|`-separated parts differs from $Z'$; the result is cached by bond graph, and a charge set that does not sum to the molecule's net formal charge raises. `'x'` takes the batch's stored `x`, a tensor is used as given, and `None` drops the term.

`::dispersion_tail` adds the dispersion the pair sum leaves out for a homogeneous crystal,

$$E_{\text{tail}} = -\frac{2\pi m}{V Z'}\,K\sum_{i,k} C^{(6)}_{ik},$$

over the asymmetric-unit atoms $i$ and $k$, with $m$ the symmetry multiplicity `sym_mult`, $V = |\det T_{fc}|$, $C^{(6)}$ the form's $r^{-6}$ coefficient ($C_{ij}$ for `'exp6'`, $4\varepsilon_{ij}\sigma_{ij}^6$ for `'elj'`), and $K = 1/(3r_c^3)$ plus, with an envelope, the part of the last shell it switches off. The result is a lattice energy per molecule: the pair sum over `edge_index_inter` divided by $2Z'$, plus the tail. The ELJ sum above is the same pair sum without the $1/(2Z')$. `analyze` reaches the module through the compute key `exp6`, which `COMPUTES_REQUIRE_CLUSTER` marks as needing a cluster. With `exp6` among the requested keys, `analyze` builds its one cluster from `mxtaltools/analysis/exp6_ff.py::with_ff_columns` applied to the batch with its `exp6_params` and `exp6_charges` keywords, so `x` carries the three columns for every compute of that call, and `mxtaltools/dataset_utils/data_class_methods/crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_exp6_energy` returns `cluster_exp6_energy` on that cluster and the pair list `construct_radial_graph` built for it, at `analyze`'s cutoff and its neighbour cap of 1000. The parameter set and charge model default to `::EXP6_DEFAULT_PARAMS` (`'fit'`) and `::EXP6_DEFAULT_CHARGES` (`'mmff'`); charges of `None` drop the Coulomb term, as in the module. The pair form is fixed at `'exp6'`: the `'elj'` form's wall below $\sigma_{ij}$ is finite at contact, so an attractive pair of point charges, clamped only at `COULOMB_R_MIN`, can pull through it, and `compute_exp6_energy` raises `ValueError` when `exp6_form` or `exp6_k_factor` is passed. It raises `RuntimeError` when `x` lacks the three columns. The relaxation target of the same name is on [crystal-search-method](crystal-search-method.md). `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.generator_energy` has no branch for `exp6`, so the trainer cannot select it.

`vdw_analysis.py::buckingham_energy` evaluates $A\,e^{-B\tilde r} - C/\tilde r^{6}$ on the *radius-normalised* distance $\tilde r = r/(R_i+R_j)$ rather than on $r$ in ångström, with three scalar defaults shared by every pair ($A = -3.2619$, $B = 1.0$, $C = -0.2$, the docstring calling them a rough fit to the 12-6 form), no splice, and `nan_to_num` applied to its output. `::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_buckingham_energy` calls it with no parameter overrides, so the `bh` compute key always evaluates those three numbers. `vdw_analysis.py::electrostatic_analysis`, exposed as `es`, is a Yukawa screen $q_iq_j e^{-\kappa r}/r$ over the charges in `x[:,0]`. Its $\kappa$ is the argument named `cutoff`, default 0.92, a screening rate and not a truncation radius; the term is neither zero in value nor zero in slope where the edge list ends. `vdw_analysis.py::old_new_hydrogen_bond_analysis` counts N-H and O-H donor contacts and returns no energy. `docs/design/crystal_forcefield_investigation.md` describes an exp-6 field on a CCDC parameter table, with 23 SMARTS-defined atom types and hydrogen-bond pair terms of their own, which neither repository contains.

## The MLIPs, and what lattice energy means

The `uma` and `mace` routes replace the pair sum with a learned potential on the periodic unit cell, and take no cluster and no edge list from this code: `COMPUTES_REQUIRE_CLUSTER` marks them `False` and `COMPUTES_REQUIRE_UNIT_CELL` `True`, so `::MolCrystalAnalysis.analyze` builds the home cell only and leaves the neighbour list to the model. They are not pair-decomposable, take no cutoff and no per-element parameters from this code, and carry per-element reference energies of their own.

Lattice energy is defined identically on both MLIP routes, in `::compute_lattice_uma` and `::compute_lattice_mace`: the crystal's total potential divided by the number of molecules in the cell, `sym_mult * z_prime`, minus a gas-phase reference, times 96.485 to convert eV to kJ/mol. The reference, `::compute_lattice_gas_phase_uma` and its MACE twin, is the same model on the isolated asymmetric unit in a diffuse P1 cell (`reset_sg_info(sg_ind=1)`), split per conformer and averaged when $Z' > 1$. Both legs carry the same per-element offsets, which cancel in the difference; a mismatch between the cells the two legs are built in does not cancel. The crystal leg defaults to `std_orientation=True` and both gas legs to `False`. `MolecularCrystal.analyze_crystal_batch` passes `std_orientation=False` to `analyze`, which hands it to `mol2ucell` when it builds the home cell and does not forward it to the compute functions. The pair-sum family has no gas-phase leg: an isolated molecule has no intermolecular edges, so its sum is identically zero and the intermolecular sum *is* the lattice energy, up to the calibration factor above. `MolecularCrystal.generator_energy` divides the non-MLIP routes by `z_prime` and leaves the MLIP routes alone, that division having happened already.
