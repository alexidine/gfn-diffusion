# Crystal force fields

*Drift: **T** (theory). Verified against commit `a637e70`, 2026-09-20. Sources at the end.*

A molecular crystal's reward is built from an *intermolecular* energy: the energy of one molecule in the periodic field of its neighbours, with the molecule's internal energy held out; the conformer is rigid on this route. The model supplying that number is selected by one string, `cfg:energy_function`, read into `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal`: an explicit pair sum over a finite cluster (`elj`, its siblings `lj`, `qlj`, `silu`, `bh`, and the electrostatic `es`), or a machine-learned interatomic potential (`uma`, `mace`). What follows is the functional forms, the parameters they carry, the cluster they are evaluated on, and what "lattice energy" means per backend. How an MLIP call is routed, chunked, timed and recovered from is [mlip-energy-routes](mlip-energy-routes.md). How the per-molecule energy becomes a reward, with the density, bounding, pressure and cell-reduction terms it is summed with, is [reward-construction](reward-construction.md); the reduction term's geometry is [cell-fundamental-domains](cell-fundamental-domains.md).

## The cluster every model scores

Every pair-sum route reads a finite cluster of explicit atoms, built by `crystal_building.py::MolCrystalBuilding.mol2cluster`: the asymmetric unit is posed, the unit cell is filled by the space group's stored operators, and `crystal_building/utils.py::ucell2cluster` tiles that cell over integer translations.

Which translations are kept is a bounding-sphere test. `crystal_building/utils.py::get_cart_translations` forms the cell vectors, then takes

$$D = \max_{\mathbf{v}\in\{100,010,001,110,101,011,111\}} \left| \mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{A} \right|,$$

the largest norm over the seven non-origin corners of the cell parallelepiped, as the cell's reach. A cluster's reach is $2r_{\text{mol}} + \text{cutoff} + 0.1 + D$, with $r_{\text{mol}}$ the molecule's bounding radius (`radius`), and every translated cell whose centroid lies inside a sphere of that radius about the canonical conformer's centroid is instantiated; the zero translation is always kept, and `supercell_size` caps the per-axis count. The cutoff entering both this test and the paring below is `cutoff + zp_buffer`, the buffer being the intra-asymmetric-unit centroid spread added by `mol2cluster` when $Z' > 1$ and zero otherwise. `crystal_building/utils.py::_pare_cluster_molwise` then drops whole molecules whose centroid is further than $\text{cutoff} + 2r_{\text{mol}} + 0.1$ from the canonical conformer's, so the cluster is molecule-complete rather than cut mid-molecule.
`crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.construct_radial_graph` then calls `vdw_analysis.py::get_intermolecular_dists_dict`, which builds two edge lists through `radial_graph.py::build_radial_graph`: `edge_index`, with both ends in the asymmetric unit (`aux_ind == 0`), and `edge_index_inter`, sources inside and targets in the convolutional window (`aux_ind == 1`). Where $Z' > 1$, edges inside the asymmetric unit that cross `mol_ind` are moved from the first list to the second, so "intermolecular" means *between molecules*, not *between asymmetric units*. Every pair potential below is a sum over `edge_index_inter` alone, scattered by `intermolecular_dist_batch`, the batch index of the *source* atom, so the sum is a per-asymmetric-unit quantity. Both `cutoff` and `max_num_neighbors` are caps, and a neighbour count saturating at either truncates the list. `MolecularCrystal.analyze_crystal_batch` passes `cutoff=10`, `supercell_size=10`, `std_orientation=False`, with `cfg:energy_config.analyze_kwargs` overriding on top.

## ELJ

The base form, `vdw_analysis.py::compute_lj_edgewise`, is a 12-6 Lennard-Jones with $\varepsilon$ hard-coded to 1 and $\sigma$ taken as the *sum of the two atoms' van der Waals radii* from `constants/atom_properties.py::VDW_RADII`, an element-indexed table:

$$V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r) = 4\left[\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\tfrac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^{6}\right], \qquad \sigma_{ij} = R_i + R_j .$$

The only per-element-pair parameter is the radius sum: no per-pair well depth, no combining rule, no atom typing. $\sigma_{ij}$ here is the *zero crossing*, so $V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(\sigma_{ij}) = 0$ and the minimum sits at $2^{1/6}\sigma_{ij}$, outside the radii sum.

`vdw_analysis.py::exponential_edgewise_lj_energy` replaces the $r^{-12}$ branch below $\sigma_{ij}$ with an exponential matched in value and slope at $\sigma_{ij}$. With $k = k_{\text{factor}}/\sigma_{ij}$, $F_0 = -24/\sigma_{ij}$ the LJ slope there, $A = -F_0/k = 24/k_{\text{factor}}$ and $B = -A$,

$$V_{\mathrm{ELJ}}(r) = \begin{cases} A\,e^{-k(r-\sigma_{ij})} - A, & r < \sigma_{ij}\\ V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r), & r \ge \sigma_{ij}\end{cases}$$

which is $C^1$ at the join and finite at contact: $V_{\mathrm{ELJ}}(0) = A\,(e^{k_{\text{factor}}} - 1)$, independent of the radii. `k_factor` is a pure stiffness, moving neither the minimum nor the attractive tail; `crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_eLJ_energy` exposes it as `repulsion`, with `stiffness = repulsion * 2.5`. The attractive branch is the bare $-4(\sigma/r)^6$, truncated at the cutoff with no shift and no tail correction, so the sum depends on the cutoff.

**Units.** An ELJ sum has $\varepsilon = 1$ and is dimensionless; it carries no energy scale. The conversion to kJ/mol is a single multiplicative constant. `compute_eLJ_energy` multiplies its result by the per-graph attribute `lj_coeff` (`::MolCrystalAnalysis._lj_coeff_for`, which defaults to 1.0 when absent and raises on a tensor that is neither scalar nor one value per graph). On a GFN run that attribute is stamped by `MolecularCrystal.stamp_lj_coeff` and checked by `::assert_lj_coeff_stamped`; its value is `cfg:energy_config.lj_coeff` unless the prior file carries a `thermal_scaling_factor`, which `train.py::Modeller.init_prior_dataset` writes over the run's coefficient. `calibrate_qm9_energy.py` produces that factor as the ratio of the bottom-quantile means of UMA and ELJ energies over the same structures, the quantile being `--quantile`, default 0.1, and the ELJ leg divided by `z_prime` first to match what training compares. Scaling the energy scales the effective temperature with it: with the factor applied `cfg:energy_config.temperature` is in kJ/mol, without it the same number is in raw ELJ units. A separate `cfg:energy_config.lj_rescale` multiplies again, on the `lj`/`qlj`/`elj` routes only, inside `::generator_energy`; the key is absent from `configs/mk_dev.yaml` and the code default is `None`, which skips the multiply.

## The typed exp-6 field

`docs/design/crystal_forcefield_investigation.md` describes a field of a different shape: a Buckingham exp-6 pair potential $A\,e^{-Br} - C/r^6$ in kJ/mol with per-type-pair $(A,B,C)$ from a CCDC parameter table, spliced linearly below the inner maximum to remove the Buckingham catastrophe; 23 SMARTS-defined atom types assigned by graph logic on the asymmetric unit only and propagated to periodic images through the feature column; a subset of type pairs designated hydrogen-bond pairs with their own parameters; and a shifted-force Coulomb term $q_iq_j(1/r - 1/r_c + (r-r_c)/r_c^2)$ over stored Gasteiger charges. The note states at its head that nothing landed in either repository, and no typing module exists in either repository today.

What is in the code is the same functional family and none of the typing. `vdw_analysis.py::buckingham_energy` evaluates $A\,e^{-B\tilde r} - C/\tilde r^{6}$ on the *radius-normalised* distance $\tilde r = r/(R_i+R_j)$ rather than on $r$ in ångström, with three scalar defaults shared by every pair ($A = -3.2619$, $B = 1.0$, $C = -0.2$, the docstring calling them a rough fit to the 12-6 form), no splice, and `nan_to_num` in place of a catastrophe cure. `::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_buckingham_energy` calls it with no parameter overrides, so the `bh` route the GFN sees is always those three numbers. The electrostatic term that exists, `vdw_analysis.py::electrostatic_analysis`, is not the note's shifted-force Coulomb but a Yukawa screen $q_iq_j e^{-\kappa r}/r$ over the same Gasteiger charges in `x[:,0]`, exposed as `es`. Its $\kappa$ is the argument named `cutoff`, default 0.92, a screening rate and not a truncation radius; the term is neither zero in value nor zero in slope where the edge list ends. Hydrogen bonds appear only in `vdw_analysis.py::old_new_hydrogen_bond_analysis`, which counts N-H and O-H donor contacts and returns no energy. The two differ in the distance variable, the parameter table, the core repair, the electrostatic form, and the existence of typing and of hydrogen-bond pair terms.

## The MLIPs, and what lattice energy means

The `uma` and `mace` routes replace the pair sum with a learned potential on the periodic unit cell, and take no cluster and no edge list from this code: `COMPUTES_REQUIRE_CLUSTER` marks them `False` and `COMPUTES_REQUIRE_UNIT_CELL` `True`, so `::MolCrystalAnalysis.analyze` builds the home cell only and leaves the neighbour list to the model. They are not pair-decomposable, take no cutoff and no per-element parameters from this code, and carry per-element reference energies of their own.

Lattice energy is defined identically on both MLIP routes, in `::compute_lattice_uma` and `::compute_lattice_mace`: the crystal's total potential divided by the number of molecules in the cell, `sym_mult * z_prime`, minus a gas-phase reference, times 96.485 to convert eV to kJ/mol. The reference, `::compute_lattice_gas_phase_uma` and its MACE twin, is the same model on the isolated asymmetric unit in a diffuse P1 cell (`reset_sg_info(sg_ind=1)`), split per conformer and averaged when $Z' > 1$. Both legs carry the same per-element offsets, which cancel in the difference; a mismatch between the cells the two legs are built in does not cancel. The crystal leg defaults to `std_orientation=True` and both gas legs to `False`; the GFN's `analyze_kwargs` passes `False`. The pair-sum family has no gas-phase leg: an isolated molecule has no intermolecular edges, so its sum is identically zero and the intermolecular sum *is* the lattice energy, up to the calibration factor above. `MolecularCrystal.generator_energy` divides the non-MLIP routes by `z_prime` and leaves the MLIP routes alone, that division having happened already.
