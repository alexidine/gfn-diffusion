# Flat directions and limit cycles

*Drift: **T** (theory). Verified against commit `a637e70`, 2026-09-20. Sources at the end.*

The trajectory-balance residual is a scalar built from three groups of parameters: the forward policy $P_F$, the backward policy $P_B$, and the learned scalar $\log Z$. A *flat direction* is a curve through that joint space along which the residual is unchanged, exactly or to leading order in the step size, so the objective supplies no restoring force along it while the sampler the parameters describe moves. This page derives the two flat directions the parameterisation has, states what the residual sees along each, and describes how a flat direction plus a lagged force from elsewhere becomes an oscillation. The objective itself is [trajectory-balance](trajectory-balance.md); the level-free alternative that drops $\log Z$ from the policy loss is [vargrad](vargrad.md).

The residual per trajectory is `gflownet_losses.py::get_tb_loss`'s `tb = log_pf + log_Z_per_traj - log_pb - log_r`, that is $\delta(\tau) = \log Z + \sum_t [\log P_F(x_{t+1}|x_t) - \log P_B(x_t|x_{t+1})] - \log R(x_T)$. `docs/design/width_and_length_scaling.md` records that $\delta$ is *intensive*: $T$ discretises a fixed $[0,1]$ horizon, so it converges to a Girsanov limit rather than accumulating with $T$.

## The in-trajectory variance direction

Both kernels are Gaussian. The forward step is drawn in `models/gfn.py::GFN.fwd_propagate` as $x_{t+1} = x_t + \Delta t\,\mu_t + \sqrt{\Delta t}\,\sigma_t\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim N(0,I)$, and scored in `::GFN.fwd_gauss_logprob`, which reduces to `::GFN.gauss_logprob` on the diagonal branch. The backward step is the bridge of the same process: mean $(1 - c_t\kappa_t)\,x_{t+1}$ with contraction $c_t$ from `::GFN.var_drift_coeff`, variance `::GFN.var_bridge_step` times the exponentiated sum of the backward head's additive log-variance correction and `::GFN.var_log_rate` (`::GFN._eval_pb_logprob`, `::GFN._pb_logprob`). Write $\kappa_f$ for the forward head's learned log-variance deviation and $\kappa_v$ for the backward head's additive correction; for a step spanning accumulated variance $\Delta V = V(t_{\text{next}}) - V(t_{\text{prev}})$ the code's two variances are

$$s_t^2 \;=\; \Delta t\,\sigma_t^2 \;=\; e^{\kappa_f}\,\Delta V, \qquad \beta_t \;=\; e^{\kappa_v}\,\Delta V\,\frac{V(t_{\text{prev}})}{V(t_{\text{next}})}.$$

On the diagonal branch both densities carry the same $n\log 2\pi$ over the same live dimension count (`::GFN._live_only` restricts each), so that term cancels and the per-step contribution is

$$\ell_t \;=\; \tfrac12\Big[\tfrac{\|z_t\|^2}{\beta_t} - \|\varepsilon_t\|^2\Big] \;+\; \tfrac{n}{2}\log\frac{\beta_t}{s_t^2}, \qquad \frac{\beta_t}{s_t^2} = e^{\kappa_v - \kappa_f}\,\frac{V(t_{\text{prev}})}{V(t_{\text{next}})},$$

with $z_t = c_t\kappa_t x_{t+1} - \Delta x_t$ the backward residual at the realised increment.

Three statements follow. The log-determinant term depends on the two heads **only through the difference** $\kappa_v - \kappa_f$, so raising both by the same amount leaves it exactly unchanged. The forward quadratic $\|\varepsilon_t\|^2$ is pivotal: a step's standardised residual under its own kernel is $\chi^2_n$ whatever $\sigma_t$ is. Only the backward quadratic retains a dependence. With $\Delta x_t = a_t + s_t\varepsilon_t$, $a_t = \Delta t\,\mu_t$, $b_t = c_t\kappa_t x_{t+1} - a_t$ and $\rho_t = \beta_t/s_t^2$,

$$\frac{\|z_t\|^2}{\beta_t} \;=\; \frac{\|\varepsilon_t\|^2}{\rho_t} \;-\; \frac{2\langle b_t,\varepsilon_t\rangle}{s_t\rho_t} \;+\; \frac{\|b_t\|^2}{s_t^2\rho_t},$$

so the dependence on the *common* level of $\kappa_f$ and $\kappa_v$ enters only through $b_t$, the drift-plus-contraction displacement, relative to the noise scale. With $a_t = O(\Delta t)$ and $c_t = O(\Delta t)$ against $s_t = O(\sqrt{\Delta t})$, the cross term is $O(\sqrt{\Delta t})$ and mean zero and the last is $O(\Delta t)$ per step. The direction is exactly flat in the normalisers and in the forward quadratic, and non-flat only through the ratio of drift displacement to injected noise, which falls as the common level rises.

The terminal responds at a different order. Extra per-step noise of amplitude $\eta$ is a mean-zero increment, so the terminal mean is unchanged at first order in $\eta$ and the terminal law responds only through the second moment, damped further by the Jacobian of the remaining drift; the realised mean squared increment responds at first order, being that second moment directly. The residual sees the log-variance difference between the two heads and the drift-to-noise ratio; it does not see their common level, and the terminal distribution registers a change in that level at second order.

How far the direction runs is bounded by the parameterisation rather than by the loss: `::GFN.split_params` passes the forward head through $\tanh$ at `cfg:model.log_var_range` unless that key is $-1$, then adds the rate baseline and clips to $\pm$ `var_clip` (16 in `models/gfn.py`); the backward correction is bounded by $\tanh$ at `cfg:model.pb_var_range` and the same clip is applied to its sum with the rate baseline. Both learned deviations are identically zero under `cfg:model.learned_variance` false, and the backward corrections under `cfg:model.learn_pb` false. The first forward step scores $\log P_B$ as a constant zero (`::GFN._eval_pb_logprob`, `is_first`). Two keys separate the sampled variance from the scored one: `::GFN.fwd_get_logvars` adds twice `cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.exploration_std`, a per-step additive log-std, to the sampled log-variance while `::GFN._fwd_step` scores $\log P_F$ un-inflated, and `cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.path_grad_scale` at 0 detaches the sampled scale from the path gradient. `configs/mk_dev.yaml` ships `exploration_std` at 0.0 and `path_grad_scale` at 1.

## The level direction

The residual is linear in $\log Z$ and in $\log P_F$ with opposite signs, so adding a constant $a$ to $\log Z$ and subtracting $a$ from every $\log P_F$ leaves every row's $\delta$ unchanged. This is the level direction, flat over the whole batch at once rather than per row. It is not free in the model: $P_F$ is a normalised density over trajectories, so a uniform downward shift of $\log P_F$ has to be paid for by mass elsewhere, and `utils.py::quick_tb_stats` records the bound as $\mathbb{E}_\mu[\log P_F] \le -\mathbb{H}(\mu)$; the same file publishes `relative_under`, the level-free re-centring of `under_coverage` on the batch's own Jensen mean. `docs/tb_level_decomposition.tex` decomposes the gap between the forward and backward levels as $\Delta = A + B - C$, three KL divergences of the policy and the buffer, and states that no choice of $\log Z$ zeroes both residuals unless $\Delta = 0$. Which rows are on-policy and which are re-scored decides which branch can move the level: [on-policy-and-off-policy-training](on-policy-and-off-policy-training.md), [trajectory-balance](trajectory-balance.md).

## What turns a flat direction into a limit cycle

Generically: a flat direction admits a slow drift, because the objective supplies no restoring force along it and whatever the other loss terms project onto it is applied at full step size under a magnitude-normalising optimiser. A cycle needs a second ingredient, a restoring force that is *lagged* and comes from somewhere the direction is not flat: another branch's loss, a controller reading a smoothed metric, or a quantity measured at eval rather than train cadence. The code carries telemetry for two coordinates where both ingredients are present.

**Realised per-step variance against terminal spread.** `train.py::Modeller._update_rolling` computes `terminal_var`, the cross-batch variance of the terminal latents, and `step_var`, the mean squared increment along the trajectory, both over live dimensions only. Its comment names `step_var` the train-cadence proxy for the joint $P_F$/$P_B$ inflation that runs along the flat direction. The two respond at different orders in the same perturbation, per the derivation above, so a motion along the direction appears in `step_var` with `terminal_var` comparatively still. The model's log-variance *parameters* are available only at eval cadence, the train sampler running with `return_gauss_params=False`; both realised quantities come off `flow_states`. `train.py::Modeller._per_step_probe`, armed by `cfg:per_step_probe_steps` and active on forward sub-steps only, writes raw per-step rows of both plus the pre-clip gradient norm and four calibration errors from `quick_tb_stats`, without EMA or tracker.

**Level against replay share.** The coupling joins two mechanisms described elsewhere. `train.py::Modeller.z_level_fill` moves $\log Z$ toward the batch's own Huber fixed point from `gflownet_losses.py::winsorized_z_root` ([z-calibration](z-calibration.md)); under `cfg:z_calibration.fill_mode` `snap` it overwrites $\log Z$ with that root behind the `fill_threshold` and `fill_se` gates, and under `absorb`, which `configs/mk_dev.yaml` ships, it applies a one-dimensional Kalman update whose gain is the measurement's precision share. $\log Z$ is the numeraire, so a fill of size $dz$ re-signs every residual at once. The `gated_ramp` controller's ratchet tracks the stage minimum of `cfg:stage.balance.ratchet_metric`, `bwd/under_coverage` in the canonical config, and `utils.py::quick_tb_stats` builds `under_coverage` as a reward-weighted root mean square of the *negative* part of that same residual. A fill therefore displaces the quantity the ratchet's metric is built from by $dz$ in one step. It shifts the tracker's two level EMAs, exactly for the unclipped one and to first order for the winsorized one, but its docstring records that `metric_tracker`'s `fwd/tb_resid_clipped` cannot be shifted from there and keeps reporting the pre-fill level for its own time constant. The ratchet trip is a Schmitt trigger on a running minimum ([balance-controllers](balance-controllers.md)), so a level recorded at one $\log Z$ is compared against levels read at another, and a veto or release changes the replay share, hence the composition of the batch whose root the next fill reads.

## The variance schedule in v-time

`models/gfn.py::GFN.init_var_schedule` installs an optional in-rollout schedule for the forward noise *rate*, $\sigma^2(t) = \sigma^2(0)\,\text{ratio}^{\,t^{\,p}}$ with `cfg:model.t_scale_ratio` and `cfg:model.t_scale_power`, and precomputes $V(t) = \int_0^t \sigma^2(s)\,ds$ by cumulative trapezoid over `VAR_SCHEDULE_GRID` (8192) intervals, read back by `::GFN.var_accum` and `::GFN.accum_var`. Everything downstream runs on $V$ rather than on $t$: at a constant rate $V(t) = $ `t_scale` $\cdot\,t$, so the two are proportional, and the docstring records that every place the earlier code wrote a time ratio it meant a variance ratio. Its three consumers are `::GFN.var_log_rate` (the $\log(\Delta V/\Delta t)$ baseline on a policy log-variance), `::GFN.var_drift_coeff` ($c_t = \Delta V/V(t_{\text{next}})$) and `::GFN.var_bridge_step` ($\Delta t\,V(t_{\text{prev}})/V(t_{\text{next}})$), each short-circuiting to the constant-rate expression when the schedule is off.

Written this way, $\beta_t/s_t^2 = e^{\kappa_v - \kappa_f}\,V(t_{\text{prev}})/V(t_{\text{next}})$ is a function of $V$-ratios and the two learned deviations alone, so the pairing that makes the joint direction flat holds under any schedule. `cfg:model.t_scale_preserve_budget` rescales $\sigma^2(0)$ so that $V(1) = $ `t_scale` exactly; the docstring records that the trajectory starts from a deterministic $x_0 = 0$ under a deterministic drift, so $V(1)$ is the entire stock of randomness available to build the terminal distribution. A schedule at preserved budget redistributes step variance along the rollout at fixed terminal budget. It moves $P_B$ with it by construction, and the docstring records that $P_B$'s drift coefficient can be rescaled only by $\pm$ `cfg:model.pb_drift_range`, 0.4 in the canonical config, which it states is not enough to absorb the difference over the last third of the rollout. `t_scale_ratio` is absent from `configs/mk_dev.yaml`, so the code default `None` applies and the schedule is off; it adds no parameters and registers `_var_accum` non-persistently, and `checkpointing.py::Checkpointer.RECONFIGURABLE_GFN_KEYS` lets a resumed run take all three keys from its own config.

## Where the notes and the code disagree

`docs/design/z_fill_process_variance.md` derives the absorber's process variance $q$ from consecutive fills as $(\mathrm{var}(\Delta\text{root}) - 2\,\overline{se^2})/N$, and records measured values above the shipped constant; `configs/mk_dev.yaml` ships `cfg:z_calibration.fill_process_var` at 0.01. The note states that the estimate rests on an assumed random-walk model for the root's motion, and that under drift instead $q$ is not a noise parameter. $q$ enters the Kalman gain, so it sets what fraction of each measurement's gap a fill applies: the gain of the level mechanism above.
