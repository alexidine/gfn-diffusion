# On-policy and off-policy training

*Drift: **T** (theory). Verified against commit `a637e70`, 2026-09-20. Sources at the end.*

A training step in this codebase runs up to three branches, and they do not see the same data. The forward branch rolls out the current policy and scores the terminals it reaches; the backward and replay branches take terminals that already exist, in the prior buffer and the replay buffer, and build trajectories to them. Both kinds of row carry the same log-weight, but the distribution it is averaged under differs, and the two averages sit at different levels. This page is about the coupling between those two streams: what holds them together under trajectory balance, what replaces it under VarGrad, and what happens to stored rows when nothing does. Left out and named: the number of steps between rollouts and the pinning of the level at one ([rollout-cadence-and-dose](rollout-cadence-and-dose.md), [z-calibration](z-calibration.md)); the lambda path ([lambda-annealing](lambda-annealing.md)); the replay buffer's own admission, eviction and draw ([replay-buffer](replay-buffer.md)).

## The two streams

`train.py::Modeller.fused_train_step` fires the branches together and fuses their per-row losses into one weighted sum. The forward branch (`::fwd_train_step`, loss in `gflownet_losses.py::get_gfn_forward_loss`) samples trajectories from $P_F$ and calls the energy function on the terminals it lands on. The backward branch (`::bwd_train_step`) draws stored terminals from the prior buffer or the prior dataset (`::draw_bwd_sample`) and samples a trajectory to each under $P_B$ (`models/gfn.py::GFN.get_traj_bwd`). The replay branch (`::replay_train_step`) draws stored rows whose whole trajectory was kept and re-scores that fixed path under the current parameters (`models/gfn.py::GFN.get_traj_replay`). Both off-policy branches go through `gflownet_losses.py::get_gfn_backward_loss`, which samples a backward trajectory when the caller passes none and replays the stored one when it does.

Every row on every branch carries the same quantity,

$$u(\tau) \;=\; \log R(x) \;+\; \log P_B(\tau \mid x) \;-\; \log P_F(\tau),$$

built from `log_r`, `log_pb` and `log_pf`. What differs is the sampling law. Write $\mu$ for the distribution over terminal states the buffer holds, and define the two levels

$$J_F := \mathbb{E}_{P_F}[u], \qquad J_B := \mathbb{E}_{\mu P_B}[u],$$

where $\mu P_B$ means draw $x \sim \mu$, then $\tau \sim P_B(\cdot \mid x)$. With $\pi = R/Z^\star$, $A = \mathrm{KL}(P_F \| \pi P_B)$, $B = \mathrm{KL}(\mu P_B \| P_F)$ and $C = \mathrm{KL}(\mu \| \pi)$, the two levels are $J_F = \log Z^\star - A$ and $J_B = \log Z^\star - C + B$, so

$$\Delta \;:=\; J_B - J_F \;=\; A + B - C .$$

$J_F$ is a lower bound on $\log Z^\star$; $J_B$ is not, and exceeds it whenever $B > C$. $A$ has the model in the first KL argument and is estimated from forward data; $B$ has it in the second and is estimated from buffer data. $\Delta$ is published as `zmatch/delta_mean` (`train.py`). The spread of $u$ about a level, rather than the level itself, is [trajectory-balance](trajectory-balance.md) and [vargrad](vargrad.md).

## In TB the branches share one scalar

The trajectory-balance residual is $r(\tau) = \log Z + \log P_F(\tau) - \log P_B(\tau\mid x) - \log R(x) = \log Z - u(\tau)$, built in `gflownet_losses.py::get_tb_loss` as `log_pf + log_Z_per_traj - log_pb - log_r` and passed through a Huber knee. Every branch that runs a TB term uses the same learned scalar, or on the conditional route the same learned function $\log Z(c)$, so the two streams' residuals are centred on one number:

$$\mathbb{E}_{P_F}[r] = \log Z - J_F, \qquad \mathbb{E}_{\mu P_B}[r] = \log Z - J_B = (\log Z - J_F) - \Delta .$$

Placing $\log Z$ at $J_F$ zeroes the forward mean residual and leaves the backward one at $-\Delta$; placing it at $J_B$ does the reverse. No placement zeroes both unless $\Delta = 0$, and $\Delta$ is a function of $(P_F, P_B, \mu, \pi)$ alone.

**When one branch trains the scalar.** `freeze_z` detaches `log_Z_learned` at source in both loss functions, so a branch with it set contributes no gradient to $Z$. In `configs/mk_dev.yaml` the base carries `cfg:bwd_loss_coeffs.freeze_z` and `cfg:replay_loss_coeffs.freeze_z` at 1 and `cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.freeze_z` at 0. If one branch updates $Z$ and only $Z$, the stationary point of its own residual is $\log Z \to J_F$ on the forward stream, so that residual self-zeroes whatever the policy is doing, and the other stream's level is unconstrained. What remains is one-sided. With $\log Z$ held at $z_0$, the mean backward residual can be driven to zero by training $P_F$ only if $z_0 \ge \mathrm{ELBO}(\mu) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[\log R] + \mathbb{H}(\mu)$, since $J_B$ ranges over $[\mathrm{ELBO}(\mu), \infty)$ as $P_F$ varies. Below that floor no forward policy makes it mean-zero, and under a squared or Huber error the optimiser distorts $P_F$ in the demanded direction rather than stopping.

## In VarGrad there is no shared scalar

VarGrad replaces the learned level with the group's own mean: a group of $g$ rows has centre $\hat c = \tfrac1g \sum_j u_j$ and the loss is $\sum_i \rho(\hat c - u_i)$ (`gflownet_losses.py::condition_grouped_empirical_z`). The per-row gradient coefficients $\bar\psi - \psi(d_j)$ sum to exactly zero, so adding a constant to every $u$ in a group changes neither the loss nor its gradient, and groups are formed within a branch, by `condition_id`. A uniform shift of $u$ across one branch's support is therefore a flat direction of that branch's term, and the offset $\Delta$ is unpenalised by either. Two mechanisms act on that coordinate.

**The pooled cross-branch term.** `gflownet_losses.py::pooled_condition_vargrad` builds one per-condition group spanning both sides: it concatenates the forward (or replay) live rows with the backward live rows and calls the same grouped estimator on the union. The rows arrive through three slots on the model, `gfn._live_fwd`, `_live_bwd` and `_live_replay`, written by the two loss functions under the `gfn._stash_live_branches` gate and cleared by `fused_train_step` at both ends of the step. `cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.pooled_source` selects which of `fwd` and `replay` pairs with `bwd`; `pooled_ratio` sets the buffer share $\lambda_b$ by subsampling the over-represented side; `pooled_bridge_only` restricts the buffer side to rows whose condition the first side also holds; `pooled_thin_by_condition` ranks forward rows with a partner first when the forward side is thinned. Because the coefficients sum to zero over the *pooled* group, a nat raised on a buffer row is taken from the forward rows. By the law of total variance the pooled objective is $(1-\lambda_b)V_f + \lambda_b V_b + \lambda_b(1-\lambda_b)\Delta^2$, whose third term is the only one the branch terms do not already carry. `pooled_beta` is its own Huber knee: forward rows sit about $\lambda_b\Delta$ from the pooled centre and buffer rows about $(1-\lambda_b)\Delta$ the other way, so keeping forward shape needs $\beta \gtrsim \lambda_b\Delta$ while leaving the buffer rows saturated needs $\beta \ll (1-\lambda_b)\Delta$. A condition present on only one side forms a single-source group, where the term degenerates to that branch's own; `pooled_mixed_frac` is the fraction of rows in a two-sided group, `pooled_pairable_frac` its ceiling, `pooled_lambda_b` the share achieved. The term enters the fused loss outside the frac-weighted sum, at `cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.pooled_vg`.

**The level tether.** `cfg:bwd_loss_coeffs.level_gap` adds a backward-only term whose per-row coefficient is the level tracker's detached EMA gap, looked up per condition by `buffer.py::ConditionLogZTracker.lookup_delta` and clamped to `level_gap_clamp` nats. Under `level_gap_pf_only` (default 1) the term is `gap * (-log_pf)`; at 0 it multiplies the whole $u$. No $\log Z$ appears, so the term cannot move $Z$. It is one-sided: the matching forward half is not added. The gap is re-read each step, so the coefficient vanishes when the levels match; `level_gap_coeff_rms` is the rms applied coefficient, while the term's own value `level_gap` is $g\,\log w$ and carries either sign. It reaches $P_F$ explicitly, the rows coming from $\mu \times P_B$, and $P_B$ only through the reparameterised sampled trajectory, so `cfg:bwd_loss_coeffs.traj_grads` at 0 removes that channel.

## Buffer absorption

Absorption is the state in which the policy has met the level demand on the stored rows without meeting it on the rows it generates: the residuals on buffer and replay draws go to zero while the on-policy residual does not. Both TB and grouped VarGrad are minimised by $u$ constant on the support they are evaluated on, and the support of the off-policy branches is a fixed bag of terminals. A policy can hold $u$ flat there and be anywhere else off-policy.

**The in-trajectory flat direction.** On a stored path the states are fixed, so $u$ moves only through the two densities. Widening the per-step variance of $P_F$ by a factor $s$ shifts $\log P_F$ on each path by a term in that path's standardised quadratic form under $P_F$; widening $P_B$ by the same $s$ shifts $\log P_B$ the same way in $P_B$'s form. Both kernels see the same increments against nearly the same bridge drift, so the two forms agree path by path, and $\log P_F - \log P_B$ can be held fixed while both terms move together. $u$ is then unchanged on every stored row: the joint $(P_F, P_B)$ widening is a flat direction of any loss built from the values of $u$ on a bag of stored rows, whichever branch's gradient moves $P_B$. The force opposing it is the reward on paths the widened policy generates, which reaches the loss only after those paths are rolled out and admitted.

**Freezing the backward policy.** A fixed $P_B$ removes the compensating half, so widening $P_F$ changes $u$ on every stored row immediately. `models/gfn.py::GFN.freeze_backward_policy` takes the snapshot: a `ModuleDict` deep copy of the three modules in `GFN.PB_SNAPSHOT_MODULES` (`t_model`, `s_model`, `backward_policy`), `requires_grad` off, in eval mode. `GFN._pb_net` evaluates $P_B$ on that snapshot under `no_grad` whenever one is installed, with the live conditioner still feeding it, detached. The snapshot is not a registered submodule, so it is invisible to `named_children` and to gradient-norm reporting, is never EMA'd and is absent from the model `state_dict`; `checkpointing.py` persists it under the key `pb_frozen`, from `GFN.pb_snapshot_state`. `train.py::Modeller.set_pb_freeze` is the switch, with three values. `None`/`false` lifts any snapshot and restores `requires_grad` on the head. `'head'` turns `requires_grad` off on `backward_policy` alone, which does not fix $P_B$: the head reads the shared `s_model`/`t_model` trunk, which $P_F$ keeps training and which the backward branch's own terms keep pushing. `'full'`/`true` installs the snapshot on both the train and EMA models as one shared object, and `gradnorm/backward_policy` then reads exactly 0. A snapshot carried by a checkpoint is restored rather than retaken.

The entry points are `cfg:freeze_backward_policy`, applied from step 0 of the process in `train.py::Modeller.init_gfn`, and the stage actions `freeze_pb[:full|head]` and `unfreeze_pb` in `protocol.py`, which call `set_pb_freeze` at a stage boundary; bare `freeze_pb` is the affirmative `full`. As an `on_enter` action it runs after the optimizer rebuild, so the fresh Adam never sees a $P_B$ gradient; as an `on_exit` action it runs before the outgoing snapshot, which then carries it.

## Where the notes and the code disagree

docs/design/replay_seat_pb_freeze.md lists `replay_loss_coeffs.detach_pb` and `bwd_loss_coeffs.detach_pb` as shipped instrumentation for detaching $\log P_B$ on stored rows. No Python file in the repository ever read `detach_pb`, so any config that set it to 1 recorded an intention and ran the un-detached branch. Both keys were removed from `configs/mk_dev.yaml` on 2026-09-20 and entered in `utils.py::_RETIRED_KEYS`, which now refuses them at load; the design note is the one thing still describing them as live. $P_B$ is controlled by `cfg:freeze_backward_policy` and the `freeze_pb`/`unfreeze_pb` stage actions.
