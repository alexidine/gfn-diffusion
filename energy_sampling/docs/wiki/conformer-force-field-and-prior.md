A conformer problem has no crystal and no periodic neighbour list: the reward is built from one molecule's intramolecular energy, and the states the trainer needs before a policy exists come from a fitted prior over conformations. This page is about those two objects and the statistics that say where a set of drawn conformations sits relative to the target. Left out and named: the spanning-tree chart and the internal coordinates ([conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates](conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates.md)); the levels, stages and losses of a run ([conformer-training](conformer-training.md)); the frozen graph encoder ([molecule-encoder](molecule-encoder.md)).

Throughout, $U$ is the force field's energy in kcal/mol, $T$ the sampling temperature in the same units, and $d$ the width of the latent state $x \in [-1,1]^d$, which `ConformerTorsions` carries as `data_ndim`.

## The force field: MMFF94 in torch

`cfg:energy_config.force_field` selects which of two builders `conformer_torsions.py::ConformerTorsions._make_ff` calls. At `'mmff'` it is `mxtaltools/conformers/energy.py::ff_from_mmff`, a complete copy of MMFF94: bond stretch, angle bend, stretch-bend, out-of-plane, proper torsion, van der Waals and electrostatics, each in MMFF's own functional form and evaluated by `energy.py::intramolecular_energy` from Cartesian positions. Bonds are harmonic times MMFF's quartic factor (`ForceField.bond_cs`); angles are harmonic times the cubic factor (`angle_cb`), with rows flagged in `angle_linear` switching to the $2k(1+\cos\theta)$ form MMFF uses at a linear centre; torsions are MMFF's three-term Fourier expansion written as three rows of $V[1 + \cos(n\phi - \gamma)]$; out-of-plane runs over the Wilson angles at a three-coordinate centre, with a force constant MMFF allows to be negative and the code does not clamp; van der Waals is `energy.py::buffered_147`, exact as published at `vdw_softcore_frac` zero and continued linearly below `softcore_frac * rstar` otherwise; electrostatics reuse `pair_index` and inherit its separation rule, with a 0.75 scale on 1-4 pairs (`MMFF_ELE_14_SCALE`) and the buffering constant `ele_delta`.

Parameters come from RDKit's MMFF94 typing through `AllChem.MMFFGetMoleculeProperties` and its per-term accessors, read off connectivity and bond orders rather than geometry, so the parameters are a function of the molecular graph alone. The van der Waals lookup takes the third and fourth returns of `GetMMFFVdWParams`, the donor-acceptor rescaled $R^*$ and $\epsilon$.

The pair list is not read from RDKit. `ff_from_mmff` takes its bond, angle, stretch-bend, out-of-plane and torsion rows from RDKit's bonds, and its van der Waals and electrostatic pairs from `mxtaltools/conformers/builder.py::nonbonded_pairs` over the tree's graph, which `ConformerTorsions` perceives from the reference conformer's geometry ([conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates](conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates.md), the tree section). Where the two graphs differ the field is not MMFF94 of the molecule. `tests/conformer/test_mmff_matches_rdkit.py` builds its tree with `mxtaltools/conformers/topology.py::spec_from_mol`, which reads RDKit's bonds, so its comparison does not exercise the geometry-perceived graph.

Verification is per term rather than on the total. `tests/conformer/test_mmff_matches_rdkit.py::test_every_term_matches_rdkit` switches every RDKit term but one off and compares at a *perturbed* geometry, so a term near zero cannot pass by being small: tolerance $3\times10^{-4}$ kcal/mol for angle, stretch-bend and out-of-plane, which the file attributes to the decimal precision RDKit's accessors report, and $10^{-9}$ for the other four. `::test_total_matches_rdkit` checks the sum at $10^{-3}$, and `::test_vdw_donor_acceptor_rescaling_is_load_bearing` asserts the un-rescaled parameters fail.

The other branch of `_make_ff` is `energy.py::ff_from_reference`, which measures $r_0$ and $\theta_0$ off the embedded conformer and carries no torsion term: bonds, angles, a ring-closure restraint and soft-core 12-6 pairs (`energy.py::soft_core_lj`), with its parameters a function of the embedding. `energy.py::ff_from_graph` adds proper torsions to that set from hand-written tables keyed on `(element, degree)` covering four bond types, six angle types and two torsion centres, with `energy.py::_lookup` raising on any key outside them; no code path in this trainer calls it, and its own docstring still says it carries no torsion term. `ForceField` separates a field that does not carry a term from one carrying it as zero: every MMFF-only field is `None` on both non-MMFF builders, and `intramolecular_energy` branches on `is None` for each.

## The stereo lock

The `stereo_coeff` keyword of `ConformerTorsions` (code default 0) adds a flat-bottom term to the potential, from `energies/stereo_lock.py`. It is settable through `energy_config`, which is filtered against the constructor signature; `configs/conformer_mk.yaml` sets it to 300. Both force fields above are functions of distances and of dihedrals through terms even under reflection, so without the term the potential takes one value on a conformer, on its mirror image, and on any relabelling that exchanges identical substituents at a centre ([conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates](conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates.md), the chirality section).

`ConformerTorsions._init_stereo` builds a `stereo_lock.py::StereoTable` at every construction, whatever the coefficient. Every atom with four bonded neighbours in the tree's graph is a tetrahedral element, RDKit stereocentre or not; `StereoTable.stereocentre` marks the ones the input SMILES specifies. Every double bond `stereo_lock.py::tagged_elements` reports is a double-bond element, except a cumulated one (either end carries a second double bond, as in an allene), which it reports as a type with no indicator. That function runs RDKit's `FindPotentialStereo` with `cleanIt=True` on the heavy-atom graph and on the explicit-hydrogen graph and unites the reports: the first omits a =NH imine, the second an unspecified ring cis/trans pair. A three-coordinate atom is not an element. All perception runs inside `stereo_lock.py::pinned_perception`, which sets RDKit's legacy stereo perception for the call and restores the process setting afterwards.

For an element on atoms $p_1, \dots, p_4$ the indicator (`stereo_lock.py::values_from_points`) is

$$v = \frac{(p_1-p_4)\cdot[(p_2-p_4)\times(p_3-p_4)]}{|p_1-p_4|\,|p_2-p_4|\,|p_3-p_4|}$$

for a tetrahedral element and, for a double bond $a$-$b$=$c$-$d$ with angles $t_1$, $t_2$ at $b$ and $c$ and dihedral $\phi$,

$$q = -\frac{[(a-b)\times(c-b)]\cdot[(b-c)\times(d-c)]}{|a-b|\,|c-b|\,|b-c|\,|d-c|} = -\sin t_1 \sin t_2 \cos\phi,$$

with every length in a denominator floored at `NORM_FLOOR` (0.1 Å), so the gradient stays finite when two atoms of a quad coincide. A tetrahedral element has eight candidate quads: each set of three neighbours with the fourth neighbour as $p_4$, and the same sets with the centre as $p_4$. A double bond has one candidate per choice of $a$ on $b$ and $d$ on $c$. `stereo_lock.py::build_table` keeps, through `stereo_lock.py::_best_quad`, the candidate with the largest $|v|$ at the reference conformer, candidates within `TIE_TOL` of the largest counting as tied and the lowest candidate index winning a tie, and stores its sign $s$, the margin $|v_{\text{ref}}|$ and the band

$$lo = \min\big(\texttt{LO\_MAX}[\text{kind}],\ \texttt{LO\_FRAC} \cdot |v_{\text{ref}}|\big),$$

with `LO_MAX` 0.1 for a tetrahedral element and 0.2 for a double bond, `LO_FRAC` 0.2 **[calibration, QM9 stereo census of the 3000-molecule sample, 2026-09-26]** and `TIE_TOL` $10^{-3}$. The second argument is the smaller exactly when $|v_{\text{ref}}| <$ `LO_MAX` / `LO_FRAC`, which is 0.5 for a tetrahedral element and 1.0 for a double bond; the double-bond indicator is bounded by $\sin t_1 \sin t_2 \le 1$, so a double bond's band is always `LO_FRAC` times its margin. The candidate choice is what separates the indicators at a strained centre: at the ETKDG reference of untagged `CC(C=O)N1C2CCC12` a bridgehead's neighbour tetrahedron has $|v|$ below 0.2 and changes sign on a large share of an MMFF94 chain's samples that RDKit re-perceives as the same isomer, while the chosen candidate stays outside its band on all of them (`tests/conformer/test_stereo_lock.py::test_best_candidate_stays_clear_where_the_neighbour_tetrahedron_flips_inside_the_isomer`, and a hand-built flattened centre in `::test_best_candidate_keeps_the_band_clear_on_a_flattened_neighbour_tetrahedron`). At the ETKDG reference of untagged `COCC1(O)C2COC12` the chain leaves the reference's basin for a lower one that RDKit reports as the same isomer, and there the chosen candidate changes sign (`::test_thermal_check_catches_a_reference_whose_chosen_indicator_fails`).

The term is $P = k\sum_e \mathrm{relu}(lo_e - s_e v_e)^2$ in kcal/mol, $k$ = `stereo_coeff`, zero wherever every element has $s_e v_e \ge lo_e$, with a continuous gradient. `ConformerTorsions.potential_energy` adds it to $U$ before `energy_clip`'s `log_rescale_positive` and before the box wall, without a factor of $T$, and skips the add when the coefficient is zero or the table empty. The baked potential of `energies/conformer_data.py::bake_energies` therefore holds `log_rescale_positive(U + P)` plus the wall, and `prebuilt_sample_to_reward` divides it by $T$ as `energy` divides the live value. The one-pass multi-molecule energy reads the same table off the condition graph ([conformer-conditioning-and-carrier](conformer-conditioning-and-carrier.md)). The baked potential holds the lock at the coefficient in force when the row was scored and is not recomputed on read, so live and baked rows agree at one coefficient only. The coefficient is a property whose setter accepts, after construction, only the value already in force: `train.py::Modeller.set_energy_coeffs` sets any `energy_config` key a stage's `coeff_schedule` or anneal names, and a ramp of this one raises at its first changed value. `energies/conformer_data.py::condition_from_energy` writes the coefficient on every condition graph as the per-graph field `ctree_stereo_coeff`, so every row descended from the graph carries it; `energies/multi_conformer.py::MultiConformerTorsions._resolve_rows` refuses a row whose record differs from the energy's (`energies/conformer_data.py::stereo_coeff_mismatch`, relative tolerance $10^{-6}$, a missing field read as 0), and `conformer_modeller.py::ConformerModeller._refuse_stereo_mismatch` refuses a `cfg:molecules_path` or `cfg:prior_path` file at load on the same test. `build_conformer_conditions.py --stereo-coeff` builds every member at the given coefficient.

Over the $2^E$ sign assignments of $E$ elements, at a point where no element lies in its band $|v_e| < lo_e$ exactly one assignment has $P_s = 0$ and every other has $P_s \ge k(lo_e + |v_e|)^2$ for some element $e$, so $\sum_s e^{-P_s/T} = \prod_e \big(1 + e^{-k(lo_e+|v_e|)^2/T}\big)$, which exceeds 1 by a finite leakage; inside a band no assignment is zero. $\sum_s Z_s = Z_{\text{unlocked}}$ therefore holds up to that leakage and the Boltzmann mass of the bands. Assignments related by a symmetry of the potential that maps the box onto itself have equal $Z_s$, and $\log Z_{\text{unlocked}} - \log Z_{\text{locked}}$ is then the log of their number: $\ln 2$ per centre when each centre's two signs are so related. `tests/conformer/test_stereo_lock.py::test_symmetry_count_and_partition_identity` evaluates both by quadrature at `dihedral` on methane, fluoromethane, bromochlorofluoromethane and methanol, and `::test_partition_identity_where_the_shift_is_not_the_symmetry_count` evaluates the identity on ethane and on 1,2-difluoroethene, whose two assignments are its E and Z isomers.

With the coefficient above zero `_init_stereo` refuses, with these `ChartRefused` codes:

- `stereo_unsupported`: a tagged three-coordinate centre, or any reported stereo type with no indicator, a cumulated double bond included;
- `stereo_unspecified`: `stereo_lock.py::consistent_isomers`, RDKit's enumerator over the unassigned elements on both graphs with three-coordinate tags stripped, returns more than one isomer;
- `stereo_verify_failed`: `stereo_lock.py::realised_isomer`, the canonical isomeric SMILES re-perceived from the reference conformer's coordinates, differs from the input's;
- `stereo_lock_in_band`: an element's margin $m$ is below `MIN_MARGIN` (0.4). At its mirror point $v = -m$ the wrong configuration pays $k(lo + m)^2$, $1.44\,k m^2$ where the band is `LO_FRAC` times the margin;
- `stereo_torsion_double_bond`: at `torsion`, a rotatable column turns a locked double bond.

`ConformerTorsions._find_rotatable` tests bridge membership and the moving side, not bond order, so at `torsion` an acyclic double bond can be a state column, and `build_prior_states.py::draw_states` draws it per column. A rotation about a bond keeps every tetrahedral parity, so at `torsion` $P$ keeps its reference value, zero, on every state of a molecule that is not refused.

With the member's `stereo_coeff` above zero, set by `build_conformer_conditions.py --stereo-coeff` or taken from `--config`'s `energy_config`, `build_conformer_conditions.py::build_member`, which `build_conformer_set.py` also calls, runs `stereo_lock.py::thermal_check` on the member and refuses one with at least one sample in a band under the reason code `stereo_lock_in_band` ([conformer-conditioning-and-carrier](conformer-conditioning-and-carrier.md)). The check runs `stereo_lock.py::mmff_thermal_samples`, a Cartesian Metropolis chain of `THERMAL_CHECK_STEPS` single-atom moves under RDKit's MMFF94 at $kT$ = 1 kcal/mol from the member's reference, independent of the chart, and evaluates the table on every 20th state after a 10% burn-in. `ConformerTorsions` does not run it, so a member built outside the builder, including the single-molecule route's `cfg:energy_config.smiles`, is not checked.

## Construction cost, tiling, and the batch cache

A batch here is one molecule replicated, and the field is rebuilt for it rather than evaluated per copy. `builder.py::collate` dispatches on object identity of the specs: `[spec] * n` takes `builder.py::_collate_replicated`, which tiles molecule zero's index arrays with an atom offset in a fixed number of array operations, and distinct-but-equal specs fall through to the loop. `ff_from_mmff` does the same for parameters: `energy.py::_repeated_block` returns the first block and a repeat count when the reduced pair rows are one block repeated, so the RDKit lookups run once and are tiled, and the repetition is checked element by element, never inferred from `n_mols`. `mxtaltools/conformers/tests/test_conformers.py::test_mmff_pair_parameters_are_the_single_molecule_answer_tiled` asserts the $n$-copy field equals the $n=1$ field repeated.

`ConformerTorsions._batch` caches the collated tree and the force field on the device, keyed on batch size, and `::potential_energy` and `::jacobian_energy` call it before use. The bound is in samples, not entries: the entry just requested is kept and moved to most-recently-used, then least-recently-used entries are dropped while more than one entry remains and the summed batch sizes exceed `_batch_cache_slack` (1.5) times the requested size. The constructor seeds the batch-size-1 entry with the tree and the force field it has already built at the reference geometry (`ConformerTorsions.ff_single`), so one construction calls the force-field builder once, not twice; the eviction rule treats that entry like any other. `tests/conformer/test_conformer_chart_guards.py::test_force_field_is_built_once_per_construction` counts the builder calls under both `cfg:energy_config.force_field` values, and `::test_seeded_batch1_entry_equals_a_cold_rebuild` compares the seeded entry with a cold rebuild field by field, along with `e_ref` and `log_jacobian_const`.

## The prior over conformations

`mxtaltools/conformers/prior.py::InternalPrior` is a table of per-type marginals fitted from observed conformers. Types are `(element, degree)` tuples; `::bond_key` takes the sorted pair, `::angle_key` keeps the apex type and only the elements of the outer atoms, `::torsion_key` takes the central bond alone. Each marginal is a `::Histogram1D` over the spans `R_RANGE`, `THETA_RANGE` and `PHI_RANGE`, the last flagged periodic, mixed toward uniform by `fatten` (0.15), so `sample` and `log_prob` describe the same density and it has full support.

`ConformerTorsions.sample_prior_states` assembles a draw in the chart's own degrees of freedom, keyed on the placement-slot numbering of the spanning tree and the references `r0`, `th0`, `ph0` measured on it. Ring systems go first: a connected component of non-bridge bonds is one block, drawn jointly from a fitted pucker subspace or a discrete bank when `ring_blocks` resolves one for a monocycle, held planar where aromatic, and otherwise rattled about the reference at `ring_jitter_scale` times thermal width; the bank-to-block correspondence is positional over the block's non-$r$ rows, and a mismatched kind sequence raises rather than permuting. A non-aromatic system whose cyclomatic number exceeds one (fused, bridged or spiro) is held even when its key resolves a bank, and a cyclomatic number below one raises. The key is the atom count and sorted `(element, degree)` multiset from `mxtaltools/conformers/prior.py::InternalPrior.ring_systems`, plus the block's DoF count, and it carries no topology; `build_ring_banks.py::RING_SMILES`, the ring list the bank builder fits, holds monocycles only. So without the hold a polycycle whose count and atom types match a monocycle's would draw that monocycle's pucker and still pass the kind-sequence check. `ring_block_info` records a held polycycle as `held_unsupported`; `n_cycles` and `bank_refused` (`'polycyclic'`) separate it from a ring for which no bank resolved. `tests/conformer/test_conformer_chart_guards.py::test_polycyclic_system_is_held_not_banked` requires the draw to equal, bit for bit, the draw from the same prior with its banks removed. The hold applies on the single-molecule route as well as on a set. Sibling torsions about a shared parent are grouped by `::torsion_groups`, every member taking the leader's angular displacement, with the rows of `::held_phi_rows` excluded: the improper rows, plus, when `stereo_coeff` is above zero, every row whose central bond is a locked double bond. Held rows are drawn about `ph0` at `::improper_phi_sigma`, and `::prior_log_prob` and the oracle in `energies/prior_diagnostics.py` score them the same way. Remaining acyclic rows draw from their type's marginal; $r$ and $\theta$ may instead draw at `::thermal_rtheta_sigma`, $\sqrt{kT/2k}$ from the force field's own constants. `InternalPrior.fit` measures each molecule with `mxtaltools/dataset_utils/data_class_methods/conformer_methods.py::MolConformerMethods.internal_dof` on the default tree of `build_conformer_tree`, with no transverse or dummy-frame mask, so a marginal keyed on a bond to a linear centre was fitted against a collinear frame; a dummy-frame row of the chart has its `ph0` measured against its dummy atom ([conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates](conformer-chart-and-internal-coordinates.md)) and draws from that marginal, and a transverse row converts the drawn polar pair to $(u, v)$ in `::state_from_dof`. `::prior_log_prob` raises on a molecule with transverse rows. `joint_rings=False` gives every ring row an independent marginal and is documented as a negative control, with `stats['closure_err']` measured on both arms.

## Clash and descent repair

A product of marginals over torsions lets a long chain intersect itself, and the excess lands in the pair term rather than in any bonded term. `conformer_modeller.py::ConformerModeller._draw_prior_states` optionally repairs it with `cfg:energy_config.prior_relax_steps` steps of `energies/prior_baselines.py::descend`, chunked; `descend` holds an autograd graph over the batch. The code default is 0. `ConformerModeller.init_anchor_buffer_seed` runs the same descent at `cfg:buffers.anchor_buffer.seed_relax_steps` on the anchor seed. The offline builders draw through `energies/conformer_prior_draw.py::draw_member_prior`, the same `sample_prior_states` call and chunked descent followed by `bake_energies`, which raises without a fitted InternalPrior; `tests/conformer/test_build_conformer_set.py::test_draw_member_prior_is_the_modeller_draw` requires its states to equal `_draw_prior_states`'s bit for bit from the same generator at 0 and 3 steps. Nothing in `conformer_modeller.py` calls it.

`descend` optimises the **state** $x$, not the degree-of-freedom rows: $x$ is the level's own coordinate, so a step cannot leave the level's manifold and there is nothing to project back, including where one state column drives several dihedral rows. The default optimizer is `'rprop'` (`torch.optim.Rprop`, `lr` 0.02; `'adam'` is available at 0.05), and the function's first docstring line still names Adam. It returns the best point seen rather than the last, and clamps $x$ into $[-1,1]$ after each step. `prior_baselines.py::tier_minimum` is the same descent from many starts, 150 steps by default, returning best and worst over starts: a multi-start local search, hence an upper bound on the true minimum, so every excess built on it is a lower bound.

## Thermal target, coverage, ESS

Excess is $(U - U_{\min})/T$, and

$$T_{\text{eff}}/T \;=\; 1 + \frac{2\,\mathrm{median}(\text{excess})}{d}.$$

Equipartition puts the median excess of a harmonic system at $d/2$. Three sites compute a quantity under this name and they do not share a zero.

- `energies/conformer_eval_metrics.py::thermal_stats` takes $U_{\min}$ from `tier_minimum` and applies the formula as written, so a correctly thermal sample reads 2.0. It publishes `frac_within_equipartition`, the fraction with excess at or under $d/2$, beside it.
- `prior_baselines.py::cell` feeds the same formula from `::excess_kt`, which is $(U - \text{zero})/T - d/2$; the subtracted $d/2$ shifts the reading down by one, so a thermal sample reads 1.0 there.
- The two print sites in `conformer_modeller.py` (`_draw_prior_states` reporting and `init_anchor_buffer_seed`) use the median minus the batch's own minimum, in kcal/mol, and their message states 2.0 as thermal.

`energies/prior_smoke.py` additionally bands a per-term $T_{\text{eff}}$, $2\langle E_{\text{bond}}\rangle/(nkT)$ over free non-ring bonds and the same over angles, where equipartition fixes the value at 1; the bands are 0.70 to 1.40 and 0.70 to 1.50.

The whole-energy statistic is degenerate on a raw prior draw with free $r$ and $\theta$: the draw width and the score use the same force constants, which cancel. `prior_baselines.py::teff_is_degenerate` marks exactly those cells and excludes optimised draws, uniform draws and levels where $r$ and $\theta$ are frozen.

*ESS.* `prior_diagnostics.py::ess_fraction` is the Kish fraction $\left(\sum w\right)^2 / (n \sum w^2)$ on self-normalised log weights, reported with bootstrap intervals and with $D = \log(1/\text{ESS fraction})$ in nats.

*Coverage.* `prior_diagnostics.py::basin_reference` enumerates the target's rotamer basins as the product of per-group leader centres, realises each with the remaining rows at the reference, scores it, and marks it accessible within `accessible_kt` (10.0) of the best. `::coverage_report` assigns draws by nearest centre and reports which accessible basins received none, with a binomial upper bound $3/n$ on an unobserved basin's probability. The two run in opposite directions: ESS is a functional of the draws obtained, so a basin never proposed contributes no large weight and no warning; coverage enumerates the target's basins first and asks what the sampler assigns them. `basin_reference` is sampler-independent, and `::rotamer_basin_labels` with `conformer_eval_metrics.py::basin_coverage` applies the same partition to a policy's samples.

## Wells and basins

A *dihedral well* is an interval of one projected torsion coordinate between barriers of that projection. A *basin* of the full surface is the set of points from which descent on $U$ over all $d$ coordinates reaches one minimum. They are different partitions: a barrier present in the projected coordinate need not be present in the full surface, so a monotone descent can begin in one dihedral well and end in another without crossing anything. `rotamer_basin_labels` labels the first; `descend` endpoints define the second. How deterministic the descent map is, is the conditional entropy $H(b_K \mid \phi_0, \text{bond})$ of the descent label over $K$ independent draws of the coordinates left free by a fixed torsion vector, in bits against the uniform value $\log_2 k$ for a $k$-rotamer bond. No such statistic is computed in this repository; the labels and the descent it would be built from are.
