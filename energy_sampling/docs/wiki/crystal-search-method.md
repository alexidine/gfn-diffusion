# Crystal search method

*Drift: **M** (mixed). Verified against commit `78de930`, 2026-10-04. Sources at the end.*

The brute-force crystal structure search is the batched local optimiser in `mxtaltools/crystal_search/`. It takes one conformer, a list of space groups and a list of $Z'$ values, instantiates many crystals per combination, initialises each at random, and relaxes all of them at once by gradient descent on an energy. Its output is a list of `MolCrystalData` objects on disk: the anchors a prior dataset is thinned from, and the landscape a comparison against known polymorphs is drawn on. This page covers the mechanics and what a single-start local descent can return. The landscape itself is [landscape-structure](landscape-structure.md); comparing two structures is [structure-comparison-metrics](structure-comparison-metrics.md); the walls the reduction penalty evaluates are derived on [cell-fundamental-domains](cell-fundamental-domains.md).

## Initialisation

`crystal_search/utils.py::init_samples_to_optim` builds the work list: for each molecule, each space group in `cfg:sgs_to_search` and each $Z'$ in `cfg:zp_to_search`, `cfg:num_samples` `MolCrystalData` objects with placeholder cell parameters, then shuffled. `cfg:init_sample_method` `'data'` or `'in_config'` instead optimises crystals supplied ready-made.

`crystal_search/utils.py::get_initial_state` fills the cell parameters per batch, seeded at `cfg:opt_seed + 10000 * batch_idx`. On `'random'` with `cfg:init_reduced` it calls `crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.sample_random_reduced_crystal_parameters`, otherwise `::sample_random_crystal_parameters`; `'reasonable'` calls `::sample_reasonable_random_parameters`. The reduced sampler draws cell angles first and lengths second (lengths are drawn at a target packing coefficient and so depend on the angles), projects with `geometry_utils.py::enforce_crystal_system`, evaluates `crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_cell_reduction_penalty` at its default `margin=0.0`, and keeps only rows at exactly zero, resampling the rest until every row is valid. Centroids, orientations and, at $Z'>1$, handedness are drawn once afterwards, outside the rejection loop. `cfg:init_target_cp` sets the packing coefficient target, as a float or one of two distributions.

## The descent loop

`crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.optimize_crystal_parameters` clones the batch, orients the molecules, and hands the full cell parameters to `crystal_opt_utils.py::gradient_descent_optimization`. The free variables sit in `crystal_opt_utils.py::CrystalParams`, one `nn.Parameter` per row, with any `fixed_dims` columns held as a buffer. Each iteration writes the current parameters onto a fresh clone of the initial batch, calls `::clean_cell_parameters` at `mode='hard'` (lengths clipped at 3 Angstrom, angles bounded around $\pi/2$, centroids into the aunit box), then scores through `crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.analyze`.

`crystal_opt_utils.py::compute_loss` selects the per-row objective by `cfg:opt.optim_target`: an energy column (`lj`, `qlj`, `elj`, `exp6`, `silu`, `ellipsoid`, `uma`, `mace`), the reduction penalty (`reduce`), or an RDF distance (`rdf_dist`). It also holds branches for a score-model output (`classification_score`, `rdf_score`), a latent distance (`latent_dist`) and a centroid separation (`inter_overlaps`); `gradient_descent_optimization` looks the target up in a table of the ten names above before the first step and raises `KeyError` on any other, so a search stage does not reach those branches. The `exp6` column is the typed exp-6 field ([crystal-force-fields](crystal-force-fields.md)); its options come in the stage's `exp6_kwargs`, a dict of `crystal_opt_utils.py::gradient_descent_optimization` that is passed, each key prefixed `exp6_`, to the `analyze` call of every step and of the final scoring pass. `::compute_auxiliary_loss` adds, where configured, a squared packing-coefficient term, a box restriction $\sum 80000/\ell^{12}$ on asymmetric-unit lengths, a compression term gated below packing coefficient 0.65, and under `cfg:opt.enforce_reduced` a $10^4 \times \mathrm{relu}(\text{reduction\_en})$ penalty. The row mean is backpropagated, gradients clipped to `cfg:opt.grad_norm_clip`.

`cfg:opt` is a list, and `crystal_search/run_search.py::crystal_search`, through its batch loop `::_search_loop`, runs its entries in order, collating one stage's output as the next stage's input batch.

## The centroid boundary

The optimised centre is the fractional unit-cell coordinate itself; the asymmetric-unit box exists only as a bound, selected per stage by the `centroid_boundary` argument of `gradient_descent_optimization` (an opt-stage key; mxtaltools `configs/crystal_searches/base.yaml` carries it at the default).

- `clamp` (default): `::clean_cell_parameters` rescales each centre to box units, bounds it with `hardtanh`, and `MolCrystalOps.assign_aunit_centroid` clips the unit-cell value to $[0, 1-10^{-4}]$. The bound is applied in the forward pass and the raw parameters are never projected back. The derivative of `hardtanh` is 0 at and beyond a face, so once a step carries a coordinate there, that coordinate's gradient is 0, and Rprop, which moves each element by the sign of its gradient, never moves it again. Only that coordinate freezes; the others keep descending to a minimum constrained to the face. On acridine sg14 $Z'=2$, 91% of pooled end states have a molecule on a face.
- `wrap`: `crystal_opt_utils.py::wrap_centroid` replaces the bound. The raw centre is lattice-translated into the unit cell, and a value that rounds to exactly 1 is mapped to 0. It is then clamped to $[0, 1-10^{-4}]$ straight-through: the value respects the builders' clip, while the gradient passes unchanged. A centre may leave the asymmetric-unit box through any face. Any unit-cell position describes a valid crystal, so symmetry faces need no handling during the descent. `records['params']` holds the unwrapped centre, so `check_convergence` sees a continuous trajectory. The returned samples pass through `::canonicalize_aunit`, which, for each molecule, keeps the symmetry image whose centre lies deepest inside its `ASYM_UNITS` box (a tie keeps the earlier operator) and raises `ValueError` when no image lies in the box. The image under an operator $(R, t)$ has centre $Rf+t$, wrapped into the cell, and pose $T_{fc} R T_{cf}$ times the molecule's own pose, the transformation of `crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps._transform_aunit_params`; `crystal_building/zp_doubling.py::proper_rotvecs` splits that pose into a rotation vector and a handedness, which flips on improper operations. The crystal is unchanged. mxtaltools `tests/test_centroid_wrap.py` checks both modes atom for atom, including on a puckered, chiral conformer.

The `fd_refold` argument (default off; wrap mode and $Z'=1$ only) re-describes a row as the same crystal when a cleaned cell angle comes within $0.025\pi$ of the $[0.2\pi, 0.8\pi]$ clamp or the cell's volume factor falls to 0.1 or below: the lattice is read off the built cell and canonicalised by `crystal_fd` (`crystal_search/fd_refold.py::batch_refold`), and Rprop keeps the median of each block's learned step sizes (`crystal_opt_utils.py::_carry_optimizer_state`). At the end of the stage it folds the returned rows with `crystal_fd` (`crystal_opt_utils.py::fd_fold_aunit`) in place of `canonicalize_aunit`. That fold never raises, but its centres need not lie in the `ASYM_UNITS` box, so `run_search.py::check_fd_refold_stages` refuses `fd_refold` before a clamp-mode stage or in a campaign, whose hop kicks clamp centres into the box.

## Convergence, the step cap, and batch composition

The loop runs while `s_ind < max_num_steps - 1` and `not converged.all()`. Two stopping rules are selected by the `convergence` argument (`crystal_opt_utils.py::CONVERGENCE_MODES`); both apply from step 50 onward (`min_num_steps`, a constant inside `gradient_descent_optimization`, not a config key), and a `cfg:opt.convergence_eps` at or below 0 turns both off, so every row takes every step.

Under `'params'`, the default, `crystal_opt_utils.py::check_convergence` decides (below). Under `'grad'`, an opt-in, each row stops on its own gradient. Each step, before the clip, `crystal_opt_utils.py::row_gradient_blocks` reads the gradient of the row's own loss and keeps its largest absolute component in each of four blocks:
- cell lengths, per Å;
- cell angles, per radian;
- centres, per Å (fractional gradient over cell length);
- rotation vectors, per radian.

A row whose largest block value stays at or below `grad_tol` (default 0.1, in loss units) for `grad_patience` consecutive steps (default 5) leaves the batch the way a cascade-retired row does, keeps its best state, and is returned with `converged_step`. Its per-block values are recorded under `records['grad']`. A coordinate held by a clean wall has zero gradient through the clamp, so a pinned row counts as converged in that coordinate. The default tolerance is a working assumption, set from stage trajectories of 4 space groups × 32 rows of one molecule on eLJ. There is no quorum. The rule is not the default because on MIPCAS latent starts most stage-1 rows never reached the tolerance (docstring of `gradient_descent_optimization`).

Under `'params'`, `::check_convergence` takes an EMA of the parameter trajectory (`::ema_trajectory`), measures the mean absolute step-to-step change over the last 50 smoothed steps per row, and marks a row converged below `cfg:opt.convergence_eps`. The smoothed trajectory is the normalised exponential average (a constant trajectory gives the constant); until 2026-09-28 `::ema_trajectory` also multiplied it by its smoothing factor 0.1, so a configured threshold acted 10 times looser than written, and configs written before then carry values tuned against that. Above 95% of rows marked, the flag is filled `True` for every row and the loop ends for all of them.

Under `'params'`, stopping is therefore a property of the batch, not of a row: a row that meets the criterion keeps stepping until the batch stops, and a row that has not met it stops when the quorum does. Under `'grad'` it is a property of the row. The learning rate is likewise one schedule for the whole batch: a single `MultiplicativeLR` over the one optimizer, whose factor is 1 unless `cfg:opt.anneal_lr`, in which case one annealing factor shrinks every row's step size together. Under `cfg:opt.optimizer_func` `rprop`, the optimiser in the search configs checked, that schedule does nothing: torch's `Rprop` reads `lr` only to initialise each element's step size on the first step and adapts it from gradient signs thereafter, so later changes to the group's `lr` are never read. Rprop steps by the sign of each gradient element, so the row-mean loss and the joint `clip_grad_norm_`, both positive rescalings, do not couple rows either; under it the batch couples rows inside a stage only through the stopping quorum (`'params'`) and the OOM restart. The latent-space umbrella repulsion is removed, and `crystal_search/run_search.py::reject_retired_keys` refuses a config carrying `umbrella_path` or any `umbrella*` stage key. Which structures share a batch is set by `cfg:batch_size`, by the shuffle in `init_samples_to_optim`, and at runtime: an OOM after step 0 saves the best parameters so far to the run's intermediates file (`::run_files`, named after the run) and re-raises; `crystal_search` cuts the batch size to 0.9 and retries the same cursor, resuming from those states (`::recover_opt_state`) except in `init_sample_method: data` runs, which redo their seeds, and `hops` runs, which draw parents again; `cfg:grow_batch_size` multiplies the size by 1.2 between sets. `::discard_intermediates` deletes the file at the start of a run and after every completed batch. Before that (runs up to mxtaltools 35a53cd8), a retry after a step-0 OOM reloaded an earlier batch's stale file: 46-55% of the acr_wrap_sep26 relaxations repeated earlier starts, and about 0.5% of the aug21 and May end states are such copies. Each completed batch writes a progress file (cursor, batch index) that a resumed run continues from, since `enforce_reduced` drops rows and the output length is not the cursor; seeded outputs carry `dataset_index`, the index of their seed. A row returns not its final state but its best: `argmin` over the recorded per-step loss picks a step index out of `params_record`, and the batch is re-analysed there.

`cfg:opt.early_stop`, a list of `[step, margin]` pairs read with `cfg:opt.early_stop_kT`, adds an energy cascade to `gradient_descent_optimization`. At each listed step, a row whose last loss exceeds `early_stop_ref + margin * early_stop_kT` is retired: later steps evaluate only the remaining rows, the retired row's loss is recorded as $+\infty$ for the steps it skips (`crystal_opt_utils.py::_full_rows`), it returns its best state from before retirement with `early_stopped` set (set by the cascade only, not by gradient convergence), and `check_convergence` takes its quorum over the rows still active. `crystal_search` passes as `early_stop_ref` the lowest final-stage energy among the run's outputs so far, so the cascade is inactive in a run's first batch. `cfg:opt.keep_lowest_fraction` passes only that fraction of a stage's rows to the next stage, lowest energy under the stage's own target first. `cfg:oom_ceiling` caps the growth: an OOM at batch size $B$ cuts the size to $0.9B$ and stops growth at $0.95B$, and after ten clean batches the cap rises by 5%.

## Converged minima and capture

The search config's `minima` block (opt-in) makes the search return converged minima, each with the Hessian of $E/kT$ there, and retire descents that reach a minimum already found. `crystal_search/run_search.py::check_minima_config` fills its keys from `::MINIMA_DEFAULTS` (`kT`, required, in the last stage target's units; `capture_d2` 9; `capture_every` 10; `merge_d2` 1; `max_iter` 40; `tol` $10^{-4}$; `lj_envelope` 1 Å) and raises on an unknown key, a non-positive `kT`, or a last stage whose target is not an energy (`crystal_search/minima.py::ENERGY_TARGETS`), that is not in wrap mode, or that sets `enforce_reduced` or `fd_refold`.

The objective is the last stage target's `analyze` compute divided by `kT`, built as a wrap-mode step builds the crystal and with no auxiliary term or wall (`minima.py::StageEnergy`); `lj_envelope` smooths the LJ-family computes at the cutoff so the double-backward Hessian exists, and the Hessian's $d$ rows come from one backward pass batched over the unit vectors. After the last stage of each batch, `minima.py::finish_minima` re-describes every row the stage did not capture in the identity description (below) and polishes it there with `::newton_polish`, which captures as the stage does (below) before each of its iterations, against the library as it stood before the call; a row captured there is reported as that minimum, with status 0. Rows of the same call that have already converged are capture targets too (one per minimum, measured in the polish's own coordinates: `newton_polish`'s `keys`), and a row captured by one is reported as the minimum that row becomes, or with that row's description and status if it becomes none. Without them every row of a run's first group, polished against an empty library, runs to convergence; on MIPCAS eLJ (2026-10-29, 1,000 rows) they halved the converged polishes (995 to 525) and cut the Hessians by a fifth, with the same minima. The polish is otherwise: per row, saddle-free Levenberg–Marquardt Newton steps, each capped per block by `::POLISH_CAPS` times a trust factor that doubles on an accepted step and halves on a rejected one, until the Hessian is positive semi-definite (`::is_psd`) and the Newton decrement is below `tol` (a Hessian every iteration by default; `hessian_every` lets a row step with an older one in between, and convergence is only declared on a fresh one); a row with no accepted step in `::POLISH_TRIES` tries is stalled, and at most `max_iter` iterations run. `StageEnergy` cleans each crystal (`clean_cell_parameters` at `mode='hard'`), so it is flat along a cell angle past the clamp; a stage's own description can sit there, and a polish in it can end on a point that is not the crystal it reports. The identity description at $Z'=1$ is a reduced cell, well inside the clamp, and a polished row whose cell the clean would still change (`::_inside_clean_box`) gets status 4 and is not added.

One crystal has many parameter descriptions: cell settings, symmetry images, and both handedness signs where the group has an improper element. Minima are therefore identified in one description per crystal, `::identity_description`: crystal_fd's canonical form (`crystal_opt_utils.py::fd_fold_aunit`) for a batch of $Z'=1$ rows, `canonicalize_aunit` otherwise, which leaves cell settings unmerged. Each polished row is re-described twice and its Hessian taken in both: in the identity description, and in the asymmetric-unit box (`canonicalize_aunit`) in which rows are reported. In ascending energy, a converged row whose identity Hessian is positive semi-definite is matched against the library (`::MinimaLibrary.match`): within `merge_d2` of a known minimum of the same key (molecule, space group, $Z'$ and handedness signs, `::identity_key`) under both ellipsoids, it is a recapture of it; otherwise it is added as a new minimum. Every row is then re-analysed on the hard cutoff in the report description and carries `minimum_id`, `recapture`, `polish_status` (`::POLISH_STATUS`), `minimum_f`, `hessian`, and the identity description `id_params`, `id_handedness` and `id_hessian`.

On the last stage `gradient_descent_optimization` receives the library: every `capture_every` steps (default 10) a copy of each active row's evaluated crystal is re-described in the identity description (`::MinimaLibrary.capture_query`), and a row within $d^2 = \delta x^\top H \delta x <$ `capture_d2` of a minimum of its key under that minimum's own Hessian leaves the batch as a cascade-retired row does and is returned with `capture_min`; `finish_minima` reports it unpolished, with the minimum's report-description parameters, handedness and Hessian. $d^2$ is taken at the nearest image of the difference: lattice translations of each centre (`::min_image`) and the space group's shifts to an equivalent origin, the same on every centre (`::centre_shifts`, `::centre_images`: the conventional cell's centring and the Euclidean normalizer's pure translations, from `SYM_OPS` and `NORMALIZER_OPS`; in P$ar{1}$ half a cell along any axis, in P1 none). Translations that normalize the group are the same set in every cell setting of it, so the reduced cells of the identity description need no conversion. Near another seam of the fundamental domain (a face of the reduced-cell domain) two descriptions of one crystal can still differ, and such a pair is counted as two minima. The library lives for the run: it is rebuilt on resume from the saved rows that define their own minimum (`::MinimaLibrary.from_rows`, which raises unless their ids run 0 to $m-1$), and a batch that raises inside `finish_minima` has its additions removed (`::MinimaLibrary.truncate`) before it is redone. The polish's evaluations count toward a shard's effort as energies plus Hessians times $d+1$.

## Streaming search

The search config's `stream` block (opt-in) replaces `_search_loop` with `crystal_search/stream_search.py::stream_search`. A batch search steps every row of a batch until the batch stops, so rows retired early leave its sequential depth unchanged. The stream keeps `slots` slots (default `cfg:batch_size`), each carrying one start through every stage on its own clock: its stage, its step count there, its Rprop step sizes (torch's rule, `::ETAS`, `::STEP_SIZES`), its best state and loss in the stage, and its convergence record. All occupied slots are evaluated together each step; every `check_every` steps (default 5) each slot is tested. A row leaves its stage at `max_num_steps - 1` steps, or from 50 steps on when its own smoothed parameter change is below `convergence_eps` (`check_convergence`'s per-row test without the 95% quorum, `::_converged`); it moves to the next stage in place from its best state, or after the last stage is emitted. Each stage is evaluated at its own `cutoff` and `supercell_size`; rows of stages sharing both are evaluated in one call, so stages with different cutoffs cost one call each per step (on MIPCAS eLJ, 2026-10-30, a 6 Å first stage made a 2,000-start run 29% slower, not faster). With the `minima` block every stage is evaluated with `lj_envelope`, as the polish is, and `::check_stream_config` refuses a cutoff not above it. The cascade (`early_stop`) retires a row whose loss at its first check past each listed step exceeds the lowest finished energy plus the margin: with the `minima` block that energy is the enveloped one at the last stage's cutoff (`minimum_f` × kT), and a bfgs row's loss is that of its accepted point. With the `minima` block the last stage captures (rows captured in the first stage reached the captured minimum on a full polish 80% of the time on MIPCAS, with or without its compression term, against 89–95% in the last stage, and capturing there saved no time; 2026-10-30), and emitted rows are finished in groups of `polish_chunk` (default 1,000; the library, and so capture and the cascade, waits for the first group).

The last stage may be `optimizer_func: bfgs` (stream only; it needs the `minima` block, no compression and `convergence_eps` > 0). Each of its rows runs gradient-only BFGS inside the stream's batched evaluations, one trial point per step (`::_bfgs_step`): a trial is accepted on the Armijo test with a strict decrease, which updates the inverse-Hessian estimate $B$ (the first update preceded by the scaling $s^\top y / y^\top y$) and doubles the row's trust factor; a rejected trial halves the trust and shortens the next step by `::BFGS_SHRINK`. Steps are capped per block by `minima.py::POLISH_CAPS` times the trust. A row leaves the stage when, after at least `::BFGS_MIN_UPDATES` updates, $\tfrac12 g^\top B g / kT$ is below `convergence_eps`, at the step cap, or after `POLISH_TRIES` rejections twice in a row ($B$ reset to the identity in between). On MIPCAS eLJ (2026-10-30, 2,000 starts, idle GPU) a Rprop stage followed by a 200-step bfgs stage cost the same wall time as two Rprop stages (113 s each) with 36% fewer polish evaluations, and found the same minima; a bfgs stage added after both Rprop stages was 40% slower. `configs/crystal_searches/minima_stream/mipcas_elj.yaml` uses the first. An emptied slot takes the next start at once. The pressure term of `compression_factor` is applied per row; the gradient-norm clip and `anneal_lr` are not, since neither changes an Rprop step. A CUDA out-of-memory error in a step halves the number of rows evaluated together and redoes the step (rows are independent, and nothing has been updated yet), and after `::REGROW_STEPS` clean steps the number doubles again up to `slots`; one in the polish undoes the group's library additions, halves `finish_minima`'s `energy_chunk` and redoes the group.

With `seeds` (which needs the `minima` block), a saved list of crystal rows (another search's output, a prior's anchors, known structures), the seeds are handled before any random start (`::_seed_starts`): each row whose molecule identifier, space group and $Z'$ (`minima.py::row_key`) the search has gets its cell parameters and handedness put on the search's copy of that molecule (no matching row raises), with start index $-(j+1)$ for row $j$, and goes straight to `finish_minima` in groups of `polish_chunk`, with no descent stage. The polish relaxes each seed and takes its Hessian, and the merge and the polish's capture are the de-duplication: seeds that reach one minimum become one library entry. The seeds are taken in a random order (a permutation seeded by `cfg:opt_seed`). With `stop_p`, before each seed group the same estimate over the seeds' rows alone (`missing_mass(..., seeds=True)`) is compared with `stop_p`; once below it the remaining seeds are skipped and the random starts begin, and a resumed run, checking before its first group, skips them too. A file of distinct minima keeps that bound high (most of its seeds reach a minimum no other seed reaches) and is polished whole. With `stop_p` (which needs the `minima` block, and refuses a search of more than one molecule, space group or $Z'$, since its band is measured from the lowest minimum), after every finished group `minima.py::missing_mass` takes the Good–Turing estimate of the chance that the next random start ends at a minimum not yet found within `stop_band_kT` (default 5) of the lowest: $f_1/n$, where $n$ counts the random starts' rows and $f_1$ the band's minima reached by exactly one of them; seeds are not in this sample. Once its upper bound, the one-sided 90% Poisson bound on $f_1$ (`coordinator.py::poisson_upper`) divided by $n$, is below `stop_p`, the run ends at once: the rows in flight and emitted rows not yet polished are dropped and counted (`stopped.dropped` in the effort file), since by the same estimate they are unlikely to hold a new minimum, and a resumed run draws them again (finishing them had taken about 30% of a MIPCAS eLJ run, 2026-10-30). A stop request still drains. The run ends at `cfg:num_samples` random starts at most. The estimates, the seeds' included, are recorded in the effort file. A minimum known from a seed and reached by one random start counts in $f_1$, so the estimate over-states the chance of a minimum not yet known. With the `minima` block the run also writes `<run>_minima.pt` (`minima.py::minimum_rows`): one row per minimum, the row that defined it, with `hits` (random starts that ended there, captures included) and `seed_hits`.

mxtaltools `configs/crystal_searches/minima_stream/mipcas_elj.yaml` is a worked example of the two blocks together (MIPCAS, space group 2, $Z'=1$, eLJ), and mxtaltools `tests/test_stream_search.py` checks that it passes both config checks.

`::check_stream_config` refuses a campaign (`cfg:coord_dir`), a start method other than `random` or `reasonable`, `stop_p` outside $(0, 1)$ or without the `minima` block, a `seeds` file that does not exist, and stages it does not reproduce: an optimiser other than rprop, clamp mode, `enforce_reduced`, `target_packing_coeff`, `fd_refold`, `keep_lowest_fraction`, `convergence: grad`, any other stage key, or stages with different targets. Starts are drawn in blocks of `slots` seeded as a batch search seeds its batches (`get_initial_state` by block index), so a stream with `slots` equal to `cfg:batch_size` relaxes the same starts, and every output carries `start_index` and `stage_steps`. Outputs and a progress record (`mode: stream`) are saved after every finished group; a resumed run skips the starts already in its output and relaxes the rest, including those in flight at the stop; a stop request lets the slots in flight finish without drawing new starts. A batch-search output is refused. The run writes its effort, per stage and for the polish, to `<run>_stream_effort.json`.

## One start is a local minimum

Gradient descent from one initialisation returns a local minimum of the objective, in the basin containing that start. Any quantity defined as a minimum over coordinates that are not being scanned, $U_{\text{eff}}(\phi) = \min_{r} U(\phi, r)$, is a global optimisation over $r$ at every $\phi$; one descent from one start returns a local minimum instead, an upper bound on $U_{\text{eff}}(\phi)$ and never a lower one. The error is one-sided, grows with the number of pinned coordinates, and is not reported: the loop terminates on parameter motion, not on any property of the minimum reached. The search has this shape, its scanned coordinate discrete (space group, $Z'$) and its unscanned ones the twelve or more cell parameters.

## Seeding versus sampling for a named basin

The random initialiser covers the latent cube, not a nominated point in it. The parameter vector has twelve components at $Z'=1$ and more above it; the probability that a draw lands within a fixed latent radius of one pre-specified structure falls off with that dimension, and the expected hits are the sample count times that probability times the probability of descending back to the structure from there. Seeding sets the first factor to one: a draw placed at a chosen latent displacement from the target and relaxed leaves only the return probability. Basin width in the return sense and basin measure under the initialiser are on [landscape-structure](landscape-structure.md).

## The reduced-cell search mode

Two independent switches act on the cell domain. `cfg:init_reduced` makes the initialiser hard: rows are resampled until the reduction penalty is exactly zero at `margin=0.0`, so every start lies in the closed fundamental domain. `cfg:opt.enforce_reduced` acts during and after descent: `reduction_en` joins the analysis computes, the penalty is evaluated at `margin=0.01` rather than 0 (a shrunken zero set), the $10^4$ term enters the loss, and the returned list is filtered to rows whose penalty at `margin=0.0` is below $10^{-3}$, so a stage can return fewer rows than it took. Which walls those are is per crystal system and per space group: triclinic gets the Niggli-condition walls by default, with `MXT_LEGACY_TRICLINIC_WALLS=1` restoring the pre-Niggli set, monoclinic the class walls ([cell-fundamental-domains](cell-fundamental-domains.md)). Inside the loop the walls are soft, a term in the loss; the hard cell constraints there are `clean_cell_parameters` and `enforce_crystal_system`.

A stage with `enforce_reduced` off returns cells in any setting. `crystal_search/standardize.py::standardize_cells` re-expresses a crystal in its reduced cell without a symmetry search. The candidates are basis changes $N$ (new basis vectors as columns, in old fractional coordinates) that map `SYM_OPS[sg]` onto itself, with an origin shift $o$ from the quarter-cell grid where the group needs one (`::setting_shift`; Cc and C2/c need $o = b/4$ for $c' = c \pm a$, P2$_1$/c admits no odd multiple of $a$ in $c'$). `::choose_basis` reduces the lattice (Gauss reduction in the ac-plane for monoclinic groups, `spglib.niggli_reduce` for triclinic ones), tries small integer corrections of that basis, and keeps the candidate with zero penalty at `margin=0` nearest the identity; for higher crystal systems the only candidate is $N = I$. Centres map as $x' = N^{-1}(x - o)$, orientations rotate by the change of Cartesian frame and are converted by `common/geometry_utils.py::rotmat2rotvec_stable` (a float64 quaternion route, exact at the identity and at a half turn), and `canonicalize_aunit` returns each molecule to its box. A crystal already at zero penalty keeps its parameters exactly; a crystal with no zero-penalty candidate, or whose operator list is not `SYM_OPS[sg]` as a set (`::standard_setting`), raises, or is left unchanged with `ok` false under `on_failure='flag'`. `::same_crystal_deviation` compares two descriptions atom by atom.

## Output, chunks and stamps

`crystal_search` writes `<cfg:out_dir>/<cfg:run_name>.pt`, a list of `MolCrystalData` objects, after every set, and resumes from it unless `cfg:force_restart_run`; `cfg:save_trajs` writes the per-stage record, parameter trajectory included, beside it. A wave of searches is a set of run names sharing a stem and ending in an integer, which `data_processing/utils.py::load_search_chunks` reassembles by globbing `<stem>_<digits>.pt`, preferring a chunk corrected under `f047_rescored`. With `require_uma_state` it raises on any chunk whose `uma_energy_state` stamp is missing or below `::UMA_ENERGY_STATE`, and `data_processing/collate_prior.py` passes that flag on the `uma` route, so an unstamped chunk stops a prior build rather than entering it.

A second stamp lives on the training side. `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.stamp_lj_coeff` writes the run's `lj_coeff` onto every freshly built batch as a per-graph attribute inside `::analyze_crystal_batch`, mxtaltools `crystal_analysis.py::MolCrystalAnalysis.compute_eLJ_energy` scales its output by it, and `::assert_lj_coeff_stamped` refuses a batch with no stamp or with values disagreeing with the run's, checking every row. The searcher does not stamp its output.

The `uma` and `mace` targets load a predictor in `crystal_search/utils.py::parse_opt_config` and pass it into `analyze`; the routes are [mlip-energy-routes](mlip-energy-routes.md).

`crystal_search/run_state.py` governs a run's files. The output list and the progress file are written by `::atomic_torch_save` and `::atomic_json_dump` (a temporary file beside the target, flushed and fsynced, then renamed over it), outputs first; on resume `::committed_outputs` truncates the output list to the progress file's `n_out`, so a batch saved without its progress entry is redone. A fresh run commits a zero-cursor progress file before its first batch, and the cursor stops at the length of the work list. `::RunLease` keeps `<run>_owner.json`, naming the owner by Slurm job, restart count, host and pid: `acquire` refuses a lease another owner renewed within `cfg:lease_stale_after_s` (default 1800 s), takes over at once one left by an earlier restart of the same Slurm job, and re-reads the file after `cfg:lease_settle_s` so two simultaneous takeovers leave one owner; a heartbeat thread touches it between saves (a read that fails does not stop it), `check` raises `RunLeaseLost` before each output save if another process owns it, and `release` removes it only if this process does. `::StopRequest` ends the batch loop between batches on SIGUSR1 or when a stop file (`cfg:stop_files`, and a campaign's) exists, and turns SIGTERM into `SystemExit` so the lease is released on the way out.

## Campaigns

`crystal_search/coordinator.py` coordinates many search jobs through a shared directory, the campaign, named by `cfg:coord_dir`. A job with that key writes one shard per completed batch (`::write_shard`): the batch's cell parameters, handedness, final energy under the last stage's target, `lj`, `dataset_index`, the latent kick length of each hop start (`kick`), and its effort (relaxations, row-evaluations of the last stage's target and of other stages, attempts, wall time, device) with the energy-model id. A failed shard write is printed and the search continues. Between batches the job stops if `STOP` or `STOP.<stream>` exists in the campaign (the stream is `cfg:coord_stream`), and, with `cfg:coord_curate_every_s`, runs a curate pass itself (`::maybe_curate`) when the last one is older than that and no other job holds the curator lease, re-reading the pass age once it holds the lease. A pass run by hand (the module's command line), by `::run_loop` or by `::export` takes the same lease (`::curate_locked`), which raises `RunLeaseLost` without waiting while another process holds it; `::run_loop` catches that and waits for its next interval, as it does after a pass that raised; it ends on two consecutive stop passes or at `max_hours`. The command line turns SIGTERM into an exit that releases the curator lease. `::energy_model_id` names the scorer: the final stage's target and, for `mace` and `uma`, the model file's basename, size and the SHA1 of its first megabyte and, for a file above 2 MiB, its last.

`::curate` is one pass over the campaign directory, configured by `coord.yaml` (`::CampaignConfig`). It ingests priors and new shards, each once, refusing any scored under another energy-model id (a prior file that fails to load is refused and recorded, not fatal), and raises if the model id, `identity_cut` or `rdf_mode` differs from the existing registry's, a registry without `rdf_mode` counting as `envwise`. A prior file is keyed by its path and the SHA1 of its content, so a file whose content has changed is ingested again, its states added to the earlier ones. A registry that lacks the lineage record described below, or whose lineage lists differ in length from its hits or basins, has it rebuilt from the shards (`::backfill_lineage`): each run of consecutive hop-stream hits from one shard is matched to that shard's lowest-energy physical rows, as many as the hits (the rows admitted at ingestion, whatever the energy reference was then), which must carry exactly the hits' energies in row order, and each hit takes its row's `dataset_index` as parent; a group that does not match keeps no parent and is counted. A registry without `confirm_effort`, the record of each stream's effort at its last verdict, has its stop-confirmation streaks reset. Each physical state (`::physical`) within `window_kT` of the energy reference is rebuilt from its stored parameters with the campaign conformer (`::rebuild_crystals`), standardised (`standardize_cells`, unhandled rows kept as stored and recorded), given an RDF (`::compute_rdfs`, 10 Angstrom, its channels set by the `coord.yaml` key `rdf_mode`: `envwise`, the default, has one channel per pair of bonded-environment labels, and `atomwise` one per pair of atom indices) and assigned by leader clustering (`::_assign`): it joins the basin of the nearest leader within `identity_cut` in `::rdf_distance_matrix` distance, else it opens a basin as its leader. A basin keeps its lowest-energy state as its representative.

For each stream $s$ and each band $b$ of `bands_kT`, `::compute_stats` counts $f_1$, the basins with exactly one state in the band where that state came from $s$, and $W_s$, the stream's row-evaluations of the campaign's energy model. Every hit records the basin its hop start was kicked from (`parent`, -1 for other streams) and every basin the parent of the hop that opened it (`basin_parent`); a hop hit whose shard carries a kick length has it stored under the hit's index in `hit_kick`; a hop hit that lies in its parent basin or in one of that basin's ancestors (`::lineage_returns`) is left out of every count. `::verdicts` marks the stream stopped once, for every band, $W_s / f_1^{\mathrm{hi}} > Z_b$, where $f_1^{\mathrm{hi}}$ is the one-sided 90% Poisson upper bound on $f_1$ (`::poisson_upper`), with at least `min_relaxations` relaxations and `min_hits` states in the band, on `passes_to_confirm` passes in a row; a pass advances the streak only if $W_s$ grew since the stream's last verdict, and a pass that fails resets it. A stream is also marked stopped on a single pass where the test holds and at least $\max($`confirm_settle_s`$, 3 \times$ the wall time of the stream's longest shard$)$ seconds have passed since the verdict that last saw $W_s$ grow (`confirm_settle_s` is a `coord.yaml` key, default 3600 s; an effort record that lacks the longest shard's wall time gives the stream's mean wall time per shard in its place); a stream's first verdict counts as growth, and a registry holding no such time for the stream starts the clock at its next verdict. $W_s / f_1$ is the Good-Turing estimate of the effort per new basin. A stream that finds no new basin stops at $W_s > 2.30\,Z_b$, since $f_1^{\mathrm{hi}}(0) = 2.30$. The pass writes `STOP.<stream>` for a stopped stream, and a stream whose `STOP.<stream>` exists counts as stopped whatever its verdict. It writes `STOP` when at least one stream has a rule in `coord.yaml` and every stream with a rule counts as stopped, a hop stream counting as stopped for this when it has no eligible parent and has brought no new hop work for `exhaust_settle_s` (a `hops` key, default 3600 s), or when the total effort reaches `hard_cap`.

With a `hops` block, each pass writes `hop_parents.pt` (`::write_hop_parents`): basins within the hop window, below the generation cap and the per-basin quota `max_per_basin` (a `hops` key, default 32), whose cell lengths survive the latent round trip (`::latent_representable`), least-hopped first; the file also records the registry's basin count and the quota. A job with `cfg:init_sample_method` `hops` (`run_search.py::_hop_batch`) draws its parents from that list. Where the file records the quota, each parent has a number of kicks left for the job, the smaller of two counts: the quota less the parent's listed hop starts, less the job's own kicks that no listing has counted yet; and the job config's `coord_hop_share` (default 1) times the quota less the listed hop starts, rounded up, less the job's kicks under the current listing. Parents are drawn uniformly over the kicks left, so a batch can hold fewer starts than `cfg:batch_size`; a file without the quota is drawn with weight $1/(1 + \text{hop starts})$. The job applies `log_noise_latent_parameters` with `keep_start_representable=True` to each parent's cell, which adds the kick to the parent's unclipped latent vector and widens the latent clamp to include the starting value, and records the parent basin as `dataset_index` and the kick length as `hop_kick`; a basin a hop opens is one generation below its parent. A hop job waits, polling every `cfg:coord_hop_poll_s` (default 60 s) between loop tops, while `hop_parents.pt` is missing or unreadable, lists no parent and was written on a registry with no basin, lists no parent but comes from a pass numbered below two more than the pass count read after the job's own last shard, or lists parents none of which has a kick left for the job; it raises `run_search.py::HopParentsUnavailable` after `cfg:coord_hop_wait_s` (default $\max(3 \times$ `cfg:coord_curate_every_s`$, 3600) + 1800$ s, the curate interval taken as 900 s when that key is unset). It stops when a list from a pass at or above that number, written on a registry with at least one basin, is empty; the number is 0 until the process has written a shard, so before then any such empty list stops it. Priors are known structures ingested as stream `prior`. `::export` first runs `::curate_locked`, then raises if any shard on disk is missing from the registry (`::pending_shards`), and writes one crystal per basin within a band (its representative, in its reduced cell) with its energy, basin index and state count, and a CSV beside it that adds the stream and run that found each basin first.

gfn_diffusion `eval/campaign_compack.py` measures, on a campaign's own shard states and with the coordinator's RDF, in the campaign's `rdf_mode`, and distance, the probability that COMPACK calls two states one packing as a function of their RDF distance. Pairs are stratified over distance in two rounds under one budget, the second inside the transition a logistic fit of the first round finds (`campaign_compack.py::fit_logistic`). Same-crystal controls compare a state with its re-description in another cell setting. It reports the identity cut by one rule, the largest distance at which the isotonic estimate of the 20-of-20 match probability is at least 0.95 (`campaign_compack.py::rule_summary`; [structure-comparison-metrics](structure-comparison-metrics.md)). Its `dedup` refuses a registry missing shards on disk unless `--allow_pending` is passed, exits when a calibration's `rdf_mode` differs from the campaign's, compares by COMPACK every pair of a band's basins closer than the rule's `d_hi` and within an energy gap (all of them unless a budget caps the count, the cap's uncompared pairs counted per merged group), and merges matched pairs. A pair with a final COMPACK result is not run again; an error or a timeout is run again on the next call.

## Could be tooling

A batch-composition record is mechanical. `gradient_descent_optimization` already holds `params_record`, the per-step loss and the stopping step, so writing per row the step it first met `convergence_eps`, the step the batch stopped, and which of the three conditions stopped it would make truncation a column in the output. Under `'grad'` the row's own stop is recorded (`converged_step`); under `'params'` it is not. The reduction filter's per-stage drop count is known at the same point and is not recorded either.

The second is a stamp audit: `load_search_chunks` already refuses a stale `uma_energy_state`, and the same pass could report per chunk the search config hash, each stage's optimisation target and `enforce_reduced`, so a prior built from chunks produced under different settings is visible before thinning.
