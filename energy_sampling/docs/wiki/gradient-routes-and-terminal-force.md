# Gradient routes and terminal force

*Drift: **T** (theory). Verified against commit `a637e70`, 2026-09-20. Sources at the end.*

A loss built from trajectory quantities reaches the policy parameters by two distinct routes, and the sampler carries switches on each. This page derives both on a per-step Gaussian policy, the asymmetry that empties one of them for whichever policy drew the trajectory, the machinery that lets $\mathrm{d}\log R/\mathrm{d}x_T$ enter, the noise floor on the terminal step, and what the closed-form step density costs a higher-order integrator. The residual these routes differentiate is on [trajectory-balance](trajectory-balance.md); which branch carries which coefficient block is on [loss-composition](loss-composition.md).

## The two routes on one step

The forward kernel is a reparameterised Gaussian step. `models/gfn.py::GFN.fwd_propagate` forms

$$x_{t+1} \;=\; x_t \;+\; \Delta t\,\mu_\theta(x_t,t) \;+\; \sqrt{\Delta t}\,\bigl(\sigma_\theta(x_t,t)\odot\varepsilon_t + V_\theta(x_t,t)\varepsilon^r_t\bigr),\qquad \varepsilon_t,\varepsilon^r_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I),$$

with the low-rank term present only under DPLR, and `::GFN.fwd_gauss_logprob` scores the same increment under $\mathcal{N}(\Delta t\,\mu_\theta,\ \Delta t\,C_\theta)$. Write $L$ for the branch loss and $\tau=(x_0,\dots,x_T)$ for the realised trajectory. Then

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}L}{\mathrm{d}\theta} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta}\Big|_{\tau\ \text{fixed}}}_{\text{explicit}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{t}\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_t}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_t}{\mathrm{d}\theta}}_{\text{pathwise}} .$$

The **explicit** route differentiates the transition log-densities at fixed samples: on a trajectory-balance residual it is $\nabla_\theta \log P_F(\tau) - \nabla_\theta\log P_B(\tau\mid x)$, weighted per row by the loss's derivative in the residual. The **pathwise** route exists only if the sampled states carry gradient. Under `detach_traj` the step's mean, its sampling log-variance (`::GFN.fwd_get_logvars`) and the low-rank factor are all detached before propagation, so $x_{t+1}$ is a constant and the second sum vanishes; the branch builders set `detach_traj=loss_coeffs.traj_grads == 0`. `cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.path_grad_scale` at 0 detaches only the sampling log-variance, through the flag `gfn.path_grad_scale_live` that `get_gfn_forward_loss` sets around the forward rollout and restores afterwards, leaving the mean channel live on the path and the density route untouched.

## The Jacobian chain and what truncation cuts

With the path live, let $f_t(x_t,\theta)$ be the step map at the drawn noise. Backward accumulation runs

$$g_t \;:=\; \frac{\mathrm{d}L}{\mathrm{d}x_t} \;=\; \frac{\partial L}{\partial x_t}\Big|_{\text{densities}} \;+\; J_t^{\!\top} g_{t+1}, \qquad J_t \;=\; \frac{\partial f_t}{\partial x_t} \;=\; I + \Delta t\,\frac{\partial \mu_\theta}{\partial x} + \sqrt{\Delta t}\,\frac{\partial (\sigma_\theta\odot\varepsilon_t)}{\partial x},$$

and the parameters collect $\sum_t (\partial f_t/\partial\theta)^{\!\top} g_{t+1}$ with $\partial f_t/\partial\theta = \Delta t\,\partial\mu_\theta/\partial\theta + \sqrt{\Delta t}\,\varepsilon_t \odot \partial\sigma_\theta/\partial\theta$. At step $t$ the terminal contribution is premultiplied by $J_{T-1}\cdots J_{t+1}$, a product of $T-t-1$ Jacobians: the forward path gradient is backpropagation through the reparameterised chain.

`cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.path_grad_last_k` bounds that product. In `::GFN.get_traj_fwd` the per-step detach flag is `step_detach = detach_traj`, overridden to `False` for $i \ge T-k$; steps before $T-k$ are detached, so $g$ is never formed there and at most $k$ Jacobians multiply. The state entering step $T-k$ is a non-grad leaf, but the step's own output still depends on $\theta$ through the drift and variance heads, so the last $k$ steps' parameters receive a genuine pathwise gradient. That function's `state_grad_hook` is registered on every live state; `get_gfn_forward_loss` supplies one when any step is live and losses are reported, and it records $\lVert \mathrm{d}L/\mathrm{d}x_{i+1}\rVert$ as `pathgrad/state_grad_step{i}`. The ratio of the two channels in $\partial f_t/\partial\theta$ is $\sqrt{\Delta t}/\Delta t$, so as $\Delta t$ shrinks the scale channel carries the larger share of a terminal force, which is the channel `path_grad_scale` removes.

## The asymmetry between sampler and non-sampler

Let $\psi_i$ be the loss's derivative in row $i$'s residual (for the Huber trajectory-balance term, $\mathrm{clip}(r_i,\pm\beta)$). With the path detached, the whole gradient is the explicit route.

**Forward branch.** $\tau \sim P_F(\cdot\,;\theta)$. The $P_B$ part, $-\mathbb{E}[\psi\,\nabla_\theta \log P_B(\tau\mid x)]$, differentiates a density under a distribution that does not depend on those parameters: an ordinary off-policy gradient at fixed data. The $P_F$ part is $\mathbb{E}_{P_F}[\psi\,\nabla_\theta \log P_F]$, a score-function term. Since $\mathbb{E}_{P_F}[\nabla_\theta\log P_F]=0$, it equals $\mathrm{Cov}_{P_F}(\psi,\nabla_\theta\log P_F)$. A component of $\psi$ common to every trajectory, which is what a shift in the level $\log Z$ or a uniform reward offset produces, contributes nothing in expectation; only the variation of $\psi$ across the batch survives. So with `traj_grads: 0` the sampling policy is reweighted between trajectories and receives no expected signal from the level of the residual.

**Backward branch.** $\tau \sim P_B(\cdot\mid x;\theta)$ from a stored terminal, and the roles exchange exactly: $+\mathbb{E}[\psi\,\nabla_\theta\log P_F]$ is the clean off-policy term, and $-\mathbb{E}_{P_B}[\psi\,\nabla_\theta\log P_B] = -\mathrm{Cov}_{P_B}(\psi,\nabla_\theta\log P_B)$ is the score, again level-blind. `mk_dev.yaml` carries `traj_grads: 0.0` on `fwd` and `replay` and `1.0` on `bwd`, and `::GFN.bwd_propagate` detaches the backward mean and variance at 0, so on that branch the pathwise route into $P_B$ is what `traj_grads` controls. The same reasoning is recorded inside `gflownet_losses.py::get_gfn_backward_loss` for the `level_gap` term, whose default `level_gap_pf_only` drops the $\log P_B$ factor, whose expectation is exactly zero.

Two qualifications. The statement holds for parameters reached through one density only: `::GFN.init_policies` builds `forward_policy` and `backward_policy` as separate networks, but both are evaluated on the outputs of the shared state encoder `s_model` and time encoder `t_model` (and, on a conditional route, the shared condition embedding), so on those shared parameters the two terms add. And the vanishing is in expectation; a finite batch still carries the score's variance, unreduced by a baseline.

## The terminal reward force

`cfg:fwd_loss_coeffs.reward_grads` at 0 makes `gflownet_losses.py::get_loss_reward` score the reward under `no_grad`. Nonzero, the energy graph stays attached and $\mathrm{d}\log R/\mathrm{d}x_T$ joins $\partial L/\partial x_T$, which the pathwise route carries into the last steps: a per-row push on the terminal toward higher $\log R$, that is, lower energy. It is inert without a live terminal, that is, unless `path_grad_last_k > 0` or `traj_grads` is nonzero.

The converse does **not** hold, and `configs/mk_dev.yaml` asserted for a time that it did. `path_grad_last_k > 0` at `reward_grads` 0 is inert *in the reward*, because `::get_loss_reward` detaches both $x_T$ and $\log R$ there — but not in the densities: on the live last step `::GFN.fwd_gauss_logprob` and `::GFN._eval_pb_logprob` both differentiate through the live `next_state`, and for a reparameterised Gaussian the $\mu$ terms cancel to leave a $-\partial\log\sigma/\partial\theta$ residue, with $\log P_B$'s dependence on $x_T$ a further genuine path term. So $k>0$ at `reward_grads` 0 is a different gradient from $k=0$. Measured on the unconditional route (`pathgrad_sep14`, 3430 steps): that arm ran 5.4% *worse* than its $k=0$ control on `fwd/loss` and cost 11.6% more per step, against the full force arm's 34% improvement. A control at $k=0$ therefore handicaps the force arm it is compared against.

Which leg supplies the force also depends on $\lambda$ in a way the mixture alone does not imply. `energies/prior_flow.py::PriorFlow.energy` wrapped its forward in `torch.no_grad()` until 2026-09-21, making $\mathrm{d}(\text{flow leg})/\mathrm{d}x$ identically zero: the terminal force was $\lambda\,\mathrm{d}E_{\text{phys}}/\mathrm{d}x$ at *every* $\lambda$, with no flow contribution even at $\lambda \to 0$ where the flow is the whole target, so a $\lambda=0$ force arm had essentially no force at all. The guard is now conditional on the caller holding grad, and every detached scoring path keeps its previous cost. Measured after the change, on phase-1 draws: flow leg $|\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x|$ median 56.8 where it had been 0, and the $\lambda=0.01$ total 100.1 against 61.2 before.

`reward_grad_clip` installs `::arm_reward_grad_hook` on the `log_r` leaf: at backward it applies `nan_to_num` unconditionally, reports `rewardgrad/nonfinite_frac`, and clips $\mathrm{d}L/\mathrm{d}\log R$ per row at the given value (0 = no clip, sanitising still runs). `reward_grad_gate` and `reward_grad_force_clip` select a different construction: `get_gfn_forward_loss` scores the reward on a detached leaf copy of the terminal, reads $F = \mathrm{d}\log R/\mathrm{d}x_T$ off that graph and $\mathrm{d}L/\mathrm{d}\log R$ off the loss, both by autograd, rescales $F$ per row to norm at most `reward_grad_force_clip`, zeroes the row weight where the detached residual $\log Z + \log P_F - \log R - \log P_B$ is at or below `reward_grad_gate`, and adds `(states[:, -1] * c).sum()` minus its own detached value: a term of value zero whose gradient on the live terminal is the reshaped push. This construction is armed only when `reward_grads` is also nonzero; with `reward_grads` at 0 both keys are read and the reshape is skipped. Once armed, `get_gfn_forward_loss` raises unless a live terminal exists (`path_grad_last_k > 0` or `traj_grads` nonzero) and `freeze_policy` is 0.

**The reshape can deliver nothing, silently, and only one metric says so.** It does two things: it scores the reward on a *detached leaf copy*, which severs the ordinary autograd route from the energy to $x_T$, and it reads $\mathrm{d}L/\mathrm{d}\log R$ by `autograd.grad(loss, log_r)` against the loss that exists *at that point in `get_gfn_forward_loss`* — the branch loss, not the fused one. On a branch whose own loss carries no live $\log R$, that call returns `None`, the weight becomes zeros, and the surrogate injects a zero push while the real path stays cut. The canonical conditional seat is exactly that case: in `conditional_vargrad`'s `var_conditioning` the forward branch has `tb: 0`, and with `vg_lb` and `vg_lme` both 0 the VarGrad rows are discarded, leaving only `emp_z` against a detached `log_Z_emp_rows`; the only term that does depend on $\log R$ is the pooled cross-branch VarGrad, assembled later in `train.py::Modeller.fused_train_step`. Every force diagnostic still reads healthy, because $F$ itself is computed correctly — `rewardgrad/force_norm_mean`, `force_norm_max`, `force_clipped_frac`, `force_nonfinite_frac` — and `rewardgrad/abs_mean` is nonzero too, since that comes from the hook firing during the real backward. `rewardgrad/push_norm_mean` is the only readout that falls to exactly 0. Measured 2026-09-21: 0 on the conditional forward seat, 0.53 to 1.09 on the `force_sep18` arms, whose forward branch carries `tb: 1`. The plain path is unaffected, being ordinary autograd through whatever the fused loss turns out to be.

On the replay branch the path is fixed, so a force needs a live terminal built from stored states. `cfg:replay_loss_coeffs.stored_force_k` re-propagates the last $k$ stored steps through the noise implied by the states themselves (`::GFN._implied_step` inverts $\varepsilon^\star = (x_{t+1}-x_t-\Delta t\mu_\theta)/(\sqrt{\Delta t}\sigma_\theta)$, detached, with the minimum-norm Woodbury split under DPLR), so `states[:, -1]` equals the stored terminal and carries $\mathrm{d}x_T/\mathrm{d}\theta$; the loss then adds $F\cdot(x_T - x_T.\mathrm{detach}())$ to $\log R$, again value zero and gradient $F$. $F$ is not recomputed at replay: `train.py::Modeller._admission_force_legs` calls `gflownet_losses.py::terminal_force_legs` at admission, which differentiates each of `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.REWARD_LEGS` (`flow`, `phys`, `bound`, captured under `::capture_reward_legs` and each divided by its row's temperature) at a leaf terminal and stores the three gradients in the buffer's `force_legs` column, `NaN` where none was recorded; `::remix_force_legs` recombines them at the draw's current $\lambda$. `stored_force_mode: 'mean'` replaces the implied tail with a fixed replay plus $F\cdot(\Delta t\,\mu_\theta(x_{T-1}) - \text{detach})$ from `::GFN.last_step_mean_shift`, and is restricted to $k=1$. `cfg:replay_loss_coeffs.resample_last_k`, mutually exclusive with `stored_force_k`, instead samples the last $k$ steps live from the stored $x_{T-k}$ and re-scores the reward on the fresh terminal by an energy call, with `replay_loss_coeffs.reward_grads` letting that reward's gradient reach the policy through the live tail.

## Accumulated-variance time and the terminal floor

`::GFN.init_var_schedule` parameterises the forward noise **rate** as $\sigma^2(t) = \sigma^2(0)\,\rho^{\,t^{p}}$ with $\rho$ = `t_scale_ratio` and $p$ = `t_scale_power`, and everything downstream runs on the accumulated variance $V(t)=\int_0^t\sigma^2$, built once as a cumulative trapezoid on a grid of `VAR_SCHEDULE_GRID` points and rescaled so $V(1) =$ `t_scale` when `t_scale_preserve_budget`. At a constant rate $V(t) = $ `t_scale`$\cdot t$, so $t$ and $V$ are proportional and `::GFN.var_log_rate`, `::GFN.var_drift_coeff`, `::GFN.var_bridge_step` short-circuit to $\log$`t_scale`, $\Delta t/t_{\text{next}}$ and $\Delta t\,t_{\text{prev}}/t_{\text{next}}$. `var_log_rate` returns the log of the step's **mean** rate $\Delta V/\Delta t$, so the variance a caller forms as $e^{\text{logvar}}\Delta t$ is exactly $\Delta V$; the terminal step's allocation is therefore $V(1)-V(1-1/T)$, an average over the final step rather than the endpoint rate $\sigma^2(1)$, and the two diverge for a steep schedule. With $x_0$ and the drift deterministic, $V(1)$ is the entire stock of randomness available to build the terminal distribution. The policy scales that allocation only within its own bound: `::GFN.split_params` adds $\tanh(\cdot/\texttt{log\_var\_range})\cdot\texttt{log\_var\_range}$ to the base before clipping at $\pm$`var_clip`, so the smallest terminal per-dimension standard deviation the kernel can express is $\sqrt{\Delta V_{\text{last}}\,e^{-\texttt{log\_var\_range}}}$, which at the canonical `cfg:model.t_scale` 0.05, `cfg:model.log_var_range` 6 and a uniform grid over `cfg:integrator.T` steps is $\sqrt{0.05/T}\,e^{-3}$. No mode narrower than that is resolvable by the last step. `mk_dev.yaml` carries none of the three schedule keys, so the code defaults apply and the schedule is off (`t_scale_ratio` default `None`); `checkpointing.py::Checkpointer.RECONFIGURABLE_GFN_KEYS` re-derives them from the current config rather than inheriting them from a checkpoint.

## Higher-order integration

Trajectory balance has no discretisation bias to remove. The residual is written in the log-densities of the discrete kernel the policy itself defines, and $P_F$ is learned, so a coarse $T$ is a less expressive family rather than a biased approximation of a continuous-time limit; there is no $O(\Delta t)$ term in the objective for a better integrator to cancel. The reference side is exact too: for a process whose increments have variances $\Delta V_t$, the bridge pinned at the origin gives $x_{t}\mid x_{t+1}$ mean $x_{t+1}V_t/V_{t+1}$ and variance $(V_{t+1}-V_t)V_t/V_{t+1}$, which is what `var_drift_coeff` and `var_bridge_step` compute when `back_mean_correction` and `back_var_correction` sit at unity.

A higher-order scheme also changes the object the objective scores. The residual requires a closed-form $\log P_F(x_{t+1}\mid x_t)$, and `fwd_gauss_logprob` supplies it because the step is affine in one Gaussian draw. A second drift evaluation at a predicted state, or a Milstein-type correction quadratic in the same draw, makes $x_{t+1}$ a nonlinear and generally non-invertible function of the noise: the transition law is no longer the Gaussian being scored, and $\log P_F$ would have to carry a change-of-variables Jacobian where the map is invertible, or a marginalisation over the intermediate draw where it is not. The backward kernel moves with it, the bridge above being the exact reversal of the first-order reference process and of no other.
