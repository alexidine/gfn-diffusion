# Per-condition evaluation

*Drift: **C** (code-bound). Verified against commit `0d0a8d99` plus uncommitted working-tree changes to `train.py`, `utils.py`, `buffer.py`, `gflownet_losses.py`, `energies/molecular_crystal.py`, `eval/cond_panel/relax.py`, `eval/cond_panel/report.py` and `eval/cond_panel/sampler.py`, and the untracked `eval/cond_panel/csp.py`, `tests/crystal/test_csp_replay.py` and `data_processing/pool_anchors.py`, 2026-10-05. Sources at the end.*

The modules under `eval/cond_panel/` evaluate one checkpoint of a conditional crystal run offline and per molecule: they rebuild the policy and the reward from a checkpoint file, draw through the trainer's own sampling function, and write tables and figures from those draws. This page is about that suite: what each module rebuilds, draws and measures. Five names recur. `log_w` is $\log R + \log P_B - \log P_F$ of one forward draw. The stored tracker is the checkpoint's `condition_log_z` entry, the saved state of `buffer.py::ConditionLogZTracker`. The conditioner output is the vector `models/gfn.py::GFN.get_condition_embedding` returns for a condition. The head is the learned log Z of a condition, the scalar `models/gfn.py::GFN._condition_flow` returns for that vector. A raw unit is the unit of the energy function's output; the temperature `cfg:energy_config.temperature` is in raw units, and an energy difference divided by it is in kT. The held-out stream and the per-condition metrics logged during training are on [conditional-route](conditional-route.md); the condition, prior and held-out files are on [molecule-conditions-and-anchors](molecule-conditions-and-anchors.md); the latent rows are on [latent-dimension-structure](latent-dimension-structure.md).

## Rebuilding a run from a checkpoint

`eval/cond_panel/sampler.py::load_run` takes a checkpoint file and the run's YAML and returns a `::Run`. It reads `problem_def`, `gfn_config`, `model_eval` (or `model_train` when the checkpoint has no `model_eval`), `pb_frozen`, `condition_log_z`, `energy_reference` and `modeller_state` from the checkpoint, and raises when the energy function, space groups, Z' list, embedding-conditioning flag or temperature of `problem_def` differ from the YAML. The device stored in `gfn_config` is overwritten before the policy is constructed. The policy is loaded from `model_eval` when the checkpoint carries it (a ConformerModeller's, or one written before 2026-10-28) and from `model_train` otherwise; when the checkpoint carries `pb_frozen`, `models/gfn.py::GFN.freeze_backward_policy` installs it, so every log P_B is scored on the snapshot. The reward is an `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal` built from the YAML's `energy_config`.

The conditions and held-out files named by `cfg:molecules_path` and `cfg:test_molecules_path` are read through `train.py::Modeller._load_condition_file`. The identifier registry is the rank of each identifier in the sorted union over the conditions file, the prior file named by `cfg:prior_path` and the held-out file, as `train.py::Modeller.init_identifiers` builds it, and `load_run` raises when its size differs from the number of slots in the stored tracker. When the energy function was built with `cfg:energy_config.energy_reference`, `load_run` installs the table the checkpoint stores through `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.set_energy_reference` and raises when the stored mode differs; it does not recompute the table.

`sampler.py::draw` makes one forward draw per conditions row: `mxtaltools/dataset_utils/data_class_methods/mol_methods.py::MolDataMethods.orient_molecule` in mode `standard`, then `eval/utils.py::sample_eval_fwd_trajs` on a uniform grid of the checkpoint's `train_T` steps, or `cfg:eval_T` for a checkpoint without one (`sampler.py::load_run`, `utils.py::uniform_discretizer`) at the temperature `cfg:energy_config.temperature`. The mode names `standard`, `std` and `standardized` select one branch of `orient_molecule`. `draw` returns the summed log P_F and log P_B, log R, `log_w`, the head (`sampler.py::head_log_z`), the condition id, the raw terminal state, the scored crystal batch and the per-sample energy fields named in `sampler.py::SAMPLE_FIELDS`. `sampler.py::pooled_levels` reduces a set of `log_w` to its mean (`jensen_z`), its log-mean-exp, the standard error of the mean and the effective-sample fraction.

## The reproduction gate

`eval/cond_panel/harness_check.py::main` draws an eval-shaped batch, rows uniform with replacement over each conditions file at `cfg:eval_num_samples` and `cfg:test_eval_num_samples`, and sets four pooled readings beside the values the run logged within `--window` steps of the checkpoint's step, read through `analysis/pull.py::pull`: `jensen_z` against `eval_fwd/jensen_z` and `eval_test/jensen_z`, and the fraction passing `sampler.py::reasonable_mask`, which calls `train.py::Modeller._reasonable_sample_mask`, against `Reasonable Sample Fraction` and `eval_test/Reasonable Sample Fraction`. A reading passes when it lies inside the logged range widened by two of its own standard errors. The log-mean-exp is printed and not gated. The process exits 1 when any gated reading fails.

## Training against held-out, on one design

`eval/cond_panel/topline.py::main` draws the same number of molecules from each file (the held-out set's size unless `--conditions` is given) and `--draws` draws per molecule. `topline.py::stream_metrics` passes them to `utils.py::quick_tb_stats` at `cfg:conditional_worst_quantile`, with residuals centred by `topline.py::tracker_centre`. When the active stage's forward coefficients carry `tb_z_source: persistent`, the centre is `gflownet_losses.py::tb_z_per_traj` on the stored tracker's `ema_logw` where a condition's `count` reaches the stored tracker's `min_visits` (`cfg:condition_log_z.min_visits` when the tracker stores none), and elsewhere the value `topline.py::untrusted_fallback` reads off the stored tracker: `global_logw` when its `untrusted_z`, the run's `cfg:condition_log_z.untrusted_z`, is `global` and that level is finite, otherwise the head. Under any other `tb_z_source` the centre is the head. `stream_metrics` passes neither `reward_floor` nor `ramp_width`, the arguments `train.py::Modeller._reward_ramp_kwargs` supplies in training, so `quick_tb_stats` computes `under_coverage` with every draw weighted equally.

The excess of a draw of condition $c$ is $(E_{\text{seed}} - E_{\text{ref}}(c)) / T$, computed by `topline.py::excess_energy`. $E_{\text{seed}}$ is `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.seed_energy_from` of the draw: the crystal energy without the Jacobian term, the reduction and bounding penalties or any clip. $E_{\text{ref}}(c)$ is `::MolecularCrystal.energy_reference_for`, the per-condition constant that `cfg:energy_config.energy_reference` installs, described on [reward-construction](reward-construction.md). The function returns `None` on a run without an energy reference and is applied the same way to both streams. `stream_metrics` reports the median, mean and 90th percentile of the excess, the share of draws below zero, and the share above a bar of `data_ndim` times `cfg:nonthermal_entropy_per_dim`, read as 4.0 when the key is absent. Beside these it reports the median of $\max\!\big(0,\ (-T \log R - E_{\text{best}}(c)) / T\big)$ over the draws whose condition has a finite $E_{\text{best}}(c)$, the stored tracker's `best_energy`. `jensen_z` is also split into the mean log R and the mean of log P_B minus log P_F, and the reasonable flag of `sampler.py::reasonable_mask` is reduced per condition by `utils.py::per_condition_fraction` at `cfg:reasonable_cond_bar`.

## Single molecules

`eval/cond_panel/select_panel.py::main` proposes molecules for ten slots from RDKit descriptors of each identifier (`::graph_descriptors`), shape descriptors of the stored geometry (`::shape_descriptors`) and the conditioner output (`::conditioner_coords`): four training slots by rule, one isosteric training pair (one heavy-atom skeleton with elements and bond orders erased, both rigid, N plus O count differing by at least two, hydrogen count within two, every principal extent within 10%), and four held-out slots at fixed quantiles `::HELD_OUT_QUANTILES` of the distance to the nearest training molecule in conditioner output. `eval/cond_panel/panel_dists.py::page` plots one molecule's draws against its reference rows on two bases, lattice energy against packing coefficient and the twelve latent marginals; a reference row enters the latent panels only when `::niggli_penalty` of its cell is at most $10^{-6}$.

## The atlas

`eval/cond_panel/atlas.py::select` takes `--n-train` training and `--n-test` held-out molecules at random under `--seed` and adds, for each, its nearest training molecule in conditioner output (`::nearest_training`, which masks a training row's own column by index). `::molecule_table` stores, per molecule, the descriptors above, the flags of `::FUNCTIONAL_GROUPS` and `::RING_FLAGS` (`::group_flags`), the encoder embedding and the conditioner output, the head's log Z, the energy reference, the tracker fields in `::TRACKER_KEYS`, and `::reference_table` of its search minima: their count, the gap from the lowest to the second, the number within 5 kT of the lowest, their spread, and the lowest one's packing coefficient and latents. `::draw_chunk` stores, per draw, the built crystal's latents (`mxtaltools/dataset_utils/data_class_methods/crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.latent_params`), log P_F, log P_B, log R, the lattice energy, the seed energy, the packing coefficient, the reduction penalty and the reasonable flag.

Draws are written one chunk of molecules at a time under `chunks/`, and a chunk already on disk is skipped. The selection is written to `selection.pt` before any draw and reloaded by a later invocation, which raises when its training count, held-out count, seed or step differ from the arguments. `atlas.py::assemble` joins the chunks and raises when a molecule of the table has no draws.

## Folded latents and distances between two sets of draws

`eval/cond_panel/latents.py::fold` maps the centroid rows v and w onto $[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, a period of 1. The fold is written for space group P-1 at Z' = 1, where the asymmetric-unit box is $(\tfrac12, 1, 1)$ of the cell and a shift of the origin by half a cell along b or c is a latent shift of 1 in v or w; `fold` reads neither the space group nor Z' from the run. `::PERIOD` holds each row's period after folding (v and w 1, phi and r 2, the rest linear) and `::RANGE` the width a row can span. `::delta` is the coordinate-wise difference on folded latents, nearest image on periodic rows, in units of the row's range. `::circ_mean_spread` returns a centre and a spread per row: mean and standard deviation on linear rows, the circular mean and $\sqrt{-2\ln R}$ on periodic rows, $R$ being the resultant length. `::w1_per_dim` is the one-dimensional Wasserstein distance per row; on a periodic row `::_circular_w1` integrates $|F - G - m|$ over one period, $m$ the length-weighted median of the difference of the two empirical distribution functions. `::energy_distance` is the energy distance under the norm of `::delta`, with each set's self-distances left out of its within-set mean.

The row indices in `fold`, `PERIOD` and `latents.py::BLOCKS` are those of one asymmetric unit, a width of twelve.

## What reads the atlas

`eval/cond_panel/trends.py::load` derives, per molecule, the mean, median and lower-decile excess, the share of draws above the bar, the mean `log_w` and its two terms, and each row's centre and spread. `::reliability` is the odd-against-even split-half correlation of a per-molecule mean, stepped up to the full draw count. The figure functions in `::ALL` are:

- `::quality_by_feature`: mean excess in bins of heavy-atom count, rotatable bonds, ring count and asphericity.
- `::group_effects`: the coefficient of every functional-group and ring flag in one least-squares fit of mean excess that also carries heavy-atom count, rotatable bonds, ring count and asphericity.
- `::variance_explained`: out-of-fold $R^2$ of a ridge fit (`::cv_r2`, five folds) from cumulative descriptor sets, from the search-minima statistics, from the encoder embedding and from the conditioner output.
- `::neighbour_matched`: a molecule's value minus its nearest training molecule's, for held-out molecules and for training molecules.
- `::latent_feature_corr`, `::model_vs_reference`, `::latent_widths`: latent centres and spreads against descriptors, against the lowest search minimum, and by row.
- `::levels`: for each of the stored tracker's `fwd_level_ema` and `bwd_level_ema`, their difference, and the head, the root-mean-square difference between a molecule and its nearest training molecule divided by the root-mean-square difference over 20,000 random pairs of molecules; and `::knn_r2`, the leave-one-out $R^2$ of predicting a molecule's value from the mean over its ten nearest rows of conditioner output. The tracker quantities are taken over training molecules whose `count` is above zero.
- `::pairs_shallow`, `::condition_map`: the energy distance between two molecules' draws against their distance in conditioner output, and a UMAP of conditioner output coloured by molecule and model quantities.

`eval/cond_panel/pairs.py::choose_pairs` takes neighbour pairs from the atlas spread evenly over the neighbour distances, random training pairs, and `::isosteric_pairs`. For each pair of molecules A and B, `pairs.py::run_pairs` computes three quantities at `--draws` draws per molecule. The first is `latents.py::energy_distance` between the two sets of draws. The second is the swap penalty: `pairs.py::score_under` rebuilds A's terminals with molecule B through `energies/base_set.py::BaseSet.log_reward`, `pairs.py::seed_excess` measures the rebuilt crystals against B's energy reference, and the penalty is their median excess minus the median excess of B's own draws, averaged with the same quantity for B's terminals rebuilt with A. The third is `pairs.py::density_lift`, which calls `eval/xcond.py::cross_condition_delta` on the two conditions. For a terminal $x$ and a condition $c$ that function draws `--lift-k` backward trajectories $\tau_k$ from $P_B(\cdot \mid x, c)$ and returns $\log \frac{1}{K} \sum_k P_F(\tau_k \mid c) / P_B(\tau_k \mid x, c)$, an estimate of the log density the forward policy gives $x$ under $c$. The lift is that value for a molecule's terminals under its own condition minus under the other's, averaged over the terminals of both molecules.

`eval/cond_panel/motifs.py::template` reads one molecule's bonds from its stored geometry (two atoms closer than 1.3 times the sum of the covalent radii in `::COVALENT`), its N-H and O-H hydrogens, its N and O atoms, and its rings: cycles of five or six heavy atoms from a minimum cycle basis whose atoms each carry at most three bonds and lie within 0.06 angstrom rms of one plane. `motifs.py::motifs_of` rebuilds crystals through `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.instantiate_crystals`, expands them with `mxtaltools/dataset_utils/data_class_methods/crystal_building.py::MolCrystalBuilding.mol2cluster` at a cutoff of 6 angstrom and keeps the atoms whose `aux_ind` is at most 1. `motifs.py::describe` returns, per crystal, the number of molecules with an atom pair inside the van der Waals sum plus 0.3 angstrom, whether the closest of them is the inverse of the central molecule or a translate, the donor hydrogens within 2.75 angstrom of a neighbour's N or O at a D-H...A angle of at least 130 degrees, and whether a ring lies over a neighbour's ring with planes within 20 degrees, 3.0 to 4.2 angstrom apart and centroids slipped by at most 2.5 angstrom. Each reading is a threshold test; a crystal that meets none is recorded as having none.

## Other geometries of a stored molecule

`eval/cond_panel/conformers.py::rows_from` builds conditions rows for new coordinates of a stored molecule: a fresh molecule object per geometry whose radius is the farthest atom from the all-atom centroid, placed in the stored row's crystal slot, standardised with `orient_molecule` in mode `std`; it returns `None` when a second standardisation moves an atom by more than $10^{-3}$ angstrom. `conformers.py::encode` embeds molecules with the frozen encoder (`mxtaltools/models/task_models/autoencoder_models.py::Mo3ENet.encode`) after recentring on all atoms, and both callers embed the new geometries in one batch with stored molecules drawn from the conditions file, `--embed-batch` in all. The stored geometry is re-embedded beside them, and the script raises when the norm of its difference from the stored embedding exceeds 0.06 of the stored embedding's norm **[calibration, qm9full conditions files, 2026-10-05]**.

`conformers.py::other_conformers` skips a molecule with any element `rdkit.Chem.FindPotentialStereo` reports, maps the stored atoms onto the SMILES by a graph isomorphism on element and bond (`::atom_map`), relaxes a copy of the stored geometry with MMFF, embeds `--n-confs` ETKDG conformers and relaxes each, and returns the relaxed copy and, of the conformers within `--window` kcal/mol of the lowest, the one with the largest heavy-atom RMSD from that copy, the RMSD being the smaller of the fits to the copy and to its mirror image; it returns a reason string when none exceeds `--min-rmsd`. `::frame_rmsds` compares two geometries three ways, as they sit in the frame, after the best proper rotation and after the best rotation or reflection, and `::frame_state` names the result `kept`, `mirrored` or `turned` at a tolerance of 0.3 angstrom. `conformers.py::main` draws under the stored condition, a jittered geometry, the relaxed copy and the alternate conformer; `eval/cond_panel/jitter.py::main` draws under the stored condition and under Gaussian noise of each size in `--levels` for training and held-out molecules. Every new row carries its parent's `mol_id`, so its excess is measured against the parent's energy reference.

## Relaxing draws

`eval/cond_panel/relax.py::run` relaxes three kinds of start for `--n-train` training and `--n-test` held-out molecules taken at random under `--seed`: `--draws` draws from the checkpoint (`sampler.py::draw`); `--randoms` starts from the initialiser of the crystal search ([crystal-search-method](crystal-search-method.md)), `relax.py::random_starts`, which calls `mxtaltools/crystal_search/utils.py::get_initial_state` with the `init_sample_method`, `init_target_cp` and `init_reduced` of the search YAML given as `--search-config`; and the molecule's stored search minima, `relax.py::reference_rows`, its rows of the prior file named by `cfg:prior_path` for a training molecule and of the file given as `--test-prior` for a held-out one. All three go through `relax.py::relax`, which passes the stages returned by `relax.py::search_stages` to `mxtaltools/dataset_utils/data_class_methods/crystal_ops.py::MolCrystalOps.optimize_crystal_parameters` in order, each stage starting from the output of the one before. The stages are the `opt` list of the search YAML in its own order, or the entries at the indices given as `--stage-plan`; `search_stages` raises on a stage whose `optimizer_func` is not `rprop`.

`mxtaltools/crystal_search/crystal_opt_utils.py::gradient_descent_optimization` returns, per row, the parameters recorded at its lowest-loss step, and stops rows by its `convergence` rule: by default every row of a batch once more than 95% of its rows have met `convergence_eps` (`::check_convergence`), or under the opt-in `convergence='grad'` each row on its own gradient (`::row_gradient_blocks`). `search_stages` sets `convergence_eps` to 0, at which neither rule stops a row and every row takes every step of a stage, unless `--search-stopping` is given; with it, the stage's own `convergence_eps` applies. `relax.py::check_batching` relaxes 32 model draws and 32 random starts whole and in two halves and prints the largest difference in end energy and in cell; for the model draws it also prints the difference between the relaxer's energy before any step and `seed_energy_from` of the same draws.

A chunk of `--chunk-molecules` molecules is written to `relax_chunks/` and skipped when already on disk. It stores, per relaxation, the molecule's index, the per-row energy the last stage optimises (the field its `optim_target` names) and the packing coefficient before the first step and at the end, the cell lengths and angles before and after, and with `--keep-crystals` the relaxed crystal; per molecule, its energy reference; and per stored minimum, its stored energy, the `elj` field of its prior row divided by `z_prime`. Energies are in raw units.

`relax.py::collect` joins the chunks. It raises when a molecule's energy reference and its lowest stored energy differ by 0.1 raw units or more, when the relaxer's energy of a stored minimum before any step and its stored energy differ by that much, or when a relaxation ended at a non-finite energy. A molecule's floor is the lower of its energy reference and the lowest end energy among its relaxations of all three kinds. `relax.py::distinct_minima` groups end energies whose neighbours in sorted order lie within `::DISTINCT_TOL`. The height of a structure is its energy minus its molecule's floor, over the temperature. `relax.py::figures` reports, per split and kind of start, the median height before and after relaxation, the mean height after, and the share of ends at a height of at most 1, 3, 5 and 10. It reports the median and mean height and the same four shares for the random starts' ends with each end weighted by $e^{-h}$, $h$ its height, and the weights normalised within a molecule. It reports the exponent $\beta$ at which the random starts' ends, weighted by $e^{-\beta h}$ within each molecule, have the same mean height, averaged over molecules, as the relaxed model draws. It reports the mean over molecules, with its standard error, of the difference between model draws and random starts in a molecule's mean end height and in its share of ends at a height of at most 3. For model draws and for random starts it reports the number of groups of `distinct_minima` per relaxation, and the share of the ends of one that lie in a group the other also reached; the groups are formed over the ends of all three kinds.

`relax.py::check_converged` relaxes the kept ends in a chunk directory again by the stages of `--stage-plan` and prints the energy they lose. `relax.py::compare` reads two chunk directories written from the same starts under different stages and reports the two end heights of each start, the floor being the lower of the two directories' floors; it raises when the two hold different molecules or their starting cells differ by more than $10^{-3}$.

## A mock structure-prediction run

`eval/cond_panel/csp.py` measures what it costs a sampler to recover a molecule's low minima. It counts two budgets per molecule: draws taken from the sampler, and energy calls, one energy-and-force evaluation of one crystal, so that one relaxation step of one row is one call. A sampler is one of three things. A checkpoint (`--model NAME=CHECKPOINT`, repeatable) proposes its forward draws. With `--random`, the search initialiser of `relax.py::random_starts` proposes starts. With `--prior`, the unconditional prior proposes them: `collect` reads the latents of every row of the file at `cfg:prior_path`, draws rows whose identifier is not the molecule's, and builds crystals from those latents with the molecule through `sampler.py::crystals_from`. The random and prior samplers read their molecules from the last run loaded; with `--skip-models` no checkpoint is collected and the first `--model` checkpoint supplies that run.

`csp.py::collect` gives every sampler `--starts` starts for each molecule of `csp.py::molecules`, which returns the molecules `relax.py::run` takes under the same `--seed`. Every start is relaxed through `relax.py::relax` with every row taking every step, and one pool file is written per sampler and molecule: for each start its canonical latents (the 12 numbers `csp.py::canonical` returns for it, described in the next paragraph), the energy at every recorded step of its relaxation, and its end energy and packing coefficient; and the molecule's energy reference. A recorded step is one entry of the optimiser's record of a stage, one energy-and-force call of one crystal; `relax.py::relax` with `trajectory` returns the records of the stages in order. A pool file on disk is skipped. With `--cluster-from M`, `collect` instead draws M starts per molecule and makes no energy call on them. A checkpoint's terminal states come from `sampler.py::draw_latents`, and `crystals_from` builds them without scoring, through `energies/molecular_crystal.py::MolecularCrystal.instantiate_crystals` alone. `csp.py::density_modes` then finds the modes of the local density of the M draws in canonical latents. Its distance is the Euclidean distance between rows of `csp.py::embed`, which divides each latent by its range and places a periodic latent on a circle. A draw's density is the Gaussian kernel sum over all M draws, with standard deviation `::D_CUT` divided by 3. Every draw points at the densest draw closer than `D_CUT`, itself included; the pointers are followed to their fixed points, which are the modes, and a mode's mass is the number of draws that reach it. This is the mean shift of `eval/paper1_results/utils.py::mean_shift_density` with another distance. The modes of the `--starts` clusters of largest mass are built again through the reward function and relaxed, and the pool is stored as `<sampler>+modes<M>` with its starts in descending mass.

One crystal has many rows of 12 latents that build it: any basis of its lattice, and any of the origins the space group allows. `csp.py::canonical` takes a batch of crystals and returns one such row for each, the same row for every description of one crystal, for space group P-1 at Z' = 1. The rows are in the trainer's chart: the trainer builds a crystal from its latents with the stored molecule rotated to its standard frame (`orient_molecule`) and at handedness +1, whatever handedness the row stores (the pose of the asymmetric unit and its handedness are on [asymmetric-unit-reduction](asymmetric-unit-reduction.md)). `csp.py::reduced_latents` takes the change of lattice basis $N$ and the origin $o$ that give the reduced cell from `mxtaltools/crystal_search/standardize.py::standardize_cells`. For a row whose cell changes it carries the atoms of the trainer's asymmetric unit, read by `data_processing/pool_anchors.py::Chart.frac_atoms`, into the new basis by $x' = N^{-1}(x - o)$ and describes that molecule at handedness +1 with `::Chart.describe` and `::Chart.into_box`; a row whose cell is already reduced keeps its latents. `reduced_latents` does not use the latents of the batch `standardize_cells` returns. In P-1 each molecule has an inversion mate, its image through the cell's inversion centre, of the opposite handedness; `standardize_cells` puts the molecule back in its asymmetric unit, which can store the mate at handedness -1, and the trainer reads that row at +1. `latents.py::fold` then puts v and w on one period. `canonical` takes `trainer_chart`: `collect` passes `False` for starts from the random initialiser, whose rows are in the search's description, and a changed row then takes the standardised batch's own latents. `csp.py::check_fold` rebuilds draws through `energies/base_set.py::BaseSet.log_reward` from seven rows each: the latents of the built crystal, the standardised batch's latents, the row of `reduced_latents`, that row with the origin moved by half a cell along b, along c and along both (a shift of 1 in v, in w and in both, wrapped into the latent box), and the row of `canonical`. It prints, for each, the difference of its excess from the draw's own and the share of rows the trainer gives a reduction penalty above $10^{-6}$.

`csp.py::replay` reads the pool files and makes no energy call: what a strategy would have spent and found is read off the stored relaxations. A relaxation is settled when its best energy fell by no more than `::E_TOL` raw units over its last `::SETTLE_WINDOW` recorded steps (`csp.py::settled`). `csp.py::assign_minima` groups ends into minima: it takes the ends in ascending energy, and an end joins a group when its energy is within `E_TOL` of the group's first member and its packing coefficient within `::CP_TOL` of it, and otherwise opens a new group. `csp.py::known_minima` sets three things per molecule. Its known minima are the groups of `assign_minima` over the settled ends of every sampler collected for it; a relaxation that had not settled ends in no minimum. Its floor is the lowest of the energies of all its ends, settled or not, and its energy reference. Its target is the known minima within `--window` kT of the floor, and `replay` raises for a molecule whose target is empty. A strategy is a row of `csp.py::STRATEGIES`: a selection rule, the share of the starts it relaxes, and a cut. From the starts drawn, `csp.py::select` picks the starts whose stored relaxations are read: all of them; or that share of them with the lowest energy at the first recorded step, charged one energy call for every start drawn. `csp.py::stop_steps` applies one of three cuts to a stored relaxation: `full`, at its last recorded step; `plateau`, at the first step where the best energy so far has improved by less than `::PLATEAU_EPS` kT over the last `::PLATEAU_WINDOW` recorded steps; `oracle`, at the first step within `::CREDIT` kT of the end. The steps up to the cut are its energy calls. A relaxation is credited when its best energy at the cut is within `CREDIT` kT of its end, and its calls are counted either way. A target minimum is recovered when a credited relaxation ends in it, so a relaxation that had not settled recovers none, credited or not. `csp.py::spend` applies one strategy to the starts drawn and returns the energy calls spent, the starts credited and the lowest best energy at a cut. Its `cascade` selection reads every start: the first quarter of the starts, in the order drawn, is cut by the strategy's cut alone, and the lowest best energy at their cuts is the cascade's reference energy; at each step of `::CASCADE`, a later start whose best energy so far is more than that step's margin in kT above the reference energy is retired, charged the steps up to there and not credited. `CASCADE` holds steps and margins for the `early_stop` argument of `mxtaltools/crystal_search/crystal_opt_utils.py::gradient_descent_optimization`. For a pool collected start by start, that is without `--cluster-from`, `replay` evaluates each strategy on `--repeats` random subsets of the starts at each number of draws. A `+modes` pool is read in its stored order under the strategies that read every start, and every point of it is charged M draws.

`csp.py::figures` draws one curve per sampler in each panel. One panel for each strategy whose cut is `plateau`, and one for the strategy whose cut is `oracle`, show the mean over molecules of the share of the target recovered against energy calls. One panel shows that share against draws under the `full` cut of every start. Two panels read the floor under the `plateau` cut of every start, against energy calls: the share of molecules whose lowest best energy at the cut is within `CREDIT` kT of the floor, and the median over molecules of that energy in kT above the floor. Its table gives, per sampler and strategy, the recovered share at the numbers of energy calls in its tuple `budgets`, the mean energy calls of a relaxation under the strategy's cut, and the share of relaxations credited. Under the figure it prints two sets of lines. The first set compares two curves at three levels: a quarter of the target recovered, half of it recovered, and the floor within `CREDIT` kT in hand for half the molecules. The baseline sampler is `random` when it was collected and otherwise the last sampler in name order. Each other sampler is compared with the baseline sampler under the same strategy, and each strategy of the baseline sampler with its own `plateau` cut of every start. A line gives the energy calls of the two curves at a level, their ratio, and the 16th to 84th percentile of that ratio over molecules drawn again with replacement. The second set is the numbers of `csp.py::selection_bounds` for each pool collected start by start, with every relaxation under the `plateau` cut. They are: the share of relaxations that had not settled; the energy calls of all the starts; the number of minima recovered and how many of them are target minima; the share of starts that end in a target minimum; the energy calls of the cheapest credited start of each minimum recovered, summed; the same sum over the target minima recovered; the share of starts whose nearest other start by `embed` distance ends in the same minimum; and that share for any two starts. The first sum is what a selection would spend that knew, before any energy call, which starts end in the same minimum; the second, one that also knew which minima are in the target.
