# Classical molecular CSP search tools: search-space definition and use of normaliser / Cheshire redundancy

Scope note: about 17 tool calls. Many primary program papers (J Comput Chem, Acta Cryst A/B) returned HTTP 403 or no full text. Section 1 (GLEE) is read from the primary paper's open-access PMC full text. Most other entries rest on abstracts or secondary descriptions; each finding is labelled. Claims I recall but could not verify in this session are listed only under Gaps as leads to check. They are not findings.

## 1. GLEE (Case, Campbell, Bygrave & Day 2016): how is the search space bounded? Is the molecular position limited to the asymmetric unit or anything smaller?

### Takeaway
GLEE samples each independent molecule's centroid over the whole unit cell, with fractional coordinates in [0,1) on all three axes in every space group. It says explicitly that it keeps translations along all three lattice vectors even where the energy does not depend on them (polar / origin-free directions). It does not use any Cheshire, normaliser or asymmetric-unit restriction. Redundancy is removed after minimisation, by clustering on Niggli-reduced cells with a COMPACK-like overlay. The paper never mentions Cheshire groups, Euclidean normalisers or origin choice.

### Cited Findings (all from the primary paper, open-access full text on PMC: D. H. Case, J. E. Campbell, P. J. Bygrave, G. M. Day, "Convergence Properties of Crystal Structure Prediction by Quasi-Random Sampling", J. Chem. Theory Comput. 12, 910–924 (2016), DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01112)
- Bibliographic details and open-access status. The paper is also on Southampton ePrints. — [Southampton ePrints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/385478); [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Sampler.** Trial structures come from "quasi-random, low-discrepancy sequences generated by the Sobol method" (Sec. 2.1). Each independent molecule "requires three parameters to determine its position and three for its orientation". The cell adds up to 6 more dimensions: "X = 6 in the case of a triclinic cell, although fewer for lattices with restrictions". The dimension is therefore about 6·Z′ + (number of free cell parameters), e.g. 12 for triclinic Z′ = 1. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Position.** Each position number is "taken as a position in fractional coordinates along a particular cell axis", with x_i ∈ [0,1). The paper says: "We include translation along all three lattice vectors in all space groups". So translations along polar axes, which leave the energy unchanged, are still sampled. The full cell is built by applying the space-group operators to the asymmetric-unit molecule(s). — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Orientation.** Uses "the quaternion based Shoemake method" (Sec. 2.1.1). Shoemake's construction is the standard uniform-on-SO(3) map from three uniform variates, but the paper does not say "uniform". It does not discuss Euler angles or q/−q double cover. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Cell angles.** Each free angle is sampled "to give an even distribution in cos(θ_j)", inside bounds chosen so the program "attempts to only generate versions of structures without flat unit cells". The exact numerical bounds are in equation images the text extraction could not recover. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Cell lengths.** The target volume is the sum of molecular volumes × TVP. TVP defaults to 1.0; values of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 were tested. Molecular "volume" is an inertia-axis bounding box that includes van der Waals radii. The first two lengths are sampled over [c·s_min, c·N_mols·s_j] with c = 0.75. The third length comes from the target volume, with Gaussian scatter (s.d. 0.15 × s_min). "We also cycle through the permutations of possible orderings in which the cell lengths could be assigned." Length ordering is therefore explicitly not reduced at generation; it is permuted instead. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Overlap handling.** Convex hulls with a separating-axis-theorem overlap test. A structure that overlaps is either rejected or has its lattice vectors expanded iteratively, up to a maximum volume of 2.5 × the summed molecular volume. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Space groups and Z′.** "Currently, 95 space groups are available to be searched in the Glee program". Production searches used a subset (e.g. P1, P2₁, C2, P2₁2₁2₁, R3, P2₁/c, Pbca, Pnma). Z′ = 1 for most systems, Z′ = 2 for quinacridone in P−1, and Z′ = 4 for artemisinin in P1. Molecular point symmetry is handled by choosing the space group. The search covers "space groups of the observed structures after removing space group symmetry elements that correspond to intramolecular symmetry". For example, CC1 in R3 was "generated in the P1 space group, which reduces to R3 on account of intramolecular symmetry". — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Cell reduction happens only after the search.** "we make use of the Niggli reduced cell representation of each crystal structure" in clustering (Sec. 2.3), and all structures were converted to reduced cells before comparison. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Duplicate removal after minimisation.** Clustering is "related to the Compack approach". It compares 25-molecule clusters with a rotation-only RMSD overlay at tolerance 0.5 + 0.05·r Å, plus centroid-distance agreement within 20%. Only convex-hull atoms are compared, with hydrogens removed. Up to S overlays are tried, where S is the molecular symmetry order, so molecular point symmetry is quotiented at comparison, not in the sampler. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Convergence.** Judged by monitoring the count of unique minima within 15 kJ/mol of the global minimum, and the stability of the lowest-energy minimum and the mean of the ten lowest. Searches are extended "starting from the highest value of Sobol seed that has previously been used". — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085)
- **Not in the paper.** The text does not discuss Cheshire cells, Euclidean normalisers, origin shifts, alternative settings, or removing redundancy at generation. — [PMC4750085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750085) (my reading of the full text through ~100k characters via WebFetch; the last ~20k characters, mostly references and conclusions, were not read)

### Inferences
- GLEE's sampled domain is at least [0,1)³ per molecule × SO(3) × the cell box. That is larger than the Cheshire-cell × orientation-fundamental-zone domain by roughly the index [N_E(G):G] × |molecular point group| × the q/−q factor, plus the multiplicity of the Z general-position copies. In polar groups (P2₁, P1, C2, Pna2₁, ...) whole continuous translational directions are sampled even though the energy does not depend on them. The authors state this choice but do not quantify its cost.
- The general pattern in GLEE is to sample generously and then quotient after minimisation: Niggli for the lattice, overlay with S symmetry trials for molecular symmetry, and cluster matching for origin and setting equivalences.

### Gaps
- The exact angle bounds (Eq. 1) and length-range equations (Eq. 2) were images and were not recovered.
- Whether later GLEE versions (e.g. the 7th blind test, or Day-group CSP after 2016) restrict positions to an asymmetric unit or Cheshire cell was not checked.

## 2. CrystalPredictor I/II (Karamertzanis & Pantelides 2005; Habgood et al. 2015; Sugden et al. 2016)

### Takeaway
CrystalPredictor runs a large number of gradient-based local minimisations from Sobol-generated starting points. The decision variables are the cell parameters, the positions and orientations of the molecules in the asymmetric unit, and (in CP II) torsions. The papers I could reach do not say how positions and orientations are bounded, or whether origin, normaliser or Niggli redundancy is handled at generation. I found no mention of Cheshire groups.

### Cited Findings
- Karamertzanis & Pantelides, "Ab initio crystal structure prediction—I. Rigid molecules", J. Comput. Chem. 26, 304–324 (2005), DOI 10.1002/jcc.20165. From the abstract (secondary record): global minimisation of lattice enthalpy; a parallel implementation that allows less common space groups and Z′ > 1. — [Zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jcc.20165); [catalogue record](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/NLM152844449/Description)
- Habgood, Sugden, Kazantsev, Adjiman & Pantelides, "Efficient handling of molecular flexibility in ab initio generation of crystal structures", J. Chem. Theory Comput. 11(4), 1957–1969 (2015). From the abstract: CrystalPredictor "performs an extensive exploration of the lattice energy surface by using low-discrepancy Sobol sequences". Flexibility is handled through QM-built local approximate models (LAMs). — [Imperial Spiral](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/fac960aa-d7b4-4744-8a22-4e410f1b9d1a)
- Sugden, Adjiman & Pantelides, Acta Cryst. B (2016), adaptive LAMs in CrystalPredictor II (PMC5134761; primary, read in part). Decision variables are "the positions and orientations of the molecules in the asymmetric unit", the cell parameters X, and independent torsions. Torsion domains are bounded by intramolecular energy of about 20–30 kJ/mol (e.g. T1, T7 ∈ [0, 360]°; T3, T5 ∈ [20, 260]°). Starting points come from Sobol, "a low-discrepancy technique that ensures the best coverage of the space of the variables". 1,000,000 candidates were used for molecule XXVI. Bounds on cell, position and orientation, the space-group list, and the duplicate-removal method are not given in the portion read. — [PMC5134761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5134761)

### Inferences
- "Positions … in the asymmetric unit" means the molecules are the asymmetric-unit contents. It does not mean the position is restricted to the asymmetric-unit region of the cell. The bounds themselves remain unverified.

### Gaps
- Unverified lead from memory: CrystalPredictor searches a fixed list of the most common space groups (I recall "59", which the search did not confirm), with fractional positions in [0,1) and Euler-type orientation angles. Check the 2005 J Comput Chem paper, Sec. 2–3, and the Habgood 2015 JCTC methods (core.ac.uk/download/77002225.pdf returned 403).

## 3. MOLPAK (Holden, Du & Ammon 1993)

### Takeaway
MOLPAK is a systematic grid search. A rigid probe molecule is placed in predefined coordination-geometry "packing types" (labelled AA, AB, AC, BA, BB, ...), all unique orientations of the central molecule are scanned, and the minimum-volume contact cell is found with a repulsion-only potential. The lowest-volume packings then go to WMIN or DMAREL/DMACRYS minimisation. Reduced cells are computed afterwards. The grid resolution and space-group list could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Holden, Du & Ammon, "Prediction of possible crystal structures for C-, H-, N-, O- and F-containing organic compounds", J. Comput. Chem. 14, 422–437 (1993), DOI 10.1002/jcc.540140406. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002%2FJCC.540140406)
- Method, from secondary search-result descriptions: coordination-sphere patterns were derived from 242 crystal structures. The search "investigates all unique orientations of a central molecule", builds the coordination pattern around it, and produces a 3-D map of minimum unit-cell volume using a repulsion-only potential. The 5–10 smallest-volume packings are minimised (WMIN). — [UCL CPOSS MOLPAK instructions](https://www.chem.ucl.ac.uk/cposs/computational/MOLPAKinstr.html) (secondary, via search snippet)
- UCL CPOSS operating notes (primary documentation of the Price-group workflow): runs are organised per packing type ("When running, runjobs will create a subdirectory for each packing type (AA, AB etc )"). Reduced cells are computed after the search with nbslattice and Platon, and the .nbs reduced cells "conserve the symmetry of the given packing type". — [UCL CPOSS MOLPAK instructions](https://www.chem.ucl.ac.uk/cposs/computational/MOLPAKinstr.html)

### Inferences
- "All unique orientations" suggests MOLPAK reduces the orientation grid at least partly by the packing-type symmetry. That is a hand-built quotient per coordination geometry rather than a general normaliser construction. This is unverified without the primary paper.

### Gaps
- Unverified lead from memory: three orientation angles on a 10° grid; about 29–30 coordination geometries across about 13 common space groups; the central molecule fixed at the origin or on a symmetry element. All need the J Comput Chem 1993 full text.

## 4. Polymorph Predictor (Karfunkel & Gdanitz 1992; Verwer & Leusen 1998), GRACE (Neumann et al.)

### Takeaway
Polymorph Predictor uses Monte Carlo simulated annealing over cell parameters, positions and orientations, then clustering, then full minimisation. GRACE (Neumann, Kendrick, Leusen) used Monte Carlo parallel tempering for generation in the 6th blind test. I found no description of how either bounds or quotients the search space.

### Cited Findings
- Karfunkel & Gdanitz, "Ab initio prediction of possible crystal structures for general organic molecules", J. Comput. Chem. 13, 1155–1162 (1992), DOI 10.1002/jcc.540131002. From the abstract: the search for the global and local minima within an energy window "is derived from Monte Carlo simulated annealing methods", without assuming known unit-cell parameters. — [Zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jcc.540131002)
- Verwer & Leusen, "Computer simulation to predict possible crystal polymorphs", Rev. Comput. Chem. 12, 327–365 (1998). Cited as the standard review. The workflow is MC simulated annealing, then clustering, then minimisation of all degrees of freedom. — (secondary description in search results; full text not accessed) [Thieme record](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-2002-19742)
- 6th blind test: Neumann, Kendrick & Leusen (Submission 14) used "MC parallel tempering" for generation, with DFT ranking. — [Reilly et al. 2016, PMC4971545](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4971545)

### Gaps
- No primary access to how Polymorph Predictor or GRACE sample positions and orientations, which space groups they use, or whether clustering uses reduced cells.

## 5. UPACK (van Eijck & Kroon), Dzyabchenko, other grid searches

### Takeaway
UPACK began as a systematic grid search (Z′ = 1; e.g. benzene in 31 space groups) and moved to random search plus clustering for Z′ > 1. The authors reported random search to be about as efficient as the grid for Z′ = 1. Grid searches were still in use in 2014–16 (Dzyabchenko; Obata & Goto; Podeszwa group). I found no evidence that any of them restricts positions by the normaliser.

### Cited Findings
- van Eijck, Spek, Mooij & Kroon, Acta Cryst. B (1998), hypothetical benzene structures: a systematic grid search with Z′ = 1 over 31 space groups, followed by clustering and a stability check with symmetry removed. — (secondary description in search results) [search record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/880733412)
- van Eijck & Kroon, "Structure predictions allowing more than one molecule in the asymmetric unit": a random search ("essential parameters take random values"), then minimisation and clustering. On hexapyranoses it was "roughly as efficient" as the earlier systematic grid method for one independent molecule. — (secondary abstract) [search record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/906669334)
- van Eijck, Mooij & Kroon, J. Comput. Chem. (2001): Z and Z′ restrict which space groups are possible, which reduces the search. — (secondary) [ScienceGate record](https://next.sciencegate.app/document/10.1002/jcc.1046)
- 6th blind test, generation method per submission (Table 2):
  - grid search: Dzyabchenko (4), Obata & Goto (9), Jankiewicz/Podeszwa (17);
  - random search: van Eijck (5), Hofmann & Kuleshova/FlexCryst (10), and others;
  - quasi-random Sobol': Day (3), Sugden/Gatsiou (15), Price (18), Metz/Szalewicz (19);
  - MC simulated annealing: Chadha & Singh (1), Mohamed (13).

  Most submissions "imposed crystallographic symmetry" in space groups "typically chosen on the basis of frequencies of occurrence in the CSD". — [Reilly et al., Acta Cryst. B 72, 439–459 (2016), DOI 10.1107/S2052520616007447, PMC4971545](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4971545)
- The 6th blind-test main text does not mention Cheshire, normaliser, origin, Niggli, reduced cell, asymmetric-unit restriction of positions, or orientation sampling details (quaternion or uniform). Duplicate removal is described generically: packing similarity, powder pattern, fingerprints, RDFs. — [PMC4971545](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4971545) (whole text checked through two WebFetch passes; per-team details are in SI gp5080sup3.zip, which was not read)

### Gaps
- Not reached: MDCP (Della Valle, Venuti, Brillante), PROMET / Zip-Promet (Gavezzotti 1991, JACS 113, 4622), CRYSCA (Schmidt & Englert), MPA/ICE9, FlexCryst internals, Tsuzuki/Sorescu, Price 2014 Chem Soc Rev, the 7th blind test reports, Beran/Day reviews, and Gavezzotti's "Are crystal structures predictable?" (Acc. Chem. Res. 1994).
- Unverified lead from memory: PROMET builds two-molecule "nuclei" related by a single symmetry operator (inversion, 2₁, glide or translation) and then completes the lattice, with a coarse orientation grid.
- Unverified lead: Dzyabchenko's PMC grid program and his reduced-cell similarity method (Acta Cryst. B 50, 414, 1994) may be the closest classical treatment of lattice and setting equivalence. Not checked.

## 6. Who uses Cheshire groups / Euclidean normalisers in trial-structure generation, and under what terminology?

### Takeaway
The clearest classical source is Hirshfeld (1968), "Symmetry in the generation of trial structures". He coined "Cheshire groups" (now called Euclidean normalisers) specifically to describe the symmetry of the six-dimensional rotation–translation space of trial-structure parameters, as the direct product of the Cheshire group with the molecular point group. That is essentially the requester's "Cheshire × orientation fundamental zone" domain. The mainstream CSP programs above, as checked, do not use this. They sample redundantly and deduplicate afterwards (COMPACK-like overlay, Niggli-reduced clustering).

### Cited Findings
- Hirshfeld, F. L., "Symmetry in the generation of trial structures", Acta Cryst. A24, 301–311 (1968), DOI 10.1107/S0567739468000501. — [Scilit record](https://www.scilit.net/publications/e7c1a4bfa847690ff23f48dd8cf76c5d)
- Abstract-level description (secondary). The transformations of the crystal axes that leave the coordinates of equivalent positions invariant define a derived symmetry that conforms to "one of thirty distinct 'Cheshire' groups". The direct product of this group with the molecular point group gives the symmetry of the six-dimensional space of trial-structure parameters. — [synapsesocial record](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W1987710456)
- CCP4 documentation. The table of Cheshire cells for all 230 space groups is given in Hirshfeld (1968). The Cheshire group is the space group left when the material contents are removed and only the symmetry elements remain. CCP4 uses Cheshire cells to bound translation searches in molecular replacement. — [CCP4 Cheshire cells](https://smb.slac.stanford.edu/facilities/software/ccp4/html-6.1.3/cheshirecell.html)
- IUCr terminology. The Euclidean normaliser is the group of motions that maps the space group's pattern of symmetry elements onto itself. The origin is free along a line (crystal classes 2, 3, 4, 6, mm2, 3m, 4mm, 6mm), in a plane (m), or in all of space (1). — [IUCr Online Dictionary: Normalizer](http://reference.iucr.org/dictionary/Normalizer); [ITA ch. 3.5](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/iucr/itc/Ac/ch3o5v0001/sec3o5o1o2/)
- The number of equivalent descriptions of a structure equals the index of G in N_E(G). — [IUCr paper on equivalence of point configurations due to Euclidean normalizers (Cheshire groups)](https://journals.iucr.org/paper?cnor=a22053&buy=yes) (abstract-level)
- Recent ML CSP work (a 2026 arXiv preprint) uses a "Euclidean-normalizer origin shift" as a training augmentation, so the model assigns the same Wyckoff labels to all equivalent descriptions. This is a cross-reference outside classical CSP. — [arXiv 2606.22866](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22866) (snippet only, not read)

### Inferences
- The idea the requester is building has a direct 1968 precedent in the packing and trial-structure literature (Hirshfeld). It was taken up mainly in macromolecular molecular replacement (CCP4 Cheshire cells for translation and rotation searches). It does not appear in the sampling stage of the organic CSP programs checked here. GLEE, the best-documented modern quasi-random code, explicitly samples the full [0,1)³ translations and permutes cell-length orderings rather than reducing them.
- Terminology to use when searching further: "Cheshire group / Cheshire cell" (Hirshfeld; CCP4), "Euclidean normalizer" (ITA Vol. A ch. 3.5; Koch & Fischer), "permissible origins" or "origin-free directions" (older direct-methods literature), and "reduced cell" / "Niggli" (Dzyabchenko; Gelder; COMPACK).

### Gaps
- The Hirshfeld 1968 full text was not read. Whether it also constructs the asymmetric unit of the 6-D Cheshire × point-group space explicitly, and whether any CSP code cites it, is unverified. A Google Scholar "cited by" check on DOI 10.1107/S0567739468000501 would answer the second question.
- COMPACK (Chisholm & Motherwell, J. Appl. Cryst. 38, 228–231, 2005) is the canonical post-hoc deduplicator, but its primary text was not fetched this session.
- No source checked describes Niggli reduction applied during the search in a classical organic CSP code. GLEE applies it only at clustering, and the MOLPAK/CPOSS workflow only after the search. This is absence of evidence across a limited sample, not a confirmed negative.
