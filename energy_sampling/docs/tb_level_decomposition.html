Technical note · trajectory-balance samplers

The Level Decomposition

What the forward and backward log-partition estimates actually measure — and why their disagreement is not a convergence criterion.

Training a GFlowNet with the trajectory-balance objective gives two quantities that both look like estimates of logZ: one from on-policy rollouts, one from backward rollouts out of a buffer. They disagree. This note shows the disagreement is exactly a signed combination of three Kullback–Leibler divergences, and draws out the consequences — the sharpest being that the gap can be closed by making the buffer worse. The only tools are Jensen’s inequality and the chain rule for KL.

01Setup

Write τ=(s0→⋯→sT) for a complete trajectory and x=x(τ):=sT for its terminal state. Given a strictly positive reward R(x) we want to sample the Boltzmann target

π(x):=R(x)Z⋆,Z⋆:=∑xR(x).

The model is a forward policy PF(τ) over complete trajectories, a backward policy PB(τ∣x) normalised over the trajectories ending at x, and a learned scalar Z. We write PF⊤(x):=∑τ→xPF(τ) for the terminal marginal — the distribution actually sampled.

One notational convention does all the work

For any distribution q over terminal states, let qPB denote the distribution over trajectories given by drawing x~q then τ~PB(·∣x):

(qPB)(τ):=q(x(τ))PB(τ∣x(τ)).

This is normalised, since PB(·∣x) sums to one for each x. In particular πPB is a perfectly ordinary distribution over trajectories — and it is the object the forward policy is really trying to match. Every divergence below is between trajectory distributions.

Trajectory balance and the importance weight

TB seeks parameters satisfying

ZPF(τ)=R(x(τ))PB(τ∣x(τ))for every τ.(1)

Summing (1) over trajectories ending at a fixed x gives ZPF⊤(x)=R(x); summing that over x forces Z=Z⋆ and PF⊤=π. So (1) says exactly “the sampler is correct and Z is the true partition function”. Define

logw(τ):=logR(x)+logPB(τ∣x)−logPF(τ).(2)

Comparing with (1): TB holds if and only if logw(τ)=logZ for every τ. So logw is constant at the optimum, and both its level and its spread diagnose TB violation. This note is about the level.

02Two facts, used twice each

Lemma 1 · Importance-sampling identity
𝔼PF[w]=Z⋆.
Proof
𝔼PF[w]=∑τPF(τ)R(x)PB(τ∣x)PF(τ)=∑xR(x)∑τ→xPB(τ∣x)=∑xR(x)=Z⋆.

Note this holds for the forward sampling distribution specifically. It is not true for an arbitrary sampler, and that asymmetry is the source of everything below.

Lemma 2 · KL chain rule with a shared kernel
For distributions q,p over terminal states, DKL(qPB‖pPB)=DKL(q‖p).
Proof
DKL(qPB‖pPB)=∑τq(x)PB(τ∣x)logq(x)PB(τ∣x)p(x)PB(τ∣x)=∑xq(x)logq(x)p(x),

since the PB factors cancel inside the logarithm and each sums to one.

03Two levels, three divergences

In training we evaluate logw under two different distributions. Let μ be the distribution over terminal states held in the buffer, and set

JF:=𝔼PF[logw](forward level),JB:=𝔼μPB[logw](backward level).

JF is the mean of logw over on-policy rollouts; JB the same average over buffer draws with backward rollouts. Three non-negative quantities govern them.

A
DKL(PF‖πPB)
How far the policy is from the target.
Mode-seeking · forward data
B
DKL(μPB‖PF)
How badly the policy fails to cover the buffer.
Mass-covering · buffer data
C
DKL(μ‖π)
How far the buffer is from the target.
Data quality · no gradient

The distinction between A and B is which argument the model sits in: A is the reverse KL (mode-seeking), B the forward KL (mass-covering). C involves no model at all — it is a property of the data.

Lemma 3 · Forward level
JF=logZ⋆−A. In particular JF≤logZ⋆ always.
Proof
JF=𝔼PF[logZ⋆π(x)PB(τ∣x)PF(τ)]=logZ⋆+𝔼PF[log(πPB)(τ)PF(τ)]=logZ⋆−A.

This is the usual ELBO statement in disguise: by Lemma 1 and Jensen, JF≤log𝔼PF[w]=logZ⋆, and Lemma 3 identifies the gap exactly as A.

Lemma 4 · Backward level
JB=logZ⋆−C+B.
Proof

Write Q:=μPB. As before JB=logZ⋆+𝔼Q[log(πPB/PF)]. Insert Q inside the logarithm:

𝔼Q[logπPBPF]=−DKL(Q‖πPB)+DKL(Q‖PF).

The second term is B by definition; the first is DKL(μPB‖πPB)=DKL(μ‖π)=C by Lemma 2.

Remark · the backward level is not a lower bound
Unlike JF, the backward level can exceed logZ⋆ — by Lemma 4 it does so whenever B>C. Lemma 1 only guarantees unbiasedness of w under PF. Treating the two levels as interchangeable estimates of logZ is a common and consequential slip.

04The master identity

Δ:=JB−JF=A+B−C

Subtract Lemma 3 from Lemma 4; the logZ⋆ cancels. Two sanity checks. If the sampler is perfect (A=0) and the buffer thermal (C=0), then B=0 and Δ=0. If the policy is perfect but the buffer arbitrary, then A=0 and DKL(μPB‖πPB)=C, so Δ=0 again — a bad buffer alone opens no gap. Conversely, with a thermal buffer but an imperfect policy,

Δ=DKL(πPB‖PF)+DKL(PF‖πPB),

the symmetrised KL (Jeffreys divergence) between policy and target — a genuine distributional distance, vanishing only at PF=πPB.

05The one-sided constraint on the normaliser

The learned normaliser enters through the TB residual, whose square is the loss:

r(τ):=logZ+logPF(τ)−logPB(τ∣x)−logR(x)=logZ−logw(τ).(3)

Averaging (3) under the backward stream gives 𝔼μPB[r]=logZ−JB, so the backward residual is centred exactly when logZ=JB. How low can that be?

Proposition · ELBO floor
Let ELBO(μ):=𝔼μ[logR]+ℍ(μ)=logZ⋆−C. Then JB≥ELBO(μ), with equality if and only if PF=μPB.
Proof

Immediate from Lemma 4: JB−ELBO(μ)=B≥0, vanishing iff PF=μPB. The identity logZ⋆−C=𝔼μ[logR]+ℍ(μ) is a one-line expansion of DKL(μ‖π).

Corollary · backward training can be asked for the impossible
Suppose logZ is held fixed (detached) at z0. The mean backward residual can be driven to zero by training PF only if z0≥ELBO(μ). Below that, no forward policy whatsoever centres it.
Proof

As PF varies, JB ranges over [ELBO(μ),∞): the lower end is the Proposition, and JB grows without bound as PF assigns vanishing mass to the buffer’s trajectories. Setting z0=JB is achievable exactly when z0≥ELBO(μ).

The asymmetry is worth stating plainly. Placing logZ too high is harmless — the policy simply raises JB to meet it. Placing it below ELBO(μ) demands more forward probability mass on the buffer than normalisation permits. Under a squared loss the optimiser does not stop when asked for the impossible; it distorts PF as far as it can in the demanded direction, degrading the sampler rather than merely stalling it.

06Consequences

The normaliser is a nuisance parameter, not an objective

logZ is one number, but the two streams place it in two spots separated by Δ:

𝔼PF[r]=logZ−JF,𝔼μPB[r]=(logZ−JF)−Δ.

Choosing logZ=JF zeroes the forward residual and leaves the backward one at −Δ; choosing logZ=JB does the reverse. No choice zeroes both unless Δ=0 — and Δ depends only on (PF,PB,μ,π), not on Z.

The placement of the normaliser decides which stream absorbs the error.
It does not decide whether error exists.

A practical corollary: a controller rule watching the residual of the branch that trains Z is measuring a tautology. If the forward branch updates Z and only Z, then logZ→JF and the forward residual self-zeroes no matter how bad the policy is. The informative residuals are on the branches that train the policy.

The gap is not a valid optimisation target

Δ=A+B−C is decreasing in C. Since C measures how bad the buffer is, degrading the buffer reduces Δ. A loop that churns its own buffer and gates on Δ therefore has a direct incentive to make the buffer worse. Whatever Δ is for, it is not a success criterion — it is a handoff diagnostic, an indicator of whether the placement of Z is currently contentious.

Only one term is the objective

Of the three, only A is an unambiguous statement about what we want — and it controls the error in the terminal marginal, which is what is actually sampled.

Proposition · data processing
DKL(PF⊤‖π)≤A.
Proof

Factor each trajectory distribution into its terminal marginal and the conditional over trajectories given the terminal state; the KL chain rule gives

A=DKL(PF⊤‖π)+𝔼x~PF⊤[DKL(PF(·∣x)‖PB(·∣x))],

and the second term is non-negative.

So driving A down drives the sampling error down, the slack being exactly the mismatch between the forward posterior over trajectories and the backward policy. Neither B nor Δ admits such a bound.

The two policy terms require different data

A has the model in the first argument — reverse KL, estimated from samples drawn from PF: forward rollouts, or a replay buffer of previously generated forward trajectories. B has the model in the second — forward KL, estimated from samples drawn from μ: backward rollouts out of the buffer. Hence:

  • Training on buffer data alone reduces B and leaves A unattended: the sampler learns to cover what it is shown and never sharpens.
  • Training on forward data alone reduces A and lets B grow: the classic mode collapse.
  • Since Δ contains both, a system in which one data stream is inactive cannot reduce Δ past the term nothing is attacking — no matter how long it runs, or how the remaining branch is weighted.

The last point is worth emphasising because it presents as slow convergence rather than as a stalled component.

07What is estimable

QuantityEstimatorNotes
JFmean of logw, forward rollouts unbiased for logZ⋆−A
JBmean of logw, buffer draws may exceed logZ⋆
ΔJB−JF=A+B−C
logZ⋆log-mean-exp of w, forward stream Lemma 1; biased low
Alog-mean-exp minus mean, forward the “Jensen gap”
C—not identifiable without π

Two cautions. The log-mean-exp estimator of logZ⋆ is biased low by Jensen and its variance grows with Var(logw) — precisely the regime where one wants it most — so the resulting estimate of A is an underestimate. And B and C are not separately identifiable from the two levels: only the combination C−B=logZ⋆−JB is. Separating them needs an independent handle on the buffer, such as knowing μ came from a thermalised procedure.

08Summary

JF=logZ⋆−A policy error, mode-seeking — bounds the sampling error
JB=logZ⋆−C+B policy coverage against buffer quality — not a bound
Δ=A+B−C improves when the buffer degrades — diagnostic only

Read as a design principle:

  1. Objective — A down. It bounds the terminal-marginal error; nothing else here does.
  2. Constraint — B bounded. Lost coverage is not recoverable by further optimisation of A.
  3. Precondition — C small. No gradient reduces it. It is a data-preparation problem.
  4. Nuisance — the normaliser. Keep it accurate enough that neither residual is systematically corrupted, and never below ELBO(μ).
  5. Diagnostic, never a target — the gap. It improves when the buffer gets worse.

Everything above is exact and holds per condition c in the conditional case: replace R,π,Z⋆,Z by their c-indexed versions and condition every policy. No step couples distinct conditions, so the quantities are computed per condition and then reduced across them — by a mean, or by a worst-case quantile.
