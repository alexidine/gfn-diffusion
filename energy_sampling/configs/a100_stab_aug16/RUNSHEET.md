## 1. The short-run / utilization-window tension — the decision

Short runs and a valid `gpu/util_policy` reading are incompatible: the metric is a
7200 s trailing window, the scheduler judges over ~2 h (inferred from kills:
prod0810 `4r351oqm` cancelled at 5.2 h; qm9anchor_aug14 v1, all six arms at pinned
batch 1000 ELJ T=10, all cancelled), and below 900 s `util_recent == util_policy`
**by construction** — one deque, two windows — so their agreement on a short run
carries zero information.

**Decision: both, explicitly tiered, never mixed.**

| Tier | Length | What its occupancy numbers can support |
|---|---|---|
| **F** functionality | 1–4 h, mostly under the ~2 h policy horizon | `gpu/util_policy`: **not quotable**. The sidecar smi CSV + `sacct Elapsed` are a valid statement about *that job's own usage over its own duration* — and nothing more. |
| **B** floors | 8–60 min | Same caveat — and legitimately so: the registry itself declares `gpu/util_policy` unusable on all four step-cost benchmarks. |
| **U** utilization | ≥ 1.15 × window by construction | The only tier that reports `gpu/util_policy`. Window fill is **verified from the sidecar CSV duration** per run, because the in-process sample count (G2) is not instrumented. |

**Quantified caveat for the short tiers:** a 20-minute job's smi mean is a
measurement of 20 minutes; the policy statistic averages 7200 s and the scheduler
appears not to evaluate a job younger than its window at all (every observed kill
came after ≥2 h). Short arms are therefore *safe from* the policy and *silent
about* it — both facts are used, neither is conflated with a proxy reading.

**Proxy bookkeeping (Phase 4 acceptance item):** `gpu/util_policy` is carried
under **case 2 (partial), leaning case 3** — cluster-visible evidence exists
(cancellations + sacct) but the statistic/threshold/window are *inferred from
kills*, not documented: working model ≈ mean `utilization.gpu` < ~60 % over ~2 h.
The error direction is known: the in-process sampler takes **zero samples during
eval**, so `util_policy` **overstates** what the scheduler sees — the dangerous
direction. U3 measures the overstatement; the sacct epilogue on every job is what
accumulates the case-2 evidence. `nvidia-smi` agreement corroborates only
sampling artifacts (same NVML counter as `torch.cuda.utilization()`); it carries
no case-2 weight. §0 of `phase6_measurement_request.md` (one email to an admin)
is still worth more than any arm here — ask before wave 2 if possible.

## 3. The three concurrent utilization readings (requirement, mechanism)

Every job — all tiers — carries in `submit_*.sbatch`:

1. **In-process:** `gpu/util_policy` + `gpu/util_recent` (already logged; one
   deque, two windows — their difference is a trend estimator, never a
   cross-check).
2. **Sidecar sampler:** `nvidia-smi` CSV at 10 s cadence spanning the *whole*
   job (startup, eval, wrapup — the minutes the in-process sensor cannot see),
   plus `--query-compute-apps` (co-tenant exclusion), throttle reasons + power
   (idle vs throttled), a one-shot MIG/UUID/scontrol record, and `dcgmi`
   SM_ACTIVE where available (the only genuinely different instrument).
3. **Epilogue:** `sacct` written by a `trap` that survives `scancel` — on a
   cancelled job this is the whole record (wandb uploads no console log for a
   run left `crashed`).

Read both a trailing **mean** and a windowed **minimum** from the CSVs — §0's
question 2 (mean vs floor-over-blocks) is open, and computing only the mean means
re-running if the answer is "minimum". The wandb system stream
(`system.gpu.0.gpu`, ~14 s cadence, samples during eval) rides along free; the
Δ11 retroactive analysis over *existing* runs should happen before wave 2 and may
shrink what wave 2 needs. Known instrument gaps, stated: G1–G3 (source flag,
sample counts, sampled-wallclock fraction) are **not landed**, so
which-sensor-answered and window-fill are established from the sidecar, not from
wandb; landing G1–G3 before the phase-6 submission remains the recommendation.

**Check of the "30–50 GPU-h for the a100-throughput floors" arithmetic:** it
assumed 5 benchmarks × 2 h × 3–5 repeats, i.e. that `min_wallclock_s: 7200`
binds all five. It binds **one** (`a100-batch-scaling-elj`) — and there the

distribution.

**Priority order if trimming:** wave 1 → B floors → U3 (proxy error direction)
→ U2 (surviving-rung floor) → U1 (instrumented cancellation) → U1c probe.

**Calendar time at up to 16 concurrent A100s:** wave 1 is one batch (5 jobs,
≤6 h); wave 2 is 30 jobs ≈ two queue batches (floors finish inside the first).
The whole battery is ~1–2 calendar days plus the read between waves — GPU-hours,
not wall-clock, are the budget that binds.

# WAVE 3 — the (T, B, route) map (2026-08-18)

**Supersedes the U tier**, which asked "does batch move occupancy" at T=10 with
production work stripped and so answered a question nobody has: 38 → 48 % across
a 7.4× batch rise, while `prod0810` at **T=60 and a smaller batch** sits at 70 %
and has run 152 h uncancelled. Occupancy is GPU work per *training step*; the
host cost is paid once per step regardless. **T multiplies the rollout only;
batch multiplies both halves**, so batch merely dilutes the fixed term.

## Tier 1 — rollout shape, ENERGY-FREE (9 arms with the flag arms, 1 h class)

`w3_roll_T{10,60,100}_b{1000,7410}` — six cells, **one route only**: the rollout
trunk is the same model whatever the energy function is.

They run a **single terminal `train_prior` stage, bwd from the dataset, weights-
only**, because of a trap: `eval_T` must equal `integrator.T` (refused at load) —
a policy learns drift per step at ONE dt, so a warm start run at another T
integrates a different SDE and its samples are wrong in a structured way, which
on an MLIP route feeds straight into cost via neighbour counts. `bwd/dataset`
calls the energy **zero times** and draws terminals from the dataset, so there is
nothing for T to contaminate.

## Tier 2 — the production boundary (14 arms, 3 h class)

Per-route batch ladders at T=60, production shape. Ranges differ ~100×, so they
are per route: ELJ {500, 1000, 2722, 7410, 20000}, UMA {100, 250, 500, 1000},
MACE {50, 100, 250}, plus the width pair `w3_elj_w{256,1024}` at batch 2722 —
the third occupancy lever, and unmeasured until now.

**ELJ 500 is the only sub-crossover rung** (`fused_grad_accum_min_samples` is
1000) — the one region where `samples_per_sec` and `updates_per_sec` have
different argmaxes.

**Survive-or-cancelled at 02:00:2x IS the measurement.** The 3 h SLURM class is
deliberately above the 2 h policy horizon so the two are distinguishable by
`Elapsed`: a kill at 02:00:2x is the occupancy policy, at 03:00:00 it is SLURM.

## The MLIP construction-path A/Bs (in the 1 h set)

Env switches, not config keys, so each arm carries a `<arm>.env` sidecar that the
sbatch sources and echoes; the drain functions log the *resolved* flags, so the
two halves can be cross-checked.

- `w3_uma_graphtimer` — `MXT_UMA_GRAPH_TIMER=1`. UMA's forward is **98.3–98.6 %**
  of the call and opaque; this is the only thing that splits fairchem's
  `otf_graph` out of it.
- `w3_mace_batchednl` — `MXT_BATCHED_MACE_NEIGHBOURS=1`. The A/B on **93 % of the
  MACE build**. The speedup has a batch-size crossover and an earlier 10.8×
  reading did not reproduce, so this is Phase 5.1's re-measurement.
- `w3_mace_gpubatch` — `MXT_GPU_MACE_BATCH=1`.

**The graph timer changes what it measures** — a CUDA synchronise per graph build
— so that arm's *wall clock* is not comparable to the un-timed cells. Phase
FRACTIONS are the readable quantity; its step times must never enter a floor.

## New instruments (landed in `train.py`, verified on a local run)

| metric | why |
|---|---|
| `batch/med_step_s`, `batch/sps_rung` | the 20-step median the batch controller **actually acts on** — computed since forever and never logged, so a ladder built from 10-step report means describes a curve no in-process controller can reproduce |
| `updates_per_sec`, `batch/accum_target` | opt-step throughput. Differs from samples/sec below the crossover, where **every MLIP arm here runs** |
| `vram/peak_train_mb` | peak reset at each eval, so the sizer's memory constraint is a train-phase quantity. Eval shares `batch_size` and is often the larger peak |

Verified locally: `updates_per_sec` 0.71094 = 710.94 / 1000, `sps_rung` 691.6 =
100 / 0.14459. `peak_reserved_mb` keeps its old lifetime-max meaning.

## Cost

9 × 1 h + 14 × 3 h ≈ **51 GPU-h worst case**, and materially less in practice:
the tier-1 and flag arms are 400-step runs that will exit in minutes, and every
below-threshold boundary cell dies at 2 h. Two queue batches on 16 A100s.
